# Conditional voice

Read author-provided samples and design constraints with their permission and
source status. Preserve `bible/voice-profile.md` as the author-facing explanation.
In a managed project, read `novel_literary_schema` for `voice`, `anchor` and
`drift_baseline`; use accepted versioned records instead of adding a giant profile
to every call. Legacy writing context may omit a profile too large for its budget;
inspect `context_summary` and read missing guidance explicitly.

For each tendency specify what it does, where it applies, permitted variation,
exceptions, evidence and overuse risk. Model narrator access, syntax and paragraph
relations, rhythm, diction, figurative behavior, sensory selection, interiority,
dialogue tactics and ambiguity. Voice is not a bag of unusual words.

Keep invariants, conditional profiles, transitions, variation budgets, motif
behavior, negative constraints, anchors and uncertainty distinct. Record voice
epochs for legitimate development. Do not let observed drift silently redefine
a frozen design. A permitted deviation is not a compulsory performance.

Calibration may begin with six to twelve short passages spanning contrasting
functions and focalizers. This is a design heuristic, not adequate statistical
power. Remove names and premise cues for some comparisons. Ask actual readers
about shared voice, viewpoint distinction and mannerism. Never label a model's
self-comparison human calibration. With no readers, keep calibration unperformed.

Use short project-owned or permitted anchors for their functional relations,
not reusable wording. Retain failed overornamented, overcompressed, generic or
imitative variants as anti-anchors, retrieved only for relevant elevated risks.
Never ship the example voice as every project's default. Statistical reference
bands need actual sources, condition and method versions; missing data is unknown.

Store design proposals in ordinary project JSON, inspect their current accepted
HEAD, and use `novel_literary_design` under delegated authority or the author's
`/PNW-literary design <kind> <path> <head>` command. Author additions are never
silently replaced. See `literary-workflow` for the execution and acceptance path.
