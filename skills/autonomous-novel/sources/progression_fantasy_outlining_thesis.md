# Abstract

Progression fantasy is organized around a distinctive narrative promise: advancement is not merely an incidental consequence of adventure but a central object of attention, anticipation, and causal explanation. Yet practical discussions of the genre often reduce outlining to a sequence of ranks, training episodes, confrontations, and larger antagonists. That reduction confuses progression with escalation, measurement with meaning, and event order with narrative structure. It also leaves unanswered the problems that most often destabilize long-form progression narratives: arbitrary advancement, inert power systems, repetitive challenge loops, static opposition, vanishing costs, social worlds that fail to react, and series whose scale expands faster than their capacity for closure.

This article develops an integrative conceptual framework for outlining progression fantasy. Because targeted searches located little peer-reviewed scholarship explicitly organized around the contemporary label *progression fantasy*, the analysis does not pretend that a mature genre-specific science already exists. It instead synthesizes relevant work in genre theory, fantasy and worldbuilding studies, narrative causality and comprehension, serial narrative, game studies, motivation, expertise and learning, and writing cognition. The resulting framework distinguishes directly supported findings from cross-domain applications and original design propositions.

The central thesis is that progression is best modeled as a persistent, reader-legible transformation of a protagonist's feasible action space. An adequate outline must connect that transformation to goals, prerequisites, attempts, feedback, choices, costs, consequences, and adaptive opposition. It must also coordinate multiple dimensions of change, including capability, knowledge, resources, institutional position, relationships, and liabilities, while producing closure at the scales of scene, arc, book, and series. On this basis, the article offers a formal state model, progression topologies, adequacy criteria, a 28-step outlining protocol, scene and arc templates, diagnostic stress tests, alternative outlining modes, and a worked example. The framework is deliberately pluralistic: it identifies functional requirements without imposing a single act structure, magic-system style, publication model, or degree of quantification.

**Keywords:** progression fantasy; novel outlining; narrative causality; serial narrative; fantasy worldbuilding; power systems; game studies; character development; story structure

# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 The problem

The phrase *properly outline* can conceal an unsupported assumption: that one universal sequence of beats is correct for every progression fantasy novel. No such conclusion follows from the existing evidence. Genre is better understood as a historically variable set of expectations, conventions, and interpretive cues than as a rigid recipe [1]. Fantasy itself accommodates substantially different rhetorical relations among protagonist, reader, and impossible world [2], and its recognizable patterns operate as strategies rather than mandatory forms [3]. An account of progression-fantasy outlining should therefore identify what the outline must accomplish, not dictate one arrangement by which every author must accomplish it.

A second problem is conceptual. Almost every extended narrative contains change. Many protagonists learn, gain allies, acquire tools, improve their social standing, or become more capable. Those facts alone do not make advancement the organizing promise of the work. In progression fantasy, the process, intelligibility, and consequences of advancement receive unusual narrative weight. The reader is invited not only to observe that the protagonist is different later, but to understand how the difference arose, what it permits, what it costs, how it compares with prior states, and what new conflicts it creates.

This emphasis creates a demanding structural task. An outline must coordinate at least five systems at once:

1. a causal story in which events follow from goals, actions, obstacles, and consequences;
2. a progression process in which capabilities or other forms of agency change intelligibly;
3. a world that supplies resources, institutions, constraints, and countermeasures;
4. a character process in which advancement matters to identity, values, and relationships;
5. a serial architecture that yields satisfaction now while preserving meaningful possibility later.

When these systems are planned independently, they frequently conflict. A carefully tiered power ladder may have little effect on the plot. A dramatic climax may require an ability the progression sequence did not prepare. A new power may remove the constraints that sustained earlier conflicts. A supposedly scarce technique may spread whenever the plot needs another opponent. A series may preserve future installments only by withholding closure from the current book. The outline is therefore not merely a list of anticipated scenes. It is a model for reconciling constraints across time.

## 1.2 Research question

The principal question is:

> What properties must an outline possess if it is to support a coherent, legible, consequential, and extensible progression fantasy novel or series?

Four subsidiary questions follow:

1. What counts as progression, as distinct from change, escalation, or numerical increase?
2. How should progression be connected to plot causality, character agency, world structure, and opposition?
3. How should an outline distribute advancement, setbacks, information, and closure across multiple narrative scales?
4. Which elements are invariant requirements of the genre promise, and which are optional design choices?

## 1.3 Central thesis

This article proposes the following adequacy standard:

> A progression fantasy outline is adequate to the extent that it represents advancement as a causally generated, reader-legible, and functionally consequential transformation of the protagonist's feasible action space, and integrates that transformation with opposition, choice, cost, social response, character development, and nested closure across scene, arc, book, and series scales.

This is a functional thesis, not a claim that only one style can succeed. A quantified LitRPG, a cultivation epic, an academy novel, a craft-centered fantasy, a political advancement story, and a noncombat mastery narrative may all satisfy the standard by different means. Conversely, visible levels and frequent victories do not by themselves satisfy it.

## 1.4 Contributions

The article makes six contributions.

First, it gives an operational definition of progression fantasy that does not depend on disputed boundary cases. Second, it formalizes progression as change in an action space rather than change in a number. Third, it distinguishes dimensions and topologies of advancement so that non-linear and noncombat forms can be outlined precisely. Fourth, it provides a multiscale architecture that joins progression loops to causal plot and serial closure. Fifth, it offers an ordered protocol and reusable planning artifacts. Sixth, it marks the evidential status of its claims so that practical recommendations are not mistaken for established empirical laws.

# 2. Scope, Method, and Evidence

## 2.1 Type of inquiry

This is an integrative conceptual study. It does not report an experiment, a reader survey, a sales analysis, or a representative content analysis of published progression fantasy. Its aim is theory construction: to assemble established concepts from adjacent fields, determine where they apply, expose where analogy rather than direct evidence is doing the work, and derive a framework that can be criticized and tested.

The framework draws on seven bodies of work:

- genre theory and fantasy studies [1-3];
- imaginary-world construction and transmedial world coherence [4];
- scholarship on Chinese internet literature and xianxia-related development [5-6];
- narrative causality, comprehension, suspense, curiosity, and engagement [10-17];
- serial narrative theory [7-9];
- game mechanics, rules, and design frameworks [18-21];
- motivation, goal pursuit, expertise, learning, and writing cognition [22-30].

This synthesis is appropriate because progression fantasy commonly foregrounds processes that resemble rule-governed interaction, skill acquisition, resource conversion, iterative challenge, and serial expansion. It would nevertheless be an error to infer that a novel is a game, that a fictional learner must obey every empirical regularity observed in real learners, or that findings about player motivation automatically establish reader motivation. Those are analogies with varying strength, not identities.

## 2.2 Evidence hierarchy

The article uses four evidence statuses.

**Status A: direct support.** The cited research directly supports a general claim about narrative causality, comprehension, serial form, motivation, learning, game systems, or writing process.

**Status B: cross-domain application.** A supported concept is applied to progression fantasy, but the application itself lacks direct genre-specific testing.

**Status C: original synthesis.** The claim, model, criterion, or template is proposed here as a useful theoretical construction. Its value depends on explanatory power, internal coherence, and eventual testing.

**Status D: practice-contingent guidance.** The recommendation depends strongly on medium, audience, publication cadence, subgenre, or authorial aim. It should be adopted only when the relevant conditions hold.

These statuses prevent a common methodological error in craft discourse: presenting an attractive design intuition as though it were a scientifically demonstrated rule.

## 2.3 Limits of the available literature

Targeted searches through September 6, 2026 located little peer-reviewed research explicitly organized around the modern English-language label *progression fantasy*. The label is prominent in publishing, author, platform, and reader discourse, but its scholarly apparatus is still limited. Adjacent work on internet literature, xianxia, seriality, fantasy rhetoric, game-like fiction, and narrative cognition is therefore more developed than direct scholarship on the named category.

This limitation has three consequences. First, the operational definition below is stipulative and analytic rather than a claim to have discovered the genre's one true essence. Second, recommendations about reader expectation are advanced with lower confidence than findings about causal comprehension or recursive writing processes. Third, the framework should be treated as a research program: it generates testable hypotheses and coding categories for later corpus studies, reader studies, and comparative analysis.

## 2.4 What the framework does not assume

The framework does not assume that:

- progression must be numerical;
- the protagonist must begin powerless;
- advancement must be monotonic;
- combat power is the only or primary dimension of progress;
- every gain must be earned through labor alone;
- a hard magic system is mandatory;
- the protagonist must become the strongest being in the setting;
- the story must use an academy, tournament, dungeon, cultivation sect, system interface, or reincarnation premise;
- the work must be a web serial;
- every installment must contain a breakthrough;
- one act structure, beat sheet, or scene formula is universally correct;
- outlining should eliminate discovery during drafting.

Each of these may be a valid design choice. None is built into the adequacy standard.

# 3. Definitions and Key Distinctions

## 3.1 Operational definition of progression fantasy

For this article, a narrative qualifies as progression fantasy when all four conditions are substantially present:

1. **Centrality:** increasing or reorganizing one or more forms of protagonist agency is a principal source of narrative movement and reader anticipation.
2. **Process visibility:** the work devotes meaningful attention to how advancement occurs, including some combination of prerequisites, attempts, learning, acquisition, trade-offs, feedback, or testing.
3. **Comparability:** the audience can make useful comparisons among earlier, current, and prospective states, even when no explicit levels exist.
4. **Consequence:** gains and losses alter later possibilities, conflicts, choices, or social relations rather than functioning as decorative labels.

This definition treats progression as a dominant organizing tendency, not a binary essence. Borderline works can be described by degree. A novel may contain a progression subplot without being principally progression fantasy. A work may also combine progression fantasy with epic fantasy, mystery, romance, horror, science fiction, political fantasy, or another mode.

## 3.2 Progression is not mere change

Change is any difference between states. Progression is a directionally interpreted transformation relative to a goal, value, capacity, or possibility. A character can change clothes without progressing. A character can also progress in disguise craft because the change increases control over future situations. The relevant difference is functional and purposive, not merely temporal.

## 3.3 Progression is not escalation

Escalation raises the magnitude, scope, urgency, or cost of conflict. Progression changes what an agent can do within conflict. The two frequently interact but can diverge.

- An enemy army becomes ten times larger: escalation without protagonist progression.
- The protagonist learns to negotiate a truce: progression that may reduce escalation.
- The protagonist gains city-destroying force and confronts a planetary enemy: both.
- The protagonist loses raw power but acquires strategic knowledge that permits a narrower victory: regression on one axis and progression on another.

Outlines that equate progression with escalation tend toward an inflation problem. Each new antagonist must be larger because no other source of difficulty has been planned. A multidimensional model permits difficulty through counters, uncertainty, conflicting goals, institutions, coordination, time limits, exposure, or moral cost.

## 3.4 Progression is not measurement

Measurement is a representational device. It can improve legibility, anticipation, and comparison, but it cannot create consequence by itself. A rank increase matters only if the new rank changes access, probability, responsibility, risk, relationship, or choice. Conversely, a narrative can provide exact functional comparability without any number. A healer who can now diagnose hidden curses but still cannot remove them has progressed in a specific, testable way.

The distinction also prevents a false inference from precision to coherence. A system may assign exact quantities while using inconsistent conversion rules. A less quantified system may remain coherent because its affordances, constraints, and exceptions are stable.

## 3.5 Progression is not identical to character development

A power arc and a character arc answer different questions.

- The power arc asks how the protagonist's capacity or position changes.
- The character arc asks how the protagonist's identity, values, attachments, interpretations, or patterns of choice change.

They can align, conflict, or remain partly independent. A protagonist may grow stronger while becoming crueler, lose status while becoming more self-governing, or achieve mastery without resolving grief. Treating all improvement as moral improvement obscures some of the genre's strongest possibilities. The outline should specify the relation between arcs rather than assume harmony.

## 3.6 Progression is not synonymous with effort

Readers may judge an advancement as deserved, prepared, coherent, or satisfying for several reasons. Labor is one possible reason, but not the only one. Inheritance, luck, patronage, accident, theft, sacrifice, discovery, or collective support may produce a gain. The structural question is what the protagonist does with the gain and how the narrative integrates it.

A useful distinction is among four kinds of earnedness:

1. **Causal earnedness:** the gain follows from established conditions rather than an unexplained rescue.
2. **Agential earnedness:** the protagonist makes consequential decisions or takes risks relevant to obtaining, retaining, understanding, or using it.
3. **Epistemic earnedness:** the audience receives enough prior information to see why the gain is possible, even if the exact outcome remains surprising.
4. **Dramatic earnedness:** the gain resolves or intensifies a conflict in proportion to its preparation, cost, and later consequences.

An unchosen gift can have low agential earnedness at acquisition but high agential and dramatic earnedness during mastery. An intensely trained ability can still feel unearned if the system changes arbitrarily at the climax.

## 3.7 An outline is not a list of events

A chronology says what happens in order. An outline should also represent why each event occurs, what state it changes, what information it reveals, which future event it enables, and which promise it pays or creates. Narrative comprehension depends substantially on causal and goal-related relations among events [10-12]. A useful outline therefore contains multiple link types:

- causal: event A produces event B;
- enabling: A makes B possible without forcing it;
- motivational: A creates or revises a goal that leads to B;
- informational: A changes what an agent or reader can infer;
- constraining: A removes options or adds costs;
- promissory: A establishes an expectation later answered by B;
- thematic: A creates a comparison or conflict of values developed by B.

The outline becomes analytically useful when these relations are inspectable.

# 4. A Formal Model of Progression

## 4.1 The protagonist state vector

Let the protagonist's narratively relevant state at time *t* be represented as:

$$
S_t = \langle C_t, K_t, R_t, P_t, B_t, L_t \rangle
$$

where:

- $C_t$ is capability: skills, powers, bodily capacities, techniques, and reliable procedures;
- $K_t$ is knowledge: facts, models, maps, diagnoses, predictions, and metacognitive understanding;
- $R_t$ is resources and access: tools, money, energy, materials, locations, permissions, and time;
- $P_t$ is social and institutional position: rank, legitimacy, office, reputation, rights, obligations, and visibility;
- $B_t$ is bonds and collective capacity: allies, trust, coordination, mentorship, dependents, and reciprocal commitments;
- $L_t$ is liabilities and constraints: injuries, debts, oaths, corruption, exposure, cooldowns, trauma, enemies, and opportunity costs.

The categories are analytic conveniences, not natural kinds. A single element may belong to more than one. A magical oath can be a liability, a source of legitimacy, and a bond. The value of the vector is that it forces the outline to track more than a scalar power score.

## 4.2 Environment and feasible action space

Let $E_t$ represent the relevant environment, including physical conditions, institutions, available information, other agents, current opposition, and world rules. The protagonist's feasible action set is then:

$$
A_t = \Phi(S_t, E_t)
$$

$A_t$ is not the set of everything imaginable. It is the set of actions the protagonist can realistically attempt at that point, with associated costs, risks, reliabilities, and scales. Progression occurs when a transition from $S_t$ to $S_{t+1}$ changes that feasible action space in a narratively important way.

A progression event may:

- add a previously impossible action;
- make an unreliable action dependable;
- reduce the cost, time, or risk of an action;
- increase range, scale, duration, precision, or transferability;
- combine existing abilities into a new strategy;
- improve selection among actions through better knowledge;
- secure permission or legitimacy to act;
- enable coordinated action with others;
- remove a binding constraint;
- transform a liability into a conditional resource.

This model explains why a small technical refinement can matter more than a large numerical increase. If the refinement opens the one action needed to alter the conflict, its action-space effect is larger.

## 4.3 Conditions for genuine narrative progression

A state transition should satisfy four conditions before the outline treats it as a substantive progression beat.

**Persistence.** The change lasts long enough to affect later decisions, or its temporary nature is itself a planned constraint. A one-scene boost can count, but only if its acquisition, use, depletion, or aftermath has continuing causal significance.

**Legibility.** The audience can update its model of what the protagonist can and cannot do. Complete explanation is unnecessary, but arbitrary reinterpretation is not.

**Causal integration.** The transition arises from established rules, circumstances, actions, relationships, or costs. Coincidence may initiate it, but later structure must absorb it.

**Functional consequence.** The changed state alters later action, opposition, stakes, relationships, or interpretation. A title change that affects nothing is nominal progression.

These conditions yield a practical counterfactual test: remove the purported gain from the outline. If almost every later event can occur unchanged, the gain is probably decorative or redundant.

## 4.4 Progression can include loss

The action-space model does not require monotonic growth. A protagonist can lose one capability while gaining knowledge, legitimacy, allies, or self-command. A permanent injury may shrink the set of direct actions but force development of delegation, planning, or political influence. A fall from institutional rank may remove resources while creating moral independence.

The crucial requirement is not that every coordinate rises. It is that the narrative makes the transformation intelligible and consequential. This permits plateau, sacrifice, respecialization, recovery, and tragic progression without abandoning the genre's central concern.

## 4.5 Progression as reorganization

More is only one form of progress. A protagonist may reorganize an existing repertoire so that previously isolated capacities become synergistic. In game-design terms, mechanics are methods by which agents interact with a world [18], while rule structures and fictional worlds jointly shape meaningful play [19-20]. Applied cautiously to fiction, this suggests that a compelling gain often changes interaction patterns rather than merely increasing output.

For example, an ability to create heat and an ability to perceive air currents may initially be separate. Learning to combine them into controlled convection creates a new strategy. The outline should therefore track not only acquisitions but interactions among acquisitions.

## 4.6 A non-numeric quality function

For diagnostic purposes, the narrative quality of a progression beat can be represented as a function:

$$
Q(p) = f(L, C, A, F, X, N, I)
$$

where:

- $L$ = legibility;
- $C$ = causal preparation;
- $A$ = protagonist agency;
- $F$ = functional consequence;
- $X$ = cost or trade-off;
- $N$ = novelty relative to prior beats;
- $I$ = integration with character, world, and plot.

This is not a validated quantitative scale. It is a checklist expressed formally to discourage single-factor judgment. A beat can succeed with low cost if it has strong consequences, or with low acquisition agency if it creates demanding later choices. The dimensions are compensatory only up to a point. Extremely low causal preparation or functional consequence usually indicates a structural defect.

# 5. Dimensions and Topologies of Progression

## 5.1 Dimensions

A robust outline can distinguish at least eight progression dimensions.

### 5.1.1 Technical or capability progression

The protagonist gains or improves a skill, spell, bodily capacity, craft, method, or combat technique. Important subdimensions include reliability, efficiency, precision, range, scale, flexibility, speed, and resistance to disruption.

### 5.1.2 Epistemic progression

The protagonist improves what can be known, inferred, predicted, diagnosed, or explained. This includes learning rules, discovering hidden causes, building maps, recognizing deception, or understanding one's own limitations. Epistemic progression can dominate a mystery-centered or research-centered progression fantasy.

### 5.1.3 Resource and access progression

The protagonist gains materials, tools, income, energy, territory, archives, transport, legal permissions, or opportunities. Because resources are convertible into other forms of agency, the outline must specify their supply, depletion, transfer, and control.

### 5.1.4 Social and institutional progression

The protagonist gains rank, reputation, authority, citizenship, membership, legal standing, or institutional leverage. This dimension changes what others permit, expect, fear, or contest. It can progress independently of technical power.

### 5.1.5 Relational and collective progression

The protagonist gains trust, allies, a team, a school, a community, or coordination capacity. Collective progression is not reducible to adding powerful companions. It includes communication, role differentiation, mutual reliability, and the capacity to act together.

### 5.1.6 Strategic and combinatorial progression

The protagonist learns to select, sequence, or combine existing capacities more effectively. This is often the best antidote to raw-stat inflation because it expands the action space without requiring a new tier.

### 5.1.7 Identity and ethical progression

The protagonist becomes able to make, sustain, or revise commitments. This dimension is not necessarily moral improvement. It may involve corruption, fanaticism, self-knowledge, principled restraint, or the acceptance of responsibility. Its progression criterion must be stated relative to the work's thematic question, not smuggled in as an objective moral ranking.

### 5.1.8 Environmental or plot-position progression

The protagonist changes position relative to the central problem: nearer to the destination, in possession of a key, free of pursuit, inside an institution, or able to name the true adversary. This is ordinary plot advancement, but it must be linked to the other dimensions if the work is to remain progression-centered.

## 5.2 Why multidimensional tracking matters

A single ladder creates clarity but can conceal structural losses. The protagonist may rise three combat tiers while losing credibility, allies, mobility, and health. If the outline tracks only rank, it cannot represent the real state of agency. Multidimensional tracking also creates trade-offs. A forbidden technique might increase capability while reducing legitimacy and increasing exposure. Such trade-offs generate conflict without pretending that every gain is pure benefit.

## 5.3 Progression topologies

A topology describes the structural form of possible advancement. The following forms can be combined.

### 5.3.1 Ladder

Advancement occurs through ordered ranks or thresholds. The ladder is easy to compare and anticipate. Its risks are predictability, inflation, and the assumption that higher rank dominates all lower-rank strategies.

### 5.3.2 Branching tree

Advancement involves specialization and mutually exclusive choices. The tree foregrounds autonomy and opportunity cost. It requires the outline to preserve the consequences of unchosen branches.

### 5.3.3 Network or synergy graph

Nodes are abilities, resources, or relationships, and value arises from connections among them. The network favors inventive recombination. It risks opacity unless the audience can understand the relevant interactions.

### 5.3.4 Mastery spiral

The protagonist revisits the same fundamental operation under more difficult conditions, with deeper understanding and finer control. Apparent repetition becomes development through changed context and interpretation.

### 5.3.5 Threshold transformation

Advancement is discontinuous. Crossing a threshold changes the kind of agent the protagonist is, not merely the degree of an existing trait. Each transformation should have prerequisites, irreversible consequences, and changed relations to the world.

### 5.3.6 Ecological or institutional expansion

Progress occurs through a workshop, settlement, sect, company, polity, or knowledge network. The protagonist's agency grows because a collective system grows. This topology requires supply chains, governance, labor, legitimacy, and coordination to be outlined rather than treated as scenery.

### 5.3.7 Cyclical loss and recovery

The protagonist repeatedly loses access and rebuilds under altered conditions. The cycle is meaningful only when recovery is not a reset. Each iteration should preserve knowledge, relationships, scars, altered values, or systemic consequences.

### 5.3.8 Collective or civilizational progression

The unit of progress is a group or world rather than one individual. The outline must then allocate agency across people and institutions, and distinguish the protagonist's contribution from collective causation.

### 5.3.9 Anti-progression or critical progression

The narrative uses advancement to expose domination, addiction, alienation, ecological cost, or institutional capture. It remains progression-centered because the mechanisms and consequences of advancement remain central, even when the work questions whether ascending the ladder is good.

## 5.4 Selecting a topology

Topology should follow the dramatic question. A story about disciplined mastery may favor a spiral. A story about identity and irreversible change may favor thresholds. A story about strategic freedom may favor a branching tree. A story about rebuilding society may favor institutional expansion. Combining topologies can produce depth, but every added topology increases the burden of legibility and continuity.

The outline should record four decisions:

1. What can increase, decrease, or transform?
2. Which routes are exclusive, reversible, or hidden?
3. What prevents immediate access to later states?
4. What changes in the world when a threshold is crossed?

# 6. Criteria for an Adequate Progression Outline

The following criteria are proposed as an audit standard. They are not a ranking of literary value, and a work may intentionally violate one. Intentional violation should nevertheless be distinguished from accidental failure.

## 6.1 Promise fit

The outline must state the experience it promises. Does the work foreground technical mastery, accumulation, discovery, social ascent, settlement growth, political leverage, survival adaptation, or some combination? “The protagonist becomes stronger” is too vague to guide selection. Promise fit is achieved when the planned scenes repeatedly deliver the kind of progress the premise advertises.

## 6.2 Baseline and target legibility

The audience needs a usable model of the starting state, immediate target, and relevant obstacle. This need not be an exposition block. It can be established through failed action, comparison, demonstration, institutional rule, or consequence. The target may later change, but the change should be motivated.

## 6.3 Causal earnedness

Advancement should arise through an intelligible chain of prerequisites, attempts, feedback, choices, resources, relationships, and costs. Research on narrative representation indicates that causal and goal-related links support coherent event models [10-12]. Applied to progression fantasy, this gives high confidence to the recommendation that major gains be embedded in visible causal structure.

## 6.4 Functional consequence

Every major gain should change at least one later decision, risk, opportunity, relationship, or opponent response. The outline should name the first scene in which the gain matters and at least one later scene in which its second-order effects matter.

## 6.5 Calibrated resistance

A new capability should meet resistance appropriate to what it can now do. Appropriate resistance is not always a numerically stronger foe. It may be a countermeasure, coordination problem, incomplete information, conflicting duty, scarce resource, time pressure, public scrutiny, or a situation in which force is the wrong instrument.

Learning research distinguishes temporary performance from durable learning [28] and emphasizes that useful challenge depends partly on the learner's current skill and the task's informational demands [29]. Fiction need not simulate laboratory learning, but the distinction supports a structural practice: test new capacities in altered conditions before treating them as mastered.

## 6.6 Agency and strategic choice

Progress should increase meaningful choice rather than merely automate victory. Self-determination research identifies competence, autonomy, and relatedness as important psychological needs [22], and a motivational model of game engagement links satisfaction of those needs to engagement in games [23]. The transfer to reading is analogical, not direct. Even so, a progression design that provides competence while eliminating autonomy and relationships risks becoming mechanically impressive but dramatically thin.

## 6.7 Cost and opportunity cost

A cost is anything the protagonist must expend, risk, surrender, postpone, reveal, or become responsible for. Opportunity cost is especially valuable because it preserves agency: choosing one path forecloses another. Costs should follow the scale and nature of the gain, but they need not be punitive or symmetrical. The aim is not to make advancement miserable. It is to keep it situated within scarcity and value conflict.

## 6.8 Adaptive world and opposition

The setting should update in response to demonstrated capability. Observers revise beliefs, institutions regulate, markets reprice, teachers change tasks, enemies develop counters, allies make demands, and dependents alter expectations. A world that ignores advancement makes the gain feel detached from reality.

## 6.9 Multiaxial integration

Capability, knowledge, resources, status, relationships, and liabilities should not move as independent spreadsheets. A promotion may grant archive access, increase scrutiny, strain friendships, and create debt. A technical breakthrough may change the protagonist's moral responsibility. Integration produces narrative density because one event alters several systems without requiring unrelated subplots.

## 6.10 Character and thematic relevance

The protagonist's method of advancement should place pressure on values and identity. The relevant question is not merely “Can the protagonist reach the next state?” but “What kind of person, relationship, or society does this route of advancement produce?” Progression becomes thematic when means and ends can be compared.

## 6.11 Nested closure and renewal

Serial narrative must balance continuation with satisfaction. O'Sullivan identifies iteration, multiplicity, momentum, world-building, personnel, and design as elements of serial narrative [7], and his account of serial satisfaction emphasizes the problem of balancing incompletion with forms of fulfillment [8]. An outline should therefore close the primary question appropriate to each scale while opening a different or enlarged question.

- A scene closes an immediate action or decision.
- A sequence closes a tactical objective.
- An arc closes a threshold, trial, or local problem.
- A book closes its governing dramatic question and changes the protagonist's state irreversibly.
- A series closes the macro promise and shows what the accumulated progression means.

A cliffhanger can intensify continuation, but it cannot replace closure at every scale.

## 6.12 Scalability and endpoint control

The outline should identify how the system remains dramatically usable as capability expands. This requires more than inventing higher ranks. It may involve specialization, political consequences, vulnerability shifts, collective responsibilities, qualitative transformations, or a finite endpoint. Without such planning, earlier relationships and places may become irrelevant, and the series may be forced into endless cosmological inflation.

## 6.13 Consistency and auditability

Rules, exceptions, costs, distances, time, resources, and prior gains should be inspectable. The outline need not expose every fact to the reader, but the author should distinguish three knowledge layers:

1. what is objectively true in the constructed world;
2. what characters believe;
3. what the audience has been given reason to believe.

Many apparent contradictions are legitimate differences among these layers. Unmarked slippage among them is not.

## 6.14 Revision flexibility

Writing is a recursive process involving planning, translating, reviewing, and the revision of goals [30]. A useful outline should support that recursion. It should preserve causal dependencies and state changes while allowing local scenes, discoveries, and expressions to evolve. Over-specification can make revision expensive; under-specification can hide contradictions. The appropriate resolution is layered outlining, discussed below.

# 7. Worldbuilding as a Progression Ecology

## 7.1 From power system to ecology

A progression system is often outlined as a catalog of ranks and abilities. That is insufficient because abilities exist inside distributions of knowledge, material, labor, law, status, and risk. Worldbuilding scholarship treats imaginary worlds as constructed systems whose coherence extends beyond the immediate plot [4]. For progression fantasy, the relevant extension is an ecology of advancement: the total arrangement that produces, restricts, recognizes, exploits, and contests progress.

A progression ecology should answer seven questions:

1. What can agents become capable of doing?
2. What inputs, conditions, and institutions make that change possible?
3. Who controls those inputs and why?
4. How is achievement recognized, measured, concealed, or disputed?
5. What individual and collective costs follow from advancement?
6. What countermeasures or balancing forces exist?
7. How has the availability of advancement already changed society?

If an outline cannot answer these questions at the level relevant to the plot, the setting may function only as a vending machine for upgrades.

## 7.2 The rule schema

Before plotting individual gains, define the system's operations. The following schema is intentionally broader than a magic-system worksheet.

| Element | Planning question | Failure if omitted |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | What phenomena can the system affect? | Abilities expand whenever convenient. |
| Unit or category | How are differences represented or discussed? | Comparisons become arbitrary or unintelligible. |
| Operation | What does an agent actually do to produce an effect? | Power becomes a label rather than action. |
| Prerequisite | What must already be true? | Later states have no causal foundation. |
| Acquisition channel | Training, initiation, discovery, inheritance, contract, craft, theft, collective process, or another route? | All gains feel interchangeable. |
| Resource input | What is consumed, occupied, risked, or maintained? | Scarcity appears only when the plot needs it. |
| Feedback | How does an agent know an attempt worked, failed, or partly worked? | Learning scenes cannot generate information. |
| Failure mode | What can go wrong, and with what consequences? | Trials lack diagnostic content. |
| Reliability | Under what conditions does the capability work? | One success is treated as universal mastery. |
| Countermeasure | How can others resist, redirect, detect, or exploit it? | Opposition can respond only by becoming stronger. |
| Transfer | Can it be taught, copied, inherited, sold, stolen, or standardized? | Social distribution becomes implausible. |
| Visibility | Who can detect use or advancement? | Reputation and secrecy change arbitrarily. |
| Ceiling | What creates hard or soft limits? | Scale has no endpoint. |
| Exception governance | Why do exceptions exist, and how are they bounded? | The system dissolves under special cases. |
| Social meaning | What moral, religious, legal, or cultural interpretations surround it? | Advancement has no thematic or institutional depth. |

The outline need not explain all of this to the audience. It should, however, preserve enough author-side structure to decide what each event can plausibly change.

## 7.3 Bottlenecks

Progression requires bottlenecks, but a bottleneck is not synonymous with a rank requirement. It is any condition that prevents immediate attainment. Useful categories include:

- **knowledge bottlenecks:** the relevant method is unknown, secret, ambiguous, or difficult to verify;
- **resource bottlenecks:** material, energy, money, time, land, or bodies are scarce;
- **skill bottlenecks:** success requires discrimination, timing, coordination, endurance, or judgment;
- **access bottlenecks:** institutions, geography, law, prejudice, or social networks control entry;
- **risk bottlenecks:** failure threatens injury, death, corruption, exposure, or collateral harm;
- **identity bottlenecks:** progress requires a commitment the protagonist resists;
- **coordination bottlenecks:** no individual can act alone, and collective trust is difficult;
- **trade-off bottlenecks:** two valuable outcomes cannot both be maximized;
- **measurement bottlenecks:** no reliable test distinguishes real progress from temporary performance;
- **ecological bottlenecks:** extracting power damages the system that supplies it.

An outline should vary bottleneck type. Repeatedly solving resource scarcity with a rarer resource creates only a longer shopping list. Variation changes the kind of agency required.

## 7.4 Distribution and inequality

If advancement is powerful, the setting must contain an explanation for its distribution. “Most people never tried” is rarely adequate when the benefits are obvious. The outline should model some combination of aptitude, cost, access, risk, institutional control, path dependence, inherited advantage, information asymmetry, ideology, legal restriction, and collective-action problems.

This does not require exhaustive political economy. It requires consequences proportional to the power being described. If one trained person can replace a hundred laborers, defeat an army, cure disease, or alter weather, institutions will respond. The world may regulate practitioners, monopolize schools, create insurance, organize counterforces, build prestige systems, or normalize the capability through infrastructure. The absence of response itself needs a reason.

## 7.5 Institutions as active systems

Schools, guilds, sects, armies, corporations, temples, clans, and governments should not exist merely to hand the protagonist a curriculum. Each institution should have:

- a public function;
- a resource base;
- a selection mechanism;
- an internal status system;
- incentives that may diverge from its public purpose;
- methods of preserving knowledge;
- mechanisms for discipline and exclusion;
- factions or role conflicts;
- a response to exceptional talent;
- a relationship to rival institutions and nonmembers.

These elements produce plot without requiring every institutional actor to be irrationally hostile or admiring. A gifted protagonist may be valuable, dangerous, expensive, politically embarrassing, difficult to classify, or all five.

## 7.6 The “why not everyone?” audit

For every high-value capability, ask:

1. Why does the protagonist seek it?
2. Why do others seek or avoid it?
3. Why has it not already transformed every relevant institution?
4. What prevents widespread imitation?
5. What would happen if the bottleneck disappeared?
6. Who benefits from preserving the bottleneck?
7. Who is harmed by it?
8. Which answer is objective, and which is propaganda or mistaken belief?

The last question is essential. A society may falsely believe only nobles can wield a power. The author still needs to know why that false belief persisted and what follows when it is challenged.

## 7.7 Cultural inheritance and xianxia-related forms

English-language progression fantasy frequently draws on cultivation structures associated with Chinese web fiction and earlier wuxia and xianxia traditions. Scholarship on Chinese internet literature situates online fiction within specific technological, commercial, linguistic, and political conditions [5]. More recent work on Jin Yong and web novelists also emphasizes continuities and transformations in martial protagonists and cultivation-oriented forms [6]. These sources do not license a single account of all xianxia, but they do show why a rank ladder cannot substitute for cultural understanding.

An outline that borrows terms such as *dao*, *qi*, cultivation, sect, meridian, or tribulation should determine whether those concepts are being used historically, philosophically, generically, or as newly invented mechanics. Names should not be treated as interchangeable decoration. When a project makes substantive cultural claims, specialized primary and scholarly research is required beyond the general framework offered here.

# 8. Character, Motivation, and the Meaning of Advancement

## 8.1 Advancement needs a motivational architecture

A protagonist who “wants to get stronger” has a direction but not yet a complete motive. Strength is normally instrumental. The outline should distinguish at least five layers:

1. **Immediate objective:** the result pursued in the current scene or sequence.
2. **Strategic objective:** the nearer threshold, license, rank, technique, cure, victory, or escape.
3. **Terminal value:** what the protagonist ultimately wants power to protect, obtain, express, or avoid losing.
4. **Identity claim:** what becoming capable would prove about who the protagonist is.
5. **Avoidance structure:** the feared state, memory, dependency, humiliation, guilt, or vulnerability driving action.

These layers may conflict. A protagonist can pursue mastery to protect a sibling while also using the pursuit to avoid intimacy. Such conflict prevents the progression track from becoming psychologically self-explanatory.

Goal-setting research supports the importance of specific and demanding goals under appropriate conditions [24], while self-efficacy concerns beliefs about one's capacity to organize and execute action [25]. These findings concern human motivation, not fictional quality. Their careful application is to help the outline differentiate goal clarity, perceived attainability, feedback, and persistence. They do not imply that every fictional protagonist should behave like an optimized worker.

## 8.2 Three motives for every major threshold

For each major advancement, record:

- the protagonist's stated reason;
- the protagonist's less acknowledged reason;
- the structural reason the story needs the threshold.

The three need not coincide. A protagonist may say a promotion is necessary to access an archive, privately crave recognition, and structurally need the promotion so that the plot can move from local apprenticeship to institutional responsibility. Recording all three exposes scenes that advance the system but not the character, or the character but not the story.

## 8.3 Competence, autonomy, and relatedness

Self-determination theory identifies competence, autonomy, and relatedness as basic psychological needs [22]. Research applying the theory to video games argues that their satisfaction contributes to engagement [23]. It is not established that progression-fantasy readers respond through exactly the same mechanism. The analogy is nevertheless productive because progression narratives can stage all three:

- **competence:** the protagonist becomes more effective and can recognize improvement;
- **autonomy:** the protagonist selects methods, specializations, risks, or values rather than merely receiving assignments;
- **relatedness:** advancement changes bonds, dependencies, loyalties, rivalries, mentorships, and obligations.

An outline dominated by competence alone may become a technical record. One dominated by externally mandated quests may deny autonomy. One that treats companions as spectators may fail to integrate relatedness. The aim is not equal emphasis but conscious design.

## 8.4 Power and identity can align or diverge

Use a two-axis matrix to define the relation between external progression and internal development.

| External capacity | Internal development | Typical dramatic question |
|---|---|---|
| Increases | Integrates or matures | Can the protagonist become capable without losing the values that justified the quest? |
| Increases | Corrupts or fragments | What does success turn the protagonist into? |
| Decreases | Integrates or matures | Can loss produce a more responsible form of agency? |
| Plateaus | Reorients | Is the original ladder the right measure of progress? |
| Transforms qualitatively | Remains unresolved | Can a new mode of action solve what the old self could not understand? |

The outline should state which relation governs each book. Otherwise, internal change is often added retrospectively as dialogue that does not alter choices.

## 8.5 The choice architecture of progression

A meaningful progression choice has at least two live options with different value profiles. It is not enough to offer a dominant option and an obviously foolish one. Choices can concern:

- specialization;
- speed versus safety;
- private mastery versus public responsibility;
- individual advancement versus collective investment;
- secrecy versus legitimacy;
- reversible growth versus irreversible transformation;
- power versus relationship;
- immediate rescue versus long-term prevention;
- using an exploit versus preserving the system;
- accepting a patron's resources versus incurring control.

The outline should preserve the consequences of the rejected option. If every branch immediately converges without residue, the choice is cosmetic.

## 8.6 Cost as characterization

The same objective price can mean different things to different protagonists. A year of training is cheap to an immortal and devastating to a parent with a sick child. Public recognition is a reward to one character and exposure to another. Cost therefore needs three entries:

1. objective expenditure or risk;
2. subjective meaning to the protagonist;
3. social distribution of the burden.

The third prevents the outline from hiding externalized costs. A protagonist may advance because servants, ecosystems, soldiers, or communities absorb the damage. Recognizing that distribution creates thematic and political consequences.

## 8.7 Relationships must update

For every major threshold, identify at least three relationship responses:

- someone who benefits;
- someone whose expectations or fears increase;
- someone whose prior relation becomes harder to sustain.

This is not a mandatory cast formula. It is an audit against the common error of treating social relations as frozen while the protagonist's power, status, and obligations radically change.

## 8.8 Mentors, rivals, and dependents

These roles serve different structural functions.

**Mentors** compress knowledge, model standards, control access, and eventually become incomplete guides. A mentor who remains the sole source of correct answers prevents epistemic independence.

**Rivals** provide comparison, alternative methods, and counterfactual paths. A rival need not be hostile. The strongest rival structure makes the protagonist's choices visible by embodying another viable route.

**Dependents** convert power into responsibility. They also expose whether progression benefits anyone beyond the protagonist. Dependents should retain agency rather than exist only as motivational hostages.

# 9. Opposition, Counterplay, and Difficulty

## 9.1 Difficulty is relational

An obstacle is difficult relative to a particular state, goal, environment, and cost tolerance. Consequently, the outline should not label challenges simply “easy,” “medium,” or “hard.” It should state why this protagonist, at this stage, under these conditions, cannot readily solve the problem.

A useful representation is:

$$
D_t = \Psi(G_t, S_t, E_t, O_t, X_t)
$$

where $G_t$ is the current goal, $S_t$ the protagonist state, $E_t$ the environment, $O_t$ active opposition, and $X_t$ unacceptable costs or constraints. Difficulty changes when any component changes.

## 9.2 Types of opposition

A long progression narrative needs more than a sequence of stronger fighters. Opposition can be:

- **symmetric:** an agent pursuing similar capabilities through a competing route;
- **asymmetric:** an agent or system that counters the protagonist's strengths rather than matching them;
- **institutional:** law, bureaucracy, hierarchy, ownership, professional standards, or collective interests;
- **ecological:** terrain, scarcity, disease, instability, or systemic limits;
- **epistemic:** hidden causes, unreliable measurement, deception, or incomplete models;
- **temporal:** deadlines, recovery time, seasonal windows, compounding threats;
- **relational:** incompatible obligations, mistrust, dependence, or coordination failure;
- **internal:** fear, compulsion, divided identity, trauma, or a cost embedded in the power;
- **ethical:** a goal that can be reached only by means the protagonist rejects;
- **scale-mismatched:** a capability effective against individuals but inadequate for institutions or networks.

Varying opposition type permits continued difficulty without invalidating prior progress.

## 9.3 The opponent update rule

Once the protagonist demonstrates a capability, any informed and capable opponent should update. The outline should track:

1. who observes or receives reports;
2. what they correctly infer;
3. what they misinterpret;
4. how quickly they can respond;
5. which countermeasures are available;
6. what those countermeasures cost;
7. how their response creates new information for the protagonist.

This produces an adaptive loop:

$$
\text{demonstration} \rightarrow \text{observation} \rightarrow \text{inference} \rightarrow \text{counterplay} \rightarrow \text{adaptation}
$$

If counterplay appears without observation or knowledge, it feels authorially imposed. If informed opponents never adapt, they become training equipment.

## 9.4 Fairness and asymmetry

Narrative fairness does not require that the audience know every rule before use. It requires a reasonable relation among prior information, surprise, and retrospective intelligibility. A new opponent ability can surprise while remaining fair if it follows from established domains, costs, institutions, or clues. A protagonist victory can be surprising while fair if it recombines known capacities or reveals a prepared interpretation.

The outline should classify major climax elements as:

- explicitly established;
- inferable from established material;
- foreshadowed but ambiguous;
- genuinely new and therefore unable to bear the main causal burden of resolution.

This classification is an epistemic audit, not a ban on novelty.

## 9.5 Testing transfer rather than repetition

Expert performance depends on domain-specific structures, feedback, and extended practice, but deliberate practice does not explain all variance in performance [26-27]. Learning research also distinguishes durable, transferable learning from short-term performance gains [28]. A fiction outline should not mimic empirical training schedules. It can, however, use the distinction between repetition and transfer.

After a capability is learned, test it under at least one changed condition:

- different opponent;
- reduced resource;
- altered environment;
- incomplete information;
- moral restriction;
- team coordination;
- time pressure;
- public scrutiny;
- interference by a countermeasure;
- need to teach or explain it to another person.

A repeated success establishes reliability. A transformed success establishes understanding.

## 9.6 The opportunity-liability pair

For every major gain, identify:

- one new opportunity;
- one new liability, obligation, exposure, dependency, or countermeasure.

This does not mean every reward must be poisoned. It means that changing one's action space also changes one's relation to the world. Greater range may create detection. Rank may create command responsibility. A rare technique may attract recruitment. Wealth may become difficult to protect. Healing capacity may produce impossible demand. The pair generates future plot from the gain itself.

# 10. Multiscale Narrative Architecture

## 10.1 Why scales must be distinguished

A progression narrative operates simultaneously at multiple temporal resolutions. Confusion arises when a requirement suitable for one scale is imposed on another. Every scene need not contain a rank increase. Every book should ordinarily contain a consequential change in the progression state. Every series cannot postpone the meaning of advancement until the last page.

A practical hierarchy is:

- **beat:** one perception, decision, action, or reversal;
- **scene:** a bounded unit of goal-directed action and changed situation;
- **sequence:** several scenes organized around a tactical objective;
- **progression arc:** a process from identified limitation through acquisition or reorganization to tested consequence;
- **book:** a governing dramatic question resolved through one or more intertwined arcs;
- **series:** the macro transformation promised by the premise.

## 10.2 The micro loop

At scene and sequence scale, a common progression loop is:

$$
\text{attempt} \rightarrow \text{feedback} \rightarrow \text{interpretation} \rightarrow \text{adaptation} \rightarrow \text{test}
$$

This is not a compulsory scene formula. Some scenes establish desire, reveal cost, compare states, or show consequence. The loop is useful because it prevents training from becoming undifferentiated effort. Feedback must change a model or a choice.

A minimum viable progression scene usually changes at least one of the following:

- ability;
- information;
- resource;
- access;
- commitment;
- relationship;
- constraint;
- expectation of a future threshold.

## 10.3 The meso arc

A progression arc can be represented as nine functions:

1. **limitation exposed:** the present state fails or proves insufficient;
2. **target specified:** a reachable next state becomes intelligible;
3. **route selected:** the protagonist commits to a method;
4. **prerequisites assembled:** knowledge, resources, access, or allies are obtained;
5. **attempt and feedback:** the route produces diagnostic success or failure;
6. **adaptation or sacrifice:** the protagonist revises method or accepts cost;
7. **threshold crossed:** a capability or position changes;
8. **transfer test:** the new state is used under altered conditions;
9. **integration:** the world, relationships, and next objective update.

Not every arc needs one scene per function, and functions may repeat or overlap. The model is diagnostic. If a breakthrough feels arbitrary, the missing function can often be identified.

## 10.4 The macro book movement

A book should not be outlined merely as “reach rank three.” It needs a dramatic question whose resolution requires, tests, and interprets the progression. Examples include:

- Can an unlicensed healer become capable enough to stop an epidemic without legitimizing the guild that caused the shortage?
- Can a disgraced officer rebuild a defensive company before the frontier city falls?
- Can an apprentice learn to enter dreams without surrendering memories that anchor identity?

The rank, company size, or dream technique matters because it changes the answer. At the end, the reader should be able to state:

1. what central problem was resolved;
2. how the protagonist's state changed;
3. what that change cost or obligated;
4. why the next book poses a genuinely new problem rather than the same one at a larger number.

## 10.5 The macro series movement

The series spine should identify a finite transformation. “Become stronger forever” cannot determine completion. A stronger series promise names an endpoint such as:

- gain enough agency to confront the source of dependency;
- transform or escape an institution that controls advancement;
- master a field and decide how it should be used;
- build a durable collective capable of surviving without the protagonist;
- discover the system's origin and choose whether to preserve it;
- reconcile human-scale values with transhuman capability.

The endpoint can remain partly unknown during early drafting, but the outline should at least specify the kind of question that can end.

## 10.6 Progression pulses

Not every installment needs acquisition. It should, however, ordinarily service the progression promise through one or more pulses:

1. baseline demonstration;
2. target clarification;
3. rule or system discovery;
4. preparation or resource acquisition;
5. attempt;
6. feedback;
7. setback that yields information;
8. cost or constraint revelation;
9. comparison with another path;
10. actual advancement;
11. application;
12. counterplay;
13. social consequence;
14. integration or new choice.

An outline can mark pulses to diagnose long stretches in which the governing promise disappears. There is no universal ratio of pulse types or fixed number per chapter.

## 10.7 Nested closure

Closure should be planned as a hierarchy of answered questions.

| Scale | Question type | Satisfactory closure | Continuing opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scene | What happens when this goal meets this obstacle now? | An action, decision, discovery, or failure changes the situation. | The consequence creates the next local problem. |
| Sequence | Can the tactical objective be achieved? | The objective is achieved, abandoned, transformed, or decisively lost. | A broader cost or implication becomes active. |
| Arc | Can the limitation be overcome by this route? | The threshold is crossed, rejected, or redefined and tested. | The new state creates different constraints. |
| Book | Can the protagonist answer the governing dramatic question? | The primary conflict reaches a determinate outcome. | A larger conflict follows from the outcome. |
| Series | What does the pursuit of advancement finally make possible or mean? | The macro transformation and its consequences are shown. | The world may continue, but the promise no longer requires another installment. |

Serial works depend on momentum, but momentum is not identical to perpetual interruption. A book ending that stops before its governing question is answered may function as an installment break, but repeated use converts anticipation into unpaid promissory debt.

## 10.8 The promissory debt ledger

Maintain a ledger with five columns:

- promise, question, rule, mystery, or relationship established;
- location established;
- expected scale of payoff;
- latest acceptable payoff window;
- actual payoff, revision, or deliberate cancellation.

This ledger does not require every detail to become a plot twist. It prevents the accumulation of signals that the work later ignores. Curiosity can arise from perceived information gaps [15], while suspense can persist even when outcomes are known because readers process possibilities dynamically [14]. The design implication is that uncertainty should be managed through meaningful questions, not through indefinite withholding alone.

# 11. Information Design, Suspense, and Reader Legibility

## 11.1 Three information models

At every major point, distinguish:

1. **world truth:** what is actually the case within the fiction;
2. **character model:** what each relevant agent believes;
3. **reader model:** what the text has made reasonable for the audience to believe.

The three may diverge. A mentor can teach a false theory. A protagonist can correctly use an ability for the wrong reason. The audience can know a countermeasure that the protagonist does not. Such divergences generate suspense and dramatic irony. They become defects only when the outline loses track of them.

## 11.2 Legibility without exposition overload

Narrative engagement includes understanding, attentional focus, emotional engagement, and a sense of presence [16-17]. Explanation that interrupts action can damage some of these dimensions, but opacity can damage understanding. The solution is not a universal amount of exposition. It is functional distribution.

System information can be conveyed through:

- failed attempts;
- demonstrations and comparisons;
- choices where rules matter;
- consequences of misuse;
- teaching under pressure;
- institutional tests;
- disagreements among practitioners;
- resource accounting that affects plans;
- opponent countermeasures;
- retrospective reinterpretation after new evidence.

Each explanation should enable prediction, choice, reinterpretation, or consequence. Information that never affects any of these may be world detail rather than progression structure.

## 11.3 The prediction test

After establishing a rule, ask what a careful reader could predict. The prediction need not identify the exact event, but it should constrain possibilities. If no future inference can be drawn, the rule may be too vague. If every outcome is fully determined, suspense may diminish. Productive legibility gives bounded uncertainty.

## 11.4 Mystery about a system versus arbitrary concealment

A system mystery is fair when:

- the unknown is marked as unknown;
- available observations are consistent with the eventual explanation;
- alternative hypotheses are possible;
- characters can investigate;
- discovery changes action;
- the answer does not invalidate all prior constraints without replacement.

Arbitrary concealment occurs when the outline withholds information that viewpoint characters would naturally know only to surprise the reader, or when a new exception appears solely to solve the current problem.

## 11.5 Reveals should revise models

A reveal is structurally valuable when it changes at least one of:

- what the protagonist thinks the next target is;
- which route appears viable;
- the understood cost of progress;
- the identity or incentives of opposition;
- the meaning of a prior success;
- the scale of the governing question;
- the ethical interpretation of the system.

A reveal that adds lore without revising action can be interesting, but it is not carrying progression structure.

# 12. Pacing, Variance, Plateaus, and Regression

## 12.1 Pacing is change management

Pacing is often treated as speed. More precisely, it is the reader's experience of the rate, magnitude, density, and significance of change. A rapid sequence of nearly identical gains can feel static. A slow investigation can feel urgent if each discovery restructures the problem.

For progression outlining, track seven variables:

- **frequency:** how often progress-related change occurs;
- **magnitude:** how large each change is;
- **domain:** which dimension changes;
- **certainty:** whether the change is secure, provisional, or misunderstood;
- **valence:** whether the state improves, worsens, or becomes mixed;
- **visibility:** who recognizes the change;
- **reversibility:** how easily it can be lost or undone.

Variance across these dimensions prevents the outline from producing a metronomic sequence of train, win, rank, repeat.

## 12.2 The cadence fallacy

There is no evidence for a universal rule such as one advancement every three chapters. Cadence depends on chapter length, release medium, subgenre, arc scale, and whether scenes provide other progression pulses. A daily web serial and a tightly revised print novel may require different local rhythms while serving the same macro structure.

Use promise coverage rather than a fixed quota. Mark what each chapter delivers: attempt, discovery, cost, comparison, breakthrough, application, counterplay, or consequence. A long interval without acquisition can remain progression-centered if the interval transforms understanding and makes the eventual threshold causally richer.

## 12.3 Plateaus

A plateau is not absence of progress. It can serve four functions:

1. consolidate a recent gain;
2. expose hidden weaknesses in apparent mastery;
3. shift attention to another progression dimension;
4. force reconsideration of the current route.

A plateau becomes stagnant when it neither changes the protagonist's model nor increases the significance of later action. The outline should give every planned plateau an exit condition.

## 12.4 Setbacks

A setback can reduce state while increasing knowledge, commitment, or strategic range. It should not merely restore an earlier status quo. Record its residue:

- what is permanently lost;
- what is learned;
- which relation changes;
- which option closes;
- which new route becomes necessary;
- what the opponent gains;
- how the protagonist's self-model changes.

If none of these persists, the setback is likely a delay rather than a development.

## 12.5 Regression and de-powering

De-powering can restore local difficulty, but it often feels manipulative when introduced solely to make old obstacles threatening again. It is better justified when it follows from established cost, chosen sacrifice, enemy counterplay, or thematic transformation. It should also preserve accumulated competence where appropriate. Losing a tool need not erase judgment, relationships, or knowledge.

## 12.6 Breakthrough density and significance

Too many breakthroughs can reduce each one's distinction. Too few can underdeliver the genre promise. The appropriate number depends on scale, but significance can be protected through differentiated functions:

- one breakthrough expands basic competence;
- another changes specialization;
- another grants social recognition;
- another reveals a liability;
- another enables collective action;
- another transforms the protagonist's understanding of the entire system.

The point is not to disguise identical rank increases with different names. It is to ensure that thresholds change different relations.

# 13. An Ordered Protocol for Outlining Progression Fantasy

## 13.1 How to use the protocol

The protocol has 28 steps in six phases. The order is epistemic rather than compulsory. Earlier steps answer questions that constrain later ones, but outlining remains recursive [30]. A discovery at the scene level may require revision of the system, protagonist, or book contract. The author should therefore preserve version history and distinguish stable commitments from provisional choices.

Each step produces an artifact and has a failure condition. The artifacts can be brief for a single novel or extensive for a long series. The objective is not paperwork. It is to make consequential assumptions visible before they become contradictions.

## Phase 0: Define the project and its claims

### Step 1: Specify medium, unit, audience, and scope

**Purpose.** Establish the production conditions under which the outline must function.

Record whether the project is a standalone novel, closed series, open serial, episodic sequence, or hybrid. State the expected book length, release cadence, viewpoint structure, age category, content boundaries, and intended degree of system explanation. These factors alter how often reminders, local closures, recaps, and progression pulses may be needed.

**Artifact.** A one-page project charter containing medium, projected length, publication unit, target readership, viewpoint, tonal range, and present endpoint assumption.

**Failure condition.** The same outline is expected to serve incompatible formats without adaptation, such as functioning simultaneously as a weekly serial plan and a tightly compressed standalone.

### Step 2: State the genre contract

**Purpose.** Define what kind of progression experience the work promises.

Complete the sentence: “The central satisfaction of this story comes from watching the protagonist become increasingly able to ______ through ______, while confronting ______.” The blanks should name an action domain, a process, and a source of resistance. Examples include diagnosing impossible diseases through experimental spirit medicine, building a defensible settlement through collective engineering, or mastering dream navigation while memory erodes.

Then separate **core promises** from **optional signals**. Core promises must recur. Optional signals, such as rankings, tournaments, or statistics, can be removed without changing the work's identity.

**Artifact.** A genre-contract statement and a two-column core/optional list.

**Failure condition.** The contract could describe almost any fantasy adventure because advancement has not been given a distinct process or consequence.

### Step 3: Formulate the governing dramatic question

**Purpose.** Prevent the progression track from replacing the novel's conflict.

Write a question that can be answered by the book's ending and cannot be answered merely by naming the final rank. It should connect capability to circumstance and value. “Will she reach the silver tier?” is a threshold question. “Can she reach the silver tier in time to defend the district without accepting the governor's binding contract?” is a dramatic question.

**Artifact.** One governing question, one provisional answer, and one statement of what must change for that answer to become possible.

**Failure condition.** The question remains open after the planned climax, or the answer does not depend on progression established in the book.

### Step 4: Define the series endpoint class

**Purpose.** Create a criterion for completion before scale inflation obscures it.

Do not require a fully known ending. Define the type of terminal transformation: liberation from a dependency, mastery plus a decision about use, reform or destruction of an institution, construction of a self-sustaining community, discovery of the system's origin, reconciliation with mortality, or another finite change.

State what would count as failure, partial success, and transformed success. This prevents later books from treating all endpoints as “higher.”

**Artifact.** A series endpoint statement with success conditions and non-numeric completion criteria.

**Failure condition.** The only imaginable ending is that the protagonist becomes maximally powerful, with no account of why maximality resolves the premise.

### Step 5: Create an assumption register

**Purpose.** Expose unsupported beliefs before they harden into structure.

List claims the project currently assumes, such as “readers need a fight every five chapters,” “higher ranks are always rarer,” “talent cannot be taught,” “the empire would suppress this technique,” or “the protagonist must conceal the interface.” For each, mark it as:

- world fact to be designed;
- character belief that may be false;
- genre hypothesis requiring support;
- practical constraint;
- provisional preference.

**Artifact.** An assumption table with claim, status, evidence or rationale, consequence if false, and revision trigger.

**Failure condition.** Design choices are defended only with “that is how the genre works,” especially when counterexamples are possible.

## Phase 1: Build the progression system and ecology

### Step 6: Establish the baseline state

**Purpose.** Make later change measurable and dramatically specific.

Populate the state vector $S_0 = \langle C, K, R, P, B, L \rangle$. Record what the protagonist can reliably do, what they merely believe they can do, what resources and permissions they possess, whom they can coordinate with, and which liabilities constrain action. Include at least one failed or costly action that can demonstrate the baseline in narrative form.

The baseline should not make the protagonist generically incompetent. A strong beginning can be defined by a narrow specialization, social weakness, false theory, inaccessible scale, or inability to act under particular constraints.

**Artifact.** Baseline state sheet and a planned baseline demonstration scene.

**Failure condition.** The starting state is described only by a rank, or later advancement cannot be compared with any observed limitation.

### Step 7: Select progression dimensions and topology

**Purpose.** Decide what changes and what structural routes change can take.

Choose primary, secondary, and background dimensions from capability, knowledge, resources, status, bonds, strategy, identity, and liabilities. Then select a topology or combination: ladder, tree, network, spiral, threshold, institution, cycle, collective expansion, or critical anti-progression.

For every primary dimension, state its units of comparison. These need not be numerical. They can be tasks, permissions, ranges, error rates, problem classes, or degrees of independence.

**Artifact.** A progression map showing dimensions, routes, exclusivities, and planned thresholds.

**Failure condition.** Every dimension rises together automatically, eliminating trade-offs and making one score a proxy for all agency.

### Step 8: Define operations, prerequisites, and limits

**Purpose.** Convert theme and terminology into usable causal rules.

For each major capability, specify what an agent does, what must already be true, what input is used, what feedback is available, and what stops unlimited repetition. Distinguish hard limits from soft limits. A hard limit makes an action impossible. A soft limit makes it dangerous, inefficient, illegal, socially costly, or unlikely.

Also specify reliability conditions. The fact that a spell worked once in calm weather does not establish that it works while injured, moving, deceived, or protecting bystanders.

**Artifact.** A rule schema for every ability or resource expected to carry a major plot resolution.

**Failure condition.** The climax depends on an operation whose prerequisites or domain were never defined even in the author-side model.

### Step 9: Design acquisition channels

**Purpose.** Differentiate how progress enters the story.

List the legitimate and illegitimate routes by which agents can advance: practice, instruction, initiation, experimentation, craft, contract, inheritance, extraction, theft, trade, sacrifice, institutional promotion, collective ritual, or accidental exposure. Give each route a characteristic cost, speed, risk, and social meaning.

This step prevents every gain from becoming a reskinned training montage. It also clarifies why the protagonist's route is unusual without requiring everyone else to be foolish.

**Artifact.** Acquisition-channel matrix with access, speed, risk, transferability, and institutional response.

**Failure condition.** A route becomes available only when the plot needs it, or produces the same kind of agency regardless of method.

### Step 10: Model resources, bottlenecks, and conversion

**Purpose.** Make scarcity consistent and plot-relevant.

Identify resources required for advancement and action. State their sources, rates of renewal, storage limits, transport problems, ownership, substitute goods, and conversion losses. Then map bottlenecks by type: knowledge, material, access, skill, risk, identity, coordination, measurement, or ecology.

Avoid false precision. Exact numbers are useful only when later decisions depend on them. Ranges and qualitative constraints may be sufficient.

**Artifact.** Resource ledger and bottleneck map.

**Failure condition.** Scarcity vanishes during climaxes or returns after victories without causal explanation.

### Step 11: Construct the institutional ecology

**Purpose.** Determine how society produces and reacts to advancement.

For each relevant institution, record its public function, resource base, recruitment, internal incentives, status markers, disciplinary mechanisms, knowledge controls, factions, and relation to outsiders. Include at least one reason an institution might rationally support the protagonist and one reason it might rationally restrict them.

**Artifact.** Institutional map with flows of resources, authority, information, and conflict.

**Failure condition.** Institutions behave as unified personalities, or exist only to praise, test, obstruct, or betray the protagonist.

### Step 12: Establish exceptions and their governance

**Purpose.** Preserve surprise without dissolving the system.

List known exceptions. For each, state whether it is a true rule exception, a rare condition within the rule, a measurement failure, a false social belief, or an unknown mechanism. Identify who knows about it, how it can be detected, why it has not become standard, and what new limit replaces the one it bypasses.

The protagonist may possess an original bloodline, interface, technique, or constitution. Originality should create a research problem and social response, not freedom from causality.

**Artifact.** Exception register with classification, evidence, discoverability, and consequences.

**Failure condition.** “Unique” functions as an unrestricted permission to ignore prior limits.

### Step 13: Build the capability and continuity ledger

**Purpose.** Maintain an auditable record of what every important agent can do.

For each capability, record acquisition point, demonstrated uses, known constraints, resource cost, visibility, countermeasures, current reliability, and later payoff. For series work, include versioned states by book or arc.

This ledger should track important opponents and allies as well as the protagonist. Otherwise their abilities tend to fluctuate according to immediate scene needs.

**Artifact.** Capability ledger linked to scene and arc identifiers.

**Failure condition.** A capability is forgotten when inconvenient, or appears at full mastery without intermediate state.

## Phase 2: Design protagonist, relationships, and opposition

### Step 14: Define the protagonist's motivational architecture

**Purpose.** Explain why this progression path matters to this person.

Record immediate objectives, strategic goals, terminal values, identity claims, fears, and avoidance patterns. Identify the point at which the protagonist would rationally stop advancing, and what later event changes that stopping rule.

Also record the protagonist's theory of progress: what they initially think strength, mastery, success, or freedom consists in. This theory can be wrong without making the protagonist irrational.

**Artifact.** Motivation stack and initial theory-of-progress statement.

**Failure condition.** The protagonist continues pursuing power after the original motive disappears, with no revised value or obligation.

### Step 15: Define internal arc relations

**Purpose.** Specify how capability and character development interact.

For each book, select whether external progression aligns with, corrupts, complicates, or becomes irrelevant to the needed internal change. Name one belief that advancement reinforces and one that it challenges. Plan at least one choice in which greater power cannot by itself determine the correct action.

**Artifact.** Power-arc/character-arc matrix with beginning belief, pressure, choice, and ending state.

**Failure condition.** Internal growth is summarized in reflection but never changes a decision.

### Step 16: Map relationship and collective capacity

**Purpose.** Treat social bonds as dynamic sources and objects of agency.

For each major relationship, record trust, dependence, shared goal, hidden conflict, asymmetry, and response to the protagonist's next threshold. Identify what the other person wants independently. For teams, specify roles, communication, failure points, and what coordinated actions are possible only collectively.

**Artifact.** Relationship-state map and collective capability sheet.

**Failure condition.** Supporting characters remain narratively interchangeable or respond only to the protagonist's needs.

### Step 17: Build the opposition portfolio

**Purpose.** Prevent difficulty from collapsing into rank comparison.

Select several opposition types: symmetric rival, asymmetric counter, institution, ecology, mystery, deadline, relationship, internal constraint, ethical conflict, or scale mismatch. For each, state the protagonist's current ineffective response and the kind of progression that could make a different response possible.

Opposition should have its own goals, resources, constraints, and learning process. Not every obstacle must be an antagonist with intentions.

**Artifact.** Opposition portfolio with type, goal, leverage, vulnerability, update capacity, and relation to progression dimensions.

**Failure condition.** Every obstacle is defeated by increasing the same capability.

### Step 18: Plan counterplay and second-order effects

**Purpose.** Make advancement change the conflict rather than end it automatically.

For each major public demonstration, trace observation, inference, response delay, countermeasure, cost, and unintended effect. Include allies and institutions, not only enemies. A city may depend on the protagonist after seeing a rescue; a guild may redefine licensing standards; a rival may imitate the method imperfectly.

**Artifact.** Counterplay ledger and ripple map.

**Failure condition.** New capability has no effect on how informed agents plan.

## Phase 3: Architect series, books, and arcs

### Step 19: Create the series threshold spine

**Purpose.** Arrange irreversible transformations between baseline and endpoint.

List the few states without which the terminal transformation cannot occur. These may be technical, epistemic, relational, institutional, or ethical. Order them by dependency, not simply by size. Mark which thresholds are irreversible, which can regress, and which change the governing question.

A threshold spine should be sparse. It is not a chapter outline. Its purpose is to preserve direction across books.

**Artifact.** One-page series spine showing baseline, threshold states, dependency arrows, and endpoint.

**Failure condition.** Books can be permuted without changing causality, or thresholds differ only in magnitude.

### Step 20: Write a contract for each book

**Purpose.** Give every volume local identity and closure.

For each book, record:

- governing dramatic question;
- baseline state at entry;
- promised progression focus;
- primary opposition type;
- threshold or transformation;
- irreversible cost or obligation;
- climax test;
- closed question;
- question opened by the outcome.

The opened question should arise from what the book resolves. It should not be attached as unrelated sequel bait.

**Artifact.** One-page book contract.

**Failure condition.** The volume functions only as transit between ranks or ends before its central question receives a determinate answer.

### Step 21: Braid the progression, plot, character, and world arcs

**Purpose.** Prevent parallel tracks that touch only at the climax.

Create separate lines for progression, external plot, internal character change, relationships, opposition, and world or institutional change. Mark intersection events where one scene advances at least two lines. Then check for any line that disappears for an extended interval or reaches its climax independently.

The aim is not maximal density in every scene. Quiet or single-purpose scenes may be necessary. The aim is causal interdependence at arc scale.

**Artifact.** Arc braid or swim-lane chart.

**Failure condition.** Removing the training arc leaves the plot unchanged, or removing the external conflict leaves the progression sequence unchanged.

### Step 22: Construct a causal and dependency graph

**Purpose.** Replace chronological plausibility with causal necessity or motivated possibility.

Represent major events as nodes. Connect them with typed edges: causes, enables, motivates, informs, constrains, promises, or pays off. Every climax-enabling action should have a path backward to earlier setup. Every major early setup should have a payoff, revision, or deliberate abandonment.

Narrative research gives strong support to the role of causal and goal relations in event representation [10-12]. The graph operationalizes that insight for outlining.

**Artifact.** Causal graph and orphan-node list.

**Failure condition.** Key events occur because “it is time for them,” or major nodes have no incoming preparation and no outgoing consequence.

### Step 23: Design the information and revelation architecture

**Purpose.** Control what truth, characters, and readers know at each stage.

For every major rule, secret, and misconception, record world truth, holder of knowledge, reader evidence, current hypotheses, reveal mechanism, and action changed by the reveal. Identify whether suspense comes from uncertainty about outcome, method, cost, identity, interpretation, or timing.

**Artifact.** Information-state matrix and promissory debt ledger.

**Failure condition.** The outline relies on viewpoint characters not thinking about knowledge they plainly possess, or reveals do not revise action.

## Phase 4: Convert architecture into sequences and scenes

### Step 24: Distribute progression pulses

**Purpose.** Maintain the genre contract without forcing constant breakthroughs.

Mark each proposed sequence or chapter with one or more pulses: demonstration, target, discovery, preparation, attempt, feedback, setback, cost, comparison, advancement, application, counterplay, social consequence, or integration. Examine long gaps and repetitive runs.

Vary magnitude, dimension, certainty, and valence. A small epistemic gain between two large capability gains may produce better rhythm than a third capability increase.

**Artifact.** Progression-pulse map.

**Failure condition.** Long sections deliver neither progression nor meaningful pressure on progression, or several consecutive chapters repeat the same pulse with no changed condition.

### Step 25: Backward-chain the climax

**Purpose.** Ensure the climax is both prepared and transformative.

Begin with the decisive action or choice. Ask what capability, knowledge, resource, relationship, position, and liability make it possible. For each prerequisite, ask where it was acquired, demonstrated, complicated, and made costly. Then identify what the opponent knows and why its countermeasure is insufficient but reasonable.

The climax should usually require both accumulated state and present choice. If the prepared state automatically produces victory, the climax may lack agency. If choice succeeds without prepared capacity, the progression may be irrelevant.

**Artifact.** Climax prerequisite tree and fairness classification.

**Failure condition.** Resolution depends principally on a new unprepared exception, or any earlier rank increase could be removed without changing the decisive act.

### Step 26: Write stateful scene cards

**Purpose.** Make each scene's structural work explicit while preserving prose freedom.

A scene card should contain:

- entry state;
- viewpoint and local goal;
- active obstacle or constraint;
- information available and withheld;
- meaningful choice;
- action and immediate result;
- feedback or interpretation;
- state changes across $C, K, R, P, B, L$;
- causal output to later scenes;
- promise served;
- setup, payoff, or counterplay links;
- exit question or changed situation.

Not every field needs a long answer. If no state, knowledge, relation, or commitment changes, the scene needs a different justification such as aesthetic pause, atmosphere, thematic contrast, or comic relief. That justification should be deliberate.

**Artifact.** Scene cards with stable identifiers.

**Failure condition.** The card describes only conversation topic or action content, not what becomes different.

### Step 27: Design transitions and compression

**Purpose.** Preserve causal clarity while controlling length.

Decide which processes require scene treatment and which can be summarized. Dramatize moments of choice, diagnostic feedback, changed method, irreversible cost, first successful application, and socially consequential demonstration. Compress routine repetition once the relevant pattern is established, unless variation itself is the point.

At each transition, state what carries forward: goal, consequence, question, deadline, emotional residue, resource deficit, or changed model. This reduces episodic drift.

**Artifact.** Transition notes and dramatize/compress decisions.

**Failure condition.** Important mastery occurs in summary while repetitive low-information actions receive full scenes, or chapters connect only by chronology.

## Phase 5: Audit, stress-test, and revise

### Step 28: Run the full audit suite and update recursively

**Purpose.** Test the outline as a system before and during drafting.

Perform the following audits:

- state continuity;
- causal graph completeness;
- capability setup and payoff;
- resource conservation;
- rule and exception consistency;
- opponent knowledge and counterplay;
- relationship response;
- progression-promise coverage;
- pacing variance;
- climax fairness;
- nested closure;
- endpoint scalability;
- cultural and factual research needs;
- assumption register review.

Then classify findings as contradiction, unsupported assumption, weak integration, optional enhancement, or intentional deviation. Fix contradictions and unsupported causal dependencies before polishing low-level detail.

**Artifact.** Versioned audit report, change log, and revised stable-state documents.

**Failure condition.** The outline is treated as finished and defended against discoveries made during drafting. Recursive revision is not failure. It is part of the writing process [30].

# 14. Alternative Outlining Modes

The protocol above is ordered for clarity, not to privilege one creative process. Different projects can enter through different questions. What should remain stable are the functional requirements: a defined promise, state change, causal integration, consequential choice, adaptive context, and closure.

## 14.1 System-first outlining

**Starting question:** What rule-governed form of agency is worth exploring?

The author first designs operations, constraints, acquisition channels, and social consequences, then constructs characters whose motives expose the system's tensions. This mode suits works in which discovery, optimization, or system critique is central.

**Strengths:** high mechanical coherence; strong capacity for foreshadowing, counterplay, and problem solving.

**Risks:** characters become demonstration instruments; explanation outruns conflict; system elegance is mistaken for narrative value.

**Required corrective:** every major rule must be linked to a desire, choice, relationship, or institutional consequence.

## 14.2 Character-first outlining

**Starting question:** What does this person need power, mastery, or status to do, and what would acquiring it threaten?

The progression system is designed around the protagonist's values, limitations, fears, and relationships. This mode suits psychologically centered or thematic works.

**Strengths:** advancement has immediate meaning; internal and external arcs integrate naturally.

**Risks:** rules become elastic to fit emotional beats; opponents lack independent logic; the world revolves around the protagonist.

**Required corrective:** maintain an author-side ecology and test whether the same rules produce plausible outcomes for other agents.

## 14.3 Ecology-first outlining

**Starting question:** How has a form of power reorganized society, and where are its contradictions?

Institutions, labor, scarcity, regulation, class, and culture are designed first. The protagonist occupies a pressure point in that system.

**Strengths:** social consequence; credible institutions; multiple forms of opposition; strong collective progression.

**Risks:** macro explanation displaces personal urgency; the protagonist lacks distinctive agency.

**Required corrective:** express each systemic conflict through immediate goals and choices rather than lectures about the world.

## 14.4 Climax-backward outlining

**Starting question:** What decisive action should the protagonist be able and willing to take at the book's end?

The author defines the climax, then works backward through prerequisites, tests, misinterpretations, costs, and relationship changes.

**Strengths:** strong preparation; clear causal spine; efficient foreshadowing.

**Risks:** middle sections feel predetermined; discoveries that would improve the premise are resisted; secondary arcs become support beams rather than lives.

**Required corrective:** preserve multiple viable routes and revise the climax when earlier material generates a better consequence.

## 14.5 Mystery-first outlining

**Starting question:** What false or incomplete model of advancement will be revised?

The progression process is also an investigation. The protagonist learns what the system is, who shaped it, or why its official explanations fail.

**Strengths:** epistemic progression; curiosity; reread value; reveals can transform goals rather than merely add lore.

**Risks:** arbitrary concealment; retroactive rule invention; protagonist ignorance that is implausible for the viewpoint.

**Required corrective:** maintain separate world-truth, character-belief, and reader-evidence matrices from the beginning.

## 14.6 Constraint-led discovery outlining

**Starting question:** What minimum structure is needed to draft productively without closing discovery?

The author fixes invariants, such as endpoint class, major costs, capability domains, causal dependencies, and book question, while leaving local scenes and some routes open. This is a layered form of “pantsing” rather than the absence of planning.

**Strengths:** preserves surprise and voice; allows character behavior discovered in prose to reshape structure.

**Risks:** hidden continuity debt; repeated local improvisation can invalidate distant payoffs.

**Required corrective:** update state, promise, and causal ledgers after each drafted sequence.

## 14.7 Rolling serial outlining

**Starting question:** What must be fixed far ahead, and what can remain responsive to audience, cadence, and discovery?

Use three horizons:

- **fixed horizon:** endpoint class, world invariants, irreversible thresholds;
- **committed horizon:** current arc and next arc, with causal dependencies;
- **flexible horizon:** local scenes, secondary routes, and optional complications.

**Strengths:** adaptability; manageable planning load; responsive pacing.

**Risks:** escalating promissory debt; accidental retcons; current engagement prioritized over eventual meaning.

**Required corrective:** schedule periodic closure and continuity audits, and refuse new mysteries that lack an approximate payoff class.

## 14.8 Ensemble or multi-protagonist outlining

**Starting question:** How do several agents' advancement paths interact?

Track a state vector for each focal character and a collective state for the group. Decide whether progression is complementary, competitive, unequal, or coordinated.

**Strengths:** diverse routes; social and collective agency; richer comparison.

**Risks:** one character monopolizes visible progress; synchronized breakthroughs feel artificial; balance is confused with equal page count.

**Required corrective:** give each protagonist a distinct causal contribution and a book-level question not reducible to supporting the strongest member.

## 14.9 No mode is universally superior

Mode selection depends on the project's uncertainty. If the author knows the system but not the person, character-first inquiry may reveal what matters. If the emotional premise is clear but the climax is vague, backward chaining may help. If a serial has already begun, rolling horizons may be unavoidable. The proper method is the one that resolves the highest-risk unknowns while preserving the framework's invariants.

# 15. Common Failure Modes and Their Causes

## 15.1 Nominal progression

**Symptom:** ranks, titles, or numbers increase, but available actions and later events remain substantially unchanged.

**Cause:** measurement has replaced function.

**Test:** remove the new label. If the plot and decisions remain intact, the progression is nominal.

**Repair:** specify the new affordance, changed reliability, altered access, or social consequence, and write a scene in which it matters.

## 15.2 Progression detached from plot

**Symptom:** the protagonist trains in one line of scenes and pursues the external conflict in another. The two meet only because the climax requires strength.

**Cause:** independent outlining of advancement and plot.

**Test:** remove the training arc. Does the external sequence still make causal sense until the climax?

**Repair:** make attempts generate clues, costs, rivals, obligations, and decisions that move the external problem.

## 15.3 Arbitrary gift or deus ex upgrade

**Symptom:** an unforeseen power resolves a major conflict without adequate preparation.

**Cause:** novelty is mistaken for surprise, or the author needs a late escape.

**Test:** could a careful reader retrospectively explain why this gain was possible and why it appeared now?

**Repair:** move the acquisition earlier, limit its first use, make understanding and retention a later problem, or assign the decisive burden to prepared capacities and choice.

## 15.4 Training without information

**Symptom:** many scenes show exertion but not changed method, diagnosis, or transfer.

**Cause:** effort is used as a proxy for earnedness.

**Test:** can the author state what the protagonist learns in each scene that was not known before?

**Repair:** introduce feedback, competing hypotheses, variable conditions, coaching limits, or opportunity costs. Compress repetition once its function is established.

## 15.5 Performance mistaken for mastery

**Symptom:** one success is treated as stable competence in every context.

**Cause:** no distinction among discovery, successful execution, reliability, and transfer.

**Test:** has the ability succeeded under altered conditions or interference?

**Repair:** plan delayed and varied tests. Let early performance overstate or understate actual learning when thematically useful [28].

## 15.6 Pure challenge inflation

**Symptom:** every new problem is the old problem with a larger number.

**Cause:** only one progression dimension and one opposition type are active.

**Test:** can the protagonist's current strength solve the problem if scale is ignored?

**Repair:** use asymmetric counters, institutions, deadlines, uncertainty, conflicting obligations, or collective coordination.

## 15.7 Static opposition

**Symptom:** opponents repeat failed tactics despite observing new capability.

**Cause:** enemies are treated as fixed tests rather than agents or systems.

**Test:** what did the opponent learn from the last encounter, and what can they afford to change?

**Repair:** use the opponent update rule and account for response delay, incomplete inference, and cost.

## 15.8 False scarcity

**Symptom:** a resource is rare until allies need it, or abundant until the protagonist needs difficulty.

**Cause:** no supply, control, substitute, or conservation model.

**Test:** trace every major unit or access grant from source to use.

**Repair:** establish flows, depletion, ownership, and why exceptions do not generalize.

## 15.9 The uniquely clever protagonist in a foolish world

**Symptom:** an obvious exploit transforms the system, yet generations of capable agents never considered it.

**Cause:** originality is asserted by degrading everyone else.

**Test:** what prevented discovery, testing, adoption, or diffusion?

**Repair:** require a rare conjunction of information, incentives, access, tolerance for risk, or complementary abilities. Show prior partial discoveries or institutional reasons for suppression.

## 15.10 Rule exceptions as plot permissions

**Symptom:** each crisis reveals a new exception favoring the protagonist.

**Cause:** the author confuses a mysterious system with an unbounded one.

**Test:** does every exception create a replacement constraint and apply beyond the current scene?

**Repair:** classify exceptions and govern their detection, rarity, transfer, and consequences.

## 15.11 Power erases earlier relationships

**Symptom:** old companions, places, and obligations become irrelevant as scale grows.

**Cause:** only combat contribution determines narrative value.

**Test:** what can an earlier relationship provide or demand that raw power cannot replace?

**Repair:** preserve distinctive knowledge, trust, legitimacy, history, vulnerability, or moral authority. Alternatively, dramatize the loss rather than ignoring it.

## 15.12 Social nonresponse

**Symptom:** extraordinary capability produces no legal, economic, political, or relational update.

**Cause:** worldbuilding is treated as scenery.

**Test:** who benefits, fears, regulates, imitates, recruits, depends on, or contests the gain?

**Repair:** write ripple effects proportional to visibility and scale.

## 15.13 Power solves the wrong class of problem

**Symptom:** a technical gain conveniently resolves grief, trust, legitimacy, or moral conflict without mediation.

**Cause:** all agency is collapsed into power.

**Test:** is the obstacle actually sensitive to the gained capability?

**Repair:** preserve domain boundaries. Let power create conditions for a conversation, sacrifice, law, or collective decision rather than substitute for it.

## 15.14 Repetitive progression loop

**Symptom:** identify next rank, train, duel, win, repeat.

**Cause:** stable topology, bottleneck, acquisition channel, and test type across arcs.

**Test:** compare the last three arcs by function rather than surface detail.

**Repair:** vary dimension, topology, route, opposition, cost, social context, and closure type.

## 15.15 Unfair climax

**Symptom:** victory depends on unknown information, an unbounded exception, opponent incompetence, or retroactive reinterpretation.

**Cause:** climax designed for surprise before causal preparation.

**Test:** backward-chain every decisive element.

**Repair:** shift novelty from capability to combination, choice, target, or consequence.

## 15.16 Serial hook replacing book closure

**Symptom:** the book stops at the point where its main question becomes urgent.

**Cause:** continuation is prioritized over satisfaction.

**Test:** can a reader state the book's resolved outcome without referring to the next volume?

**Repair:** resolve the local problem, then let its consequence open the next scale.

## 15.17 Endpoint collapse

**Symptom:** later tiers make the original premise trivial, but the series continues through larger enemies alone.

**Cause:** no non-numeric endpoint or scale transition was defined.

**Test:** what human, institutional, or thematic question survives maximum plausible capability?

**Repair:** reorient toward responsibility, collective structure, origin, identity, or a finite terminal decision.

## 15.18 Over-outlining

**Symptom:** the draft becomes mechanical, resistant to better discoveries, or burdened by unused documentation.

**Cause:** every detail is treated as equally stable.

**Test:** which outline elements are genuine dependencies, and which are speculative expression?

**Repair:** use layers. Fix invariants and causal obligations; leave local behavior, dialogue, imagery, and some routes provisional.

## 15.19 Under-outlining disguised as freedom

**Symptom:** each scene works locally, but gains, costs, and mysteries accumulate without coherent payoff.

**Cause:** discovery writing lacks state maintenance.

**Test:** can the current protagonist state, active promises, and next threshold be stated accurately?

**Repair:** update ledgers after sequences rather than attempting to predetermine every scene.

## 15.20 Cultural flattening

**Symptom:** concepts with distinct histories and meanings become interchangeable labels for energy, ranks, and attacks.

**Cause:** genre imitation substitutes for research.

**Test:** can the author distinguish the project's inventions from borrowed cultural or philosophical claims?

**Repair:** research specific traditions and sources; use invented terminology when the system is substantively invented; avoid claiming authenticity unsupported by the design [5-6].

# 16. Worked Example: *The Cartographer of Ash*

This example is invented for the framework. It demonstrates a non-numeric, partly noncombat progression structure. It is not offered as the one ideal form.

## 16.1 Premise and contract

In the caldera city of Veyra, airborne magical ash changes streets, poisons water, and erases stable routes. Licensed cartographers bind temporary safe paths by sensing pressure changes, inscribing relational maps, and coordinating civic beacons. Neris Vale, an unlicensed map-copyist from an outer district, can feel micro-instabilities that licensed instruments miss. The gift is useful but painful, unreliable, and legally suspect.

**Genre contract:** The central satisfaction comes from watching Neris become increasingly able to perceive, model, and stabilize dangerous ash routes through experimental cartography and collective coordination, while confronting a guild system that controls safety by restricting knowledge.

**Book question:** Can Neris become capable and legitimate enough to save the outer district from an approaching route collapse without accepting a guild contract that would conceal the cause and transfer control of the district's beacons?

**Series endpoint class:** Neris and her allies build an open, self-correcting cartographic network that can survive without a monopoly or a uniquely gifted hero, then decide how much instability any society has the right to impose on another region.

## 16.2 Baseline state

$$
S_0 = \langle C_0, K_0, R_0, P_0, B_0, L_0 \rangle
$$

**Capability $C_0$:** precise copying; visual memory for symbols; involuntary sensing of nearby ash shifts; no reliable method for separating true signals from pain.

**Knowledge $K_0$:** official map grammar; street-level route lore; false belief that guild beacons merely detect instability rather than redirect it.

**Resources $R_0$:** copying tools; illegal fragments of retired maps; little money; no access to calibration towers.

**Position $P_0$:** unlicensed laborer; family associated with a prior mapping scandal; subject to penalties for field inscription.

**Bonds $B_0$:** trust of neighborhood runners; strained relation with an older sibling who works for the guild; no trained field team.

**Liabilities $L_0$:** sensory overload; debt; legal exposure; tendency to conceal uncertainty; responsibility for an ill parent.

**Baseline demonstration:** Neris predicts a street shift that official markers miss, guides three children to safety, then collapses because she cannot distinguish a secondary current. The rescue establishes capacity, limit, cost, social value, and the official system's incompleteness.

## 16.3 Progression dimensions and topology

Primary dimensions:

- epistemic: sensing, hypothesis formation, map interpretation;
- technical: stable inscription and beacon coordination;
- institutional: apprentice access and contested legitimacy;
- relational: forming a field crew and repairing sibling trust;
- liability: managing pain, visibility, and contractual obligation.

Topology:

- mastery spiral for sensing and mapping;
- branching tree for private guild certification versus open neighborhood practice;
- institutional progression for access and authority;
- network progression for coordinated beacons and people.

The system has no universal level number. Comparability is provided by task classes:

1. perceive an instability after it begins;
2. predict it locally;
3. distinguish interacting currents;
4. inscribe a temporary safe route;
5. coordinate multiple beacons;
6. model how stabilization displaces risk elsewhere;
7. design a network that updates from distributed reports.

## 16.4 System ecology

**Operation:** A cartographer senses relational pressure, translates it into a map grammar, and synchronizes an inscription with physical beacons.

**Prerequisites:** spatial model, calibrated reference, stable hand, sufficient ash tolerance, and confirmation from a second observer for public routes.

**Resources:** treated vellum, conductive ink, tower access, trained runners, and time before current convergence.

**Failure modes:** false stability, displaced collapse, feedback pain, beacon desynchronization, and routes that are safe only for bodies of a certain mass.

**Institutional bottleneck:** the guild owns calibration towers and treats raw maps as dangerous instruments. Its monopoly has a rational basis, because bad maps kill, but also preserves political control.

**Hidden truth:** central beacons stabilize wealthy districts partly by redirecting turbulence toward outer districts. Most junior cartographers do not know this. Senior officials justify it as the lowest-casualty arrangement.

**Why Neris's gift is not an unrestricted exception:** she detects small instabilities without a tower, but cannot initially locate their source, quantify cumulative exposure, or bind routes. Her sensitivity also makes central calibration more painful.

## 16.5 Book contract

**Entry:** Neris can sense but not interpret or stabilize.

**Primary progression focus:** turn involuntary perception into a reliable, communicable method.

**Primary opposition:** institutional restriction plus an ecological crisis, with a sibling rival who has legitimate reasons to distrust unsupervised maps.

**Threshold:** Neris coordinates a distributed route using three imperfect neighborhood beacons and a corrected model of displacement.

**Irreversible cost:** she publishes evidence that the guild knowingly redirects danger, losing the provisional license she earned and exposing her crew to political retaliation.

**Climax test:** stabilize an evacuation route while the official grid is configured to preserve the central district, without causing an unmodeled collapse elsewhere.

**Closed question:** the neighborhood survives, and Neris proves her method can work collectively rather than as private intuition.

**Opened question:** who should govern an open map network, and can it scale without reproducing the guild's unequal risk distribution?

## 16.6 Arc braid

### Arc 1: Limitation exposed

Neris's rescue and collapse demonstrate the baseline. The guild accuses her of illegal field work. Her sibling, Tovan, prevents imprisonment by securing a temporary copyist examination. The external threat is a seasonal ash convergence predicted to strike somewhere outside the central ring.

**Progression pulse:** demonstration, cost, target.

**Character pressure:** Neris's habit of hiding uncertainty nearly harms the children.

**Institutional movement:** the guild classifies her sensitivity as either fraud or an unregistered instrument.

### Arc 2: Access and false success

Neris gains supervised archive access and learns calibration grammar. She quickly outperforms apprentices at detecting small errors, which appears to confirm exceptional talent. In a controlled trial, however, she follows pain intensity rather than current direction and maps a safe route that would fail under crowd load.

**Progression pulse:** preparation, apparent advancement, diagnostic failure.

**New knowledge:** signal strength is not location.

**Liability:** her early success makes officials and neighborhood supporters expect certainty she does not possess.

### Arc 3: Competing models

Neris works with a retired cartographer and a group of runners who know embodied route patterns. She combines formal grammar with timed reports from moving observers. Tovan argues that distributed observations are too inconsistent for public safety. Both positions have evidence.

**Choice:** pursue rapid individual certification or spend limited time building a team method.

**Opportunity cost:** team coordination may not mature before the convergence.

**Relationship progression:** Neris learns to state confidence levels; the runners gain authority as observers rather than messengers.

### Arc 4: Midpoint application and counterplay

The team successfully predicts and stabilizes a market shift. The public rescue earns Neris a provisional license. It also reveals the method to the guild and proves that unsanctioned beacons can interfere with the central grid. Officials confiscate the neighborhood beacons and require Neris to sign a secrecy contract.

**Progression:** local stabilization under moving conditions.

**Counterplay:** regulation and resource control, not a stronger cartographer.

**Second-order effect:** merchants begin paying for private route forecasts, creating pressure to serve those who can afford them.

### Arc 5: Model revision

Using archive discrepancies, Neris discovers that official stabilization displaces instability. Her family's “scandal” arose when her mother tried to report the same effect without adequate proof. Neris's gift is partly a result of childhood exposure to redirected ash, not a chosen blessing.

**Reveal function:** revises system truth, family identity, institutional opposition, and the meaning of progress.

**Ethical problem:** becoming licensed would give Neris influence but require silence.

### Arc 6: Setback with residue

Neris attempts to publish incomplete data. The guild discredits her by reproducing her earlier crowd-load error. Tovan withdraws support, the retired mentor is arrested, and Neris loses tower access. The convergence shifts toward the outer district.

**Losses:** position, resources, trust.

**Residue:** Neris now understands displacement, possesses a distributed reporting protocol, and recognizes that no single map can be treated as final.

### Arc 7: Synthesis and preparation

Neris rejects the plan to seize one central tower. Instead, she builds a temporary network from household chimneys, runners, bells, and copied calibration fragments. Tovan returns after discovering that the guild's proposed evacuation sacrifices a hospital route. He does not accept Neris's full political conclusion, but contributes technical knowledge.

**Strategic progression:** network coordination from existing capacities.

**Relational progression:** cooperation without forced ideological agreement.

**Cost:** every beacon operator becomes legally identifiable.

### Arc 8: Climax and consequence

The network detects that the official route would redirect a collapse into the hospital. Neris can preserve the neighborhood only by transferring part of the current toward an abandoned industrial basin, where trapped workers were omitted from official maps. She pauses the operation, publicly states uncertainty, and delegates recalculation to the runners. The team creates a narrower corridor that evacuates both groups but destroys the guild's central calibration archive.

The decisive action depends on prepared perception, formal grammar, distributed reports, relationship trust, and willingness to relinquish sole control. No unprepared power appears. The victory is partial: lives are saved, the archive is lost, the license is revoked, and the evidence becomes public.

### Arc 9: Integration

The neighborhood forms an open mapping cooperative. Neris is technically more capable but institutionally outlawed. Demand for maps increases faster than the cooperative can verify them. Tovan remains in the guild to argue for reform, preserving relational tension. The book closes its rescue and method questions while opening governance and scale.

## 16.7 Example scene card

**Scene ID:** B1-A3-S4

**Entry state:** Neris can detect small shifts and knows formal calibration notation, but mistakes signal intensity for source direction. She wants to prove she deserves provisional field access.

**Local goal:** map a supervised route through a training yard before two senior apprentices.

**Obstacle:** instructors introduce weighted carts after mapping begins, altering current behavior.

**Information state:** Neris knows the weights matter but does not know that official instruments average over moving mass. The reader has seen runners use cadence to predict street shifts.

**Meaningful choice:** trust the high-confidence instrument reading or interrupt the test to ask moving observers for timed positions, which would appear indecisive.

**Action:** she trusts the instrument and completes first.

**Immediate result:** her route passes the empty-yard check and wins praise.

**Feedback:** a weighted cart crosses and the route fractures. No one is injured because the trial is contained.

**State change:**

- $K$: learns that static calibration can conceal load sensitivity;
- $P$: gains attention but not trust;
- $B$: alienates a runner whose warning she dismissed;
- $L$: public expectation increases; confidence in her quick method becomes dangerous.

**Causal output:** motivates the distributed-observer method, supplies evidence used later to discredit her, and makes her climax decision to state uncertainty a demonstrated change rather than a speech.

**Promise served:** diagnostic feedback and non-numeric mastery.

## 16.8 Why the example qualifies as progression fantasy

The example does not rely on levels or combat dominance. It qualifies under the operational definition because:

- expanding cartographic agency is central;
- the process is visible through rules, attempts, feedback, and testing;
- earlier and later states are comparable by task class and reliability;
- gains alter what Neris and her community can do;
- institutions and opponents adapt;
- the climax depends on accumulated progression;
- social, ethical, and relational consequences follow from advancement.

The example also shows that progression can move from individual sensitivity toward distributed collective capacity. The protagonist's endpoint need not be maximal personal power.

# 17. Diagnostic and Validation Suite

## 17.1 Purpose and limitations

The following tests are design diagnostics, not validated measures of literary quality. They are intended to expose missing causal relations, unsupported assumptions, and under-specified consequences. A low score directs attention; it does not prove that a story will fail.

## 17.2 Core stress tests

### Test 1: Action-space delta

For each major gain, complete: “Before this event, the protagonist could not reliably ______. After it, the protagonist can ______ under ______ conditions, at the cost of ______.”

If the sentence cannot be completed, the gain may be nominal.

### Test 2: Counterfactual removal

Remove the gain from the outline. Identify every later scene that must change. If none changes, the gain is structurally inert.

### Test 3: Arbitrary-gift substitution

Replace the acquisition process with an unexplained gift. If the rest of the arc remains equally meaningful, attempts, feedback, and choices may not be doing causal work.

### Test 4: Transfer

Place the new capability in a changed environment or against a countermeasure. If the outline cannot predict its performance, reliability has not been specified.

### Test 5: Opportunity-liability pair

Name one new option and one new obligation, exposure, dependency, or counter. If there is no second-order effect, the world may be static.

### Test 6: Opponent update

After each public demonstration, ask what every informed agent believes and can afford to do. If no one changes plans, justify the nonresponse.

### Test 7: Social ripple

Trace the gain through at least one relationship, institution, and resource flow. Not every gain must affect all three immediately, but major gains should not remain socially invisible without reason.

### Test 8: Domain boundary

List which problems the gain cannot solve. If no boundary exists, future conflict will require arbitrary immunity or inflation.

### Test 9: Climax dependency

Trace the decisive act backward through all prerequisites. Any prerequisite introduced in the climax is suspect unless it bears only a minor role.

### Test 10: Closure

At each scale, state the answered question. If the answer is merely “continued in the next installment,” closure debt is accumulating.

### Test 11: Endpoint

Imagine the protagonist at the highest plausible state. What unresolved question still belongs to the premise? If the answer is only “find a stronger enemy,” the series spine is underdefined.

### Test 12: Assumption reversal

Select a central design assumption and reverse it. For example: the guild does not suppress talent; ranks are not linear; effort does not guarantee growth; the mentor is correct; the gift can be copied. Determine whether the alternative produces a stronger causal structure. This test resists attachment to inherited genre conventions.

## 17.3 A four-level audit rubric

Score each criterion from 0 to 3.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Absent or contradicted. |
| 1 | Asserted but not causally represented. |
| 2 | Represented with minor gaps or limited consequence. |
| 3 | Clearly represented, consequential, and integrated across later events. |

Audit criteria:

1. genre promise;
2. baseline legibility;
3. target legibility;
4. causal earnedness;
5. functional action-space change;
6. protagonist agency;
7. cost and opportunity cost;
8. transfer or reliability testing;
9. adaptive opposition;
10. social and institutional response;
11. multiaxial integration;
12. character and thematic pressure;
13. information fairness;
14. pacing variance;
15. book closure;
16. series scalability;
17. rule and resource continuity;
18. revision flexibility.

The total score should not be treated as a probability of success. A profile is more informative than a sum. A project with exceptional character integration and deliberately soft system legibility may need a different revision than a mechanically exact project with no social response.

## 17.4 Contradiction triage

Classify audit findings in this order:

1. **logical contradiction:** two author-side facts cannot both hold;
2. **causal gap:** an event lacks sufficient enabling conditions;
3. **knowledge error:** a character acts on information they do not possess, or ignores information without reason;
4. **resource error:** inputs, time, or access do not reconcile;
5. **promise gap:** a substantial section stops serving the declared experience;
6. **integration weakness:** a gain has limited consequences outside its immediate domain;
7. **pacing concern:** rhythm is repetitive or disproportionate;
8. **optional enhancement:** added depth that is not required for coherence.

Fixing in this order prevents cosmetic revision from concealing structural defects.

# 18. Propositions for Future Testing

The framework yields hypotheses that can be evaluated empirically.

## Proposition 1: Functional progression predicts perceived progression better than nominal increase

Readers should rate an advancement as more substantial when it changes later options, reliability, or consequences than when it changes an explicit rank without altering action. This could be tested with controlled narrative passages.

**Evidence status:** C, supported indirectly by narrative causality and game-mechanics concepts.

## Proposition 2: Causal visibility moderates satisfaction with unchosen gains

An inherited or accidental power should be judged more satisfying when its later mastery, use, retention, and costs depend on protagonist agency and established constraints.

**Evidence status:** C.

## Proposition 3: Multidimensional trade-offs reduce perceived repetitiveness

Arcs that vary which progression dimension rises or falls should be judged less repetitive than arcs with identical rank structure, even when the number of thresholds is equal.

**Evidence status:** C.

## Proposition 4: Adaptive counterplay increases perceived world coherence

Readers should judge a setting as more coherent when informed opponents and institutions respond to demonstrated capability, provided response follows plausible knowledge and cost.

**Evidence status:** B-C, derived from agency and system reasoning rather than direct genre experiments.

## Proposition 5: Nested closure predicts installment satisfaction independently of desire to continue

A book can increase desire for the next volume while still resolving its governing question. Satisfaction and continuation should not be treated as opposites.

**Evidence status:** B, grounded in serial narrative theory [7-9].

## Proposition 6: Progression-pulse variance improves perceived pacing

Sequences that vary attempt, feedback, cost, comparison, advancement, application, and counterplay should be perceived as better paced than sequences with equally frequent but functionally identical gains.

**Evidence status:** C.

## Proposition 7: Collective progression preserves earlier-scale relevance

When later capability depends on networks, institutions, teaching, or delegation, earlier relationships and locations should remain more relevant than in purely scalar individual progression.

**Evidence status:** C.

## Proposition 8: State-ledger maintenance reduces continuity defects in long serials

Writers using versioned capability, knowledge, resource, and promise ledgers should produce fewer detectable contradictions than writers relying on prose summaries alone, especially as series length increases.

**Evidence status:** C, consistent with the external-memory function of outlining and recursive writing models [30].

# 19. Discussion

## 19.1 What “proper” means

The analysis does not define proper outlining by conformity to a beat sheet. “Proper” means adequate to declared aims under inspectable constraints. An outline is proper when it can explain how the promised experience is generated and when its major events remain causally, systemically, and thematically compatible.

This definition permits radical variation. A literary progression fantasy may obscure exact rules while preserving functional consequence. A LitRPG may quantify every attribute. A cultivation saga may organize around metaphysical thresholds. A political progression narrative may make legitimacy and coalition its primary action space. The framework judges none by another's optional signals.

## 19.2 The main conceptual shift

The most important shift is from **power as possession** to **agency as situated possibility**. A protagonist does not act with capability alone. Action depends on knowledge, resources, permission, allies, liabilities, environment, and opposition. Consequently, progression can occur through greater precision, lower cost, better coordination, new legitimacy, deeper understanding, or transformed responsibility. It can also become less free even while more powerful.

This shift resolves several recurrent confusions:

- higher numbers may not equal greater narrative agency;
- a setback can coexist with progression;
- noncombat stories can be strongly progression-centered;
- social and institutional changes are not side effects but components of capability;
- later difficulty need not invalidate earlier mastery;
- the endpoint can concern use, governance, or identity rather than maximal force.

## 19.3 The role of causality

Narrative-cognition research gives the framework its strongest direct support. Readers construct models of events, goals, time, space, agents, and causes [10-12]. A progression outline that makes prerequisites, actions, feedback, and consequences inspectable aligns its distinctive promise with basic conditions of coherent event representation. This does not prove that one causal density is optimal, but it explains why disconnected training, forgotten abilities, and unprepared climaxes are structural rather than merely stylistic problems.

## 19.4 The role and limit of game-design analogy

Game studies helps articulate mechanics, rules, feedback, and interaction [18-21]. Progression fantasy often makes similar relations salient. Yet novels differ from games in decisive respects. Readers do not normally choose the protagonist's action, mechanical balance is not identical to dramatic fairness, and fictional rules may function metaphorically or ambiguously. The analogy is strongest when used to ask what actions are possible, what feedback exists, and how systems respond. It is weakest when it treats a novel as a passive game whose value can be optimized by reward schedules alone.

## 19.5 The role and limit of learning research

Research on deliberate practice, performance, and challenge offers useful distinctions [26-29]. It supports skepticism toward effortless universal mastery and toward treating one successful performance as durable competence. It does not require fictional training to reproduce real-world timescales, and it does not show that detailed practice scenes are inherently entertaining. Compression, magic, altered cognition, and speculative biology may change the world's learning constraints. The author must establish those constraints and then use them consistently.

## 19.6 The role of seriality

Progression fantasy is particularly compatible with serial form because thresholds create repeatable structures and prospective questions. Serial theory also clarifies the danger: repetition and continuation can become ends in themselves. The outline must design both momentum and satisfaction [7-9]. A series remains alive not because it refuses to answer questions, but because answers create new states from which different questions follow.

## 19.7 Outlining as external cognition

The outline's ledgers, maps, and graphs are not bureaucracy for its own sake. They externalize relations difficult to hold across hundreds of scenes: who knows what, which ability is reliable, which cost remains unpaid, what an institution observed, and which promise awaits resolution. Flower and Hayes's model emphasizes the recursive and goal-directed nature of writing [30]. The present framework extends that insight by treating the outline as a revisable external model, not a command issued once to the draft.

# 20. Limitations

## 20.1 Lack of direct genre-specific validation

The framework is not based on a representative sample of progression fantasy or controlled reader studies within the genre. Its genre-specific claims are principally analytic and inferential. Dedicated corpus research could determine how often particular progression topologies, closure structures, and failure patterns actually occur.

## 20.2 Cultural and linguistic limits

The article is written from an English-language framing and cannot stand in for scholarship on Chinese, Korean, Japanese, Russian, or other traditions that contribute to contemporary progression forms. Xianxia, wuxia, Korean web novels, Japanese light novels, role-playing traditions, and local publishing systems require distinct histories and languages. The cited work on Chinese internet literature provides an entry point, not comprehensive coverage [5-6].

## 20.3 Normative pluralism

The adequacy criteria presuppose that coherence, causal preparation, legibility, agency, and closure are relevant aims. Experimental works may reject some of them. The framework can still identify the departure, but it should not reduce intentional disorientation or anti-climax to incompetence.

## 20.4 No predictive commercial model

Nothing in this article predicts sales, platform performance, retention, or marketability. Commercial outcomes depend on cover, title, positioning, release cadence, prose, platform, price, advertising, existing audience, and many other factors not modeled here.

## 20.5 Outline quality is not prose quality

A coherent architecture can produce lifeless fiction. Voice, imagery, rhythm, dialogue, scene embodiment, ambiguity, humor, and emotional specificity remain essential. The framework is designed to make progression structurally supportable, not to replace literary execution.

# 21. Conclusion

Progression fantasy creates an unusually explicit obligation: the narrative must make change in agency central, intelligible, and consequential. Meeting that obligation requires more than ranks, training, victories, or larger enemies. It requires an outline capable of tracking a protagonist's state, the feasible actions that state supports, the world conditions that enable and resist those actions, and the meanings produced as capability changes.

The article's central thesis can now be stated in operational form. A progression fantasy outline is strongest when it:

1. declares the specific advancement experience being promised;
2. establishes a multidimensional baseline and a finite endpoint class;
3. defines operations, prerequisites, resources, bottlenecks, limits, and exceptions;
4. makes each major gain a persistent and legible change in feasible action;
5. connects gains to attempts, feedback, choice, cost, transfer, and consequence;
6. causes opponents, institutions, relationships, and resource systems to update;
7. braids progression with external plot, internal character change, and theme;
8. distributes varied progression pulses rather than repeating one reward loop;
9. prepares climaxes through causal dependencies while preserving present choice;
10. resolves questions at scene, arc, book, and series scales;
11. maintains auditable state and promise records;
12. remains revisable as drafting produces better information.

No one act structure, topology, system hardness, or planning style is mandatory. The invariants are functional. Progress must matter to what the protagonist can attempt, to what the world does in response, and to what the story ultimately asks advancement to mean.

# Evidence Status

## Directly supported conclusions

High confidence:

- Causal and goal-related relations are central to coherent narrative event representation [10-12].
- Serial narrative involves structured relations among repetition, continuation, world expansion, personnel, and design, and serial satisfaction requires more than indefinite deferral [7-9].
- Writing is recursive and goal-directed rather than a strictly linear plan-then-execute process [30].
- Learning performance in one moment is not identical to durable, transferable learning [28].
- Deliberate practice is important in many domains but is neither a complete nor universally sufficient explanation of performance differences [26-27].

## Cross-domain applications

Moderate confidence:

- Progression gains should be tested in altered conditions rather than certified by one success.
- Mechanics and rule-system concepts can clarify fictional affordances and counterplay [18-21].
- Competence, autonomy, and relatedness provide a useful lens for diagnosing progression narratives, although evidence from motivation and games does not directly establish reader response [22-23].
- Nested closure principles from serial theory apply fruitfully to book and series design.

## Original theoretical proposals

Provisional confidence:

- the protagonist-state vector and feasible-action-space model;
- the four conditions of persistence, legibility, causal integration, and functional consequence;
- the eight progression dimensions and nine topologies;
- the 14 adequacy criteria;
- the 28-step outlining protocol;
- the progression-pulse taxonomy;
- the opportunity-liability pair;
- the audit rubric and stress tests.

These proposals should be evaluated through comparative textual analysis, practitioner studies, reader experiments, and longitudinal study of serial production.

# Appendix B. Full Progression-System Worksheet

## B.1 Ontology and domain

1. What exists in the world that makes the system possible?
2. Is that account objectively true, culturally interpreted, or unknown?
3. Which phenomena can the system affect?
4. Which phenomena are explicitly outside its domain?
5. Are categories natural, institutional, conventional, or measurement artifacts?
6. Does the system act continuously, discretely, or through thresholds?
7. Can several explanatory models predict the same observations?

## B.2 Operations

1. What actions must the user perform?
2. Which steps require conscious choice, embodied skill, language, instrument, ritual, computation, emotion, or collective coordination?
3. Which operations can be interrupted?
4. Which operations leave evidence?
5. Which can be automated, delegated, or standardized?
6. What distinguishes novice execution from expert execution?
7. Which operations combine, interfere, or conflict?

## B.3 Acquisition

1. What can be inherited?
2. What can be learned?
3. What can be purchased, stolen, granted, contracted, or discovered?
4. Which prerequisites are cumulative?
5. Which are mutually exclusive?
6. What makes fast advancement possible?
7. Why is the fast route not universally used?
8. What aspects of a gain remain unusable without interpretation or practice?

## B.4 Costs and constraints

1. What is consumed immediately?
2. What degrades over time?
3. What opportunity is lost?
4. Who bears externalized costs?
5. What obligations follow from use?
6. What psychological, bodily, legal, social, ecological, or metaphysical liabilities accumulate?
7. What happens at the limit?
8. What does recovery require?
9. Can the cost be transferred to another person or group?

## B.5 Measurement and feedback

1. How do practitioners judge progress?
2. How reliable are the measures?
3. Who certifies them?
4. What incentives distort certification?
5. What false positives and false negatives occur?
6. What can a protagonist observe directly?
7. What requires an institution or instrument?
8. Can apparent progress be temporary performance?
9. What delayed evidence confirms mastery?

## B.6 Distribution and institutions

1. Who can access instruction and materials?
2. Who is excluded and by what mechanism?
3. What occupations and institutions depend on the system?
4. What laws regulate it?
5. What markets arise?
6. What forms of crime or resistance arise?
7. What ideologies legitimize the distribution?
8. Which institutions preserve safety, and which preserve privilege?
9. How would the world change if a bottleneck vanished?

## B.7 Conflict and counterplay

1. What counters each operation?
2. Can the counter be anticipated, detected, or exhausted?
3. What information must an opponent possess?
4. What does the counter cost?
5. What counter-counter becomes possible?
6. Which conflicts cannot be solved within the system's primary domain?
7. Which abilities are powerful in one environment and weak in another?

## B.8 Scale and endpoint

1. What changes quantitatively with advancement?
2. What changes qualitatively?
3. Which earlier constraints remain relevant?
4. Which earlier concerns become obsolete, and is that loss dramatized?
5. At what point does individual action require institutions or networks?
6. What is the highest useful state for the premise?
7. What question can end the pursuit?

# Appendix H. Outline Resolution by Project Stage

Outlining should increase in resolution as uncertainty decreases. Attempting full scene detail for distant books often creates false precision.

| Horizon | Appropriate resolution | Stable content | Flexible content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Series endpoint | Very low | terminal transformation, world invariants, core thematic conflict | exact scenes, many intermediate antagonists |
| Distant books | Low | book function, threshold class, major consequence | sequence order, supporting cast, local route |
| Next book | Medium | governing question, entry/exit state, arcs, climax prerequisites | scene expression, minor complications |
| Current arc | High | causal sequence, information states, costs, counterplay | dialogue, imagery, some tactical choices |
| Current sequence | Very high | scene goals, state deltas, transitions, continuity | sentence-level realization |

This layered approach reconciles planning with discovery. It also reduces the cost of revising a distant premise after a current scene reveals a better character truth.

# Appendix I. A Compact Formal Specification

The framework can be summarized as a set of constraints.

Let:

- $S_t$ be the protagonist state;
- $E_t$ be the environment and institutional context;
- $O_t$ be active opposition;
- $G_t$ be the current goal;
- $A_t = \Phi(S_t, E_t, O_t)$ be the feasible action set;
- $I_t$ be the information state distributed among world, characters, and reader;
- $D_t$ be outstanding promissory debt;
- $R_t$ be unresolved questions appropriate to each narrative scale.

A major progression transition $p_t: S_t \rightarrow S_{t+1}$ is structurally adequate when:

1. $p_t$ follows from an established causal path or is subsequently integrated through one;
2. the audience can infer a meaningful delta between $A_t$ and $A_{t+1}$;
3. the delta changes at least one later choice, obstacle, relationship, institution, or resource flow;
4. informed elements of $E_t$ and $O_t$ update when observation and capacity permit;
5. costs, liabilities, or opportunity structures are updated rather than silently reset;
6. the transition contributes to answering a question in $R_t$ or creates a new question caused by that answer;
7. any new debt added to $D_t$ has a plausible payoff class and horizon.

An outline is globally coherent when major transitions compose into a path from the declared baseline to the endpoint class without violating world invariants, knowledge constraints, resource continuity, or the independent goals of important agents.

This specification does not generate a story. It defines what the story's outline should be able to account for.
