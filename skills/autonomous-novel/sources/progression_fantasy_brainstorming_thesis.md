# Abstract

Brainstorming is often described as the informal production of many ideas before outlining begins. That description is too weak for progression fantasy. A progression fantasy concept must coordinate a protagonist, an initial limitation, a desired transformation, an advancement mechanism, a resource and institutional ecology, adaptive opposition, changing relationships, thematic costs, repeatable scene generators, and a credible terminal condition. A memorable power, setting image, reincarnation device, academy, dungeon, or rank ladder may be an effective seed, but none of these is yet a durable narrative engine. The principal design problem is not simply to invent more material. It is to search a large, coupled possibility space while preserving variation long enough to discover promising combinations and imposing enough constraint to test whether those combinations can generate a coherent novel or series.

This article develops an integrative framework for brainstorming progression fantasy. It synthesizes research on creativity, divergent and convergent thinking, fixation, incubation, analogical transfer, constraints, group ideation, idea selection, writing cognition, genre, narrative causality, serial form, game mechanics, and motivation. Because peer-reviewed research directly focused on the modern publishing category *progression fantasy* remains limited, the article distinguishes direct empirical support from cross-domain application, original synthesis, and practice-contingent guidance. It also conducts comparative structural analyses of published works, including *Unsouled*, *Mother of Learning: ARC 1*, *Sufficiently Advanced Magic*, *Into the Labyrinth*, *Iron Prince*, *The First Step*, *Street Cultivation*, *Beware of Chicken*, *Dungeon Crawler Carl*, *The Rage of Dragons*, *Super Powereds: Year 1*, *Bastion*, *The Wandering Inn*, and *Soulhome* [51-64]. These works are used as evidence of available design relations, not as formulas to copy.

The central thesis is that effective brainstorming for progression fantasy is a staged search-and-selection process whose proper output is a tested portfolio of candidate story engines, not a single attractive premise. A candidate is increasingly adequate when it can generate causally distinct scenes, make advancement intelligible and consequential, support opposition that adapts rather than merely scales, alter social and relational possibilities, sustain variation across serial units, and imply a meaningful form of closure. On this basis, the article introduces a formal idea vector, a distinction among surface, mechanism, relational, and value-level originality, a concept graph, generative-yield and closure tests, a 36-step protocol, eleven alternative entry modes, a hybrid individual and collaborative workflow, safeguards for AI-assisted brainstorming, twenty-eight failure modes, a sixteen-part diagnostic suite, and twelve practical appendices. The framework is pluralistic. It does not assume numerical levels, combat centrality, a powerless protagonist, a hard magic system, web serialization, monotonic growth, or any universal beat sheet.

**Keywords:** progression fantasy; brainstorming; creative ideation; premise development; narrative design; fixation; analogical transfer; serial fiction; fantasy worldbuilding; writing process

# Note on Literary Examples and Spoilers

Every concrete literary example in this article is drawn from an identifiable published novel, collected web serial, or continuing web serial. No invented novel is used to make a design claim appear more established than it is. The analysis paraphrases rather than reproduces extended passages. It concentrates mainly on first-volume premises, early structural relations, and publicly described series features, but it necessarily contains spoilers about setup, advancement mechanisms, institutional arrangements, and some later directions. The works are cited as primary literary sources in References [51-64].

The phrase *real literature* is used here descriptively rather than hierarchically. Independently published novels, web-originated novels, conventionally published novels, and continuing serials are all treated as literary works capable of structural analysis. Publication route is not a proxy for artistic or analytical importance.

# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 The hidden difficulty of the blank page

The apparent freedom of brainstorming is misleading. A writer beginning a progression fantasy can choose among an enormous number of protagonists, worlds, systems, ranks, resources, professions, conflicts, tones, relationships, and publication forms. Yet these choices are not independent. A progression mechanism that rewards solitary meditation produces different institutions, scenes, and social pressures from one that requires teams, public trials, inherited capital, commercial contracts, or construction inside the self. A protagonist motivated by rescue creates different stopping conditions from one motivated by recognition, revenge, curiosity, security, moral reform, or escape. A serial centered on an inn generates a different geography of recurrence from a serial centered on a tower, military academy, traveling sect, lethal dungeon, or repeating month.

The brainstorming problem is therefore combinatorial and relational. The writer is not selecting one premise from a shelf. The writer is constructing a configuration in which each decision changes the value and meaning of several others. An unusual magic noun cannot compensate for a mechanism that produces no difficult choices. A compelling social order cannot compensate for a protagonist whose progress never changes that order. A spectacular first trial cannot support a series if it has no repeatable variation. A limitless progression ladder can preserve expansion while destroying the prospect of completion.

Published works reveal how much structural work may be performed by one well-coupled relation. In *Mother of Learning: ARC 1*, the repeating time interval is not merely a premise-level novelty. It joins learning, investigation, social experimentation, failure, comparative measurement, secrecy, and mystery in one renewable engine [52]. In *Street Cultivation*, spiritual power is embedded in ordinary economic life, so advancement cannot be separated from wages, contracts, healthcare, class position, and institutional ownership [57]. In *Beware of Chicken*, the protagonist's refusal of the expected cultivation race does not remove progression. It redirects attention toward land, craft, community, reciprocal dependence, and forms of flourishing that question the genre's conventional value hierarchy [58]. These are not successful because they contain more isolated ideas. They are productive because one relation reorganizes many domains.

## 1.2 Research question

The principal question is:

> How should a writer brainstorm a progression fantasy novel or series so that the resulting concept is original enough to distinguish itself, structured enough to support causal development, generative enough to produce sustained narrative, and bounded enough to permit meaningful closure?

This question contains several subordinate problems:

1. What is the proper unit of brainstorming: a trope, a premise, a system, a scene, or a larger configuration?
2. How can a process preserve divergence without producing an unmanageable archive of disconnected fragments?
3. How can evaluation occur without suppressing unusual ideas too early?
4. What kinds of constraints improve search, and what kinds merely narrow it?
5. How can existing literature stimulate structural transfer without inducing imitation or fixation?
6. How should individual, collaborative, and AI-assisted methods be combined?
7. What tests can reveal whether an idea will continue generating story after its first display?

## 1.3 Central thesis

This article advances the following thesis:

> Proper brainstorming for progression fantasy is a staged, constraint-sensitive search-and-selection process over coupled narrative systems. Its appropriate output is not a polished logline or a single favored premise but a small portfolio of externally represented candidate story engines that have been diversified, recombined, simulated, compared, incubated, and stress-tested for progression centrality, causal density, emotional leverage, counterplay, scene yield, serial variation, differentiation, feasibility, and closure.

The thesis rejects two extremes. The first is romantic spontaneity: the belief that the best concept arrives whole and should be protected from analysis. The second is mechanical optimization: the belief that a scoring table can identify the objectively best novel before it is written. Creative ideation involves both generative and evaluative processes [1-2, 23]. Evaluation is necessary because originality without effectiveness is insufficient [1]. Yet evaluation is noisy, goal-dependent, and vulnerable to familiarity bias, premature certainty, and mismeasurement [16-18]. A disciplined process therefore delays some judgments, makes other judgments explicit, and preserves reversibility until evidence from development becomes available.

## 1.4 Why progression fantasy requires a specialized account

General brainstorming methods are useful but incomplete. Progression fantasy places unusual weight on the intelligibility and recurrence of change. A concept must answer not only who wants what and what blocks them, but also:

- what improvement means in this world;
- how improvement is acquired, detected, compared, and contested;
- why access is unequal;
- what advancing changes beyond victory probability;
- how opponents and institutions respond;
- which costs, dependencies, and identities accompany growth;
- how repeated advancement avoids becoming repeated narration;
- where the process can end without feeling arbitrarily stopped.

The opening configuration of *Unsouled* is instructive. Lindon's socially defined lack of standing, the visible existence of sacred advancement, his exclusion from normal participation, and the future destruction of his home form a coupled motivational structure [51]. The brainstorming lesson is not simply “begin with an underdog.” It is that baseline, status system, desire, future horizon, and departure pressure can be designed as one causal unit. *Iron Prince* also begins from bodily and institutional disadvantage, but its science-fictional combat devices, exceptional growth potential, academy selection, and competitive measurement produce a different engine [55]. Superficial similarity at the level of “weak protagonist becomes strong” conceals different relations among body, technology, institution, time, and social recognition.

## 1.5 Contributions

The article makes eight contributions.

First, it distinguishes brainstorming from adjacent activities such as research, freewriting, premise formulation, outlining, and worldbuilding. Second, it defines the proper object of progression-fantasy ideation as a coupled story engine. Third, it proposes a formal representation that exposes missing relations without claiming that fiction can be reduced to variables. Fourth, it integrates empirical findings about fixation, group productivity, incubation, constraints, and idea selection into a writing-specific workflow. Fifth, it supplies a method for extracting structural analogies from published literature while reducing surface imitation. Sixth, it provides comparative studies of fourteen real works. Seventh, it presents a 36-step protocol and alternative workflows. Eighth, it marks evidence status and states testable propositions rather than treating craft advice as settled law.

# 2. Scope, Method, and Evidence

## 2.1 Type of inquiry

This is an integrative conceptual study. It does not report a controlled experiment on progression-fantasy authors, a representative survey of readers, a sales analysis, or a computational corpus study. Its purpose is to construct a defensible framework by combining established findings from adjacent research with close structural analysis of published fiction. The article asks what follows from the evidence, what must remain provisional, and how abstract findings can be converted into inspectable creative practices.

The synthesis draws on six research domains:

1. creativity definitions, generative and evaluative cognition, divergent thinking, and incubation [1-9, 22-23];
2. individual and group brainstorming, production blocking, cognitive stimulation, collaborative fixation, and idea selection [10-21];
3. analogy, transfer, design fixation, constraint, morphological analysis, and problem-solution coevolution [20-30];
4. writing cognition and recursive planning [31-33];
5. genre, fantasy, worldbuilding, web literature, and serial narrative [34-41];
6. narrative comprehension, information gaps, mechanics, rules, and motivation [42-47].

The analysis also considers recent evidence concerning generative AI and creative work [48-50]. That evidence is relevant but limited. Studies of short creative tasks, design prompts, or assisted prewriting do not establish the best workflow for a long-form progression-fantasy series. The article therefore treats AI-specific conclusions cautiously.

## 2.2 Comparative literary method

The literary cases were selected purposively, not statistically. The set includes cultivation-derived fantasy, academy fiction, time-loop fantasy, LitRPG, gamelit, science-fictional progression, profession-centered progression, ensemble progression, and works that revise or resist conventional advancement values. Selection was guided by analytical contrast. The purpose is to show multiple ways in which published works couple progression to narrative generation.

Each case is examined through the same questions:

- What is the protagonist's initial constraint or asymmetry?
- What process makes change possible?
- What feedback makes change legible?
- Which institutions distribute or regulate opportunity?
- What repeatable scene families follow from the premise?
- How does opposition adapt?
- Which relationships are changed by progression?
- What source of variation prevents pure repetition?
- What kind of ending is implied or endangered?

This method cannot establish that the selected design caused a work's popularity or quality. It can establish that the relation exists in the text and can be separated analytically from surface expression.

## 2.3 Evidence hierarchy

Four evidence statuses are used.

**Status A: direct empirical or primary-source support.** The cited study directly supports a general claim about creativity, cognition, group ideation, narrative comprehension, or writing. A cited novel directly supports a claim about its own structure.

**Status B: cross-domain application.** An established finding is applied to progression-fantasy brainstorming, but the application has not itself been directly tested in that domain.

**Status C: original synthesis.** A model, distinction, score, protocol, or diagnostic is proposed here. It should be evaluated by conceptual clarity, usefulness, and later testing, not by an implied empirical authority it does not possess.

**Status D: practice-contingent guidance.** A recommendation depends on the writer's aims, medium, release model, resources, taste, and tolerance for discovery. It may be excellent under one condition and harmful under another.

## 2.4 Search limits and uncertainty

The modern English-language label *progression fantasy* has a rich practitioner and reader discourse but a comparatively small dedicated peer-reviewed literature. Adjacent scholarship on fantasy, seriality, online literature, creative cognition, and game-like systems is more developed. This asymmetry requires methodological restraint. The article does not claim to identify the essence of all works associated with the label. It proposes an operational account suitable for concept development.

Book publication metadata also varies across web-serial, self-published, audiobook, collected, and later trade editions. References identify a stable edition or the originating serial where practical. Structural claims concern the works, not a claim that one edition has unique textual authority.

## 2.5 Claims not made

The framework does not claim that:

- brainstorming quantity automatically produces quality;
- group brainstorming is always worse than solitary work;
- divergent and convergent thought are neurologically or temporally separable in a simple binary;
- originality can be measured without reference to an audience and comparison class;
- commercial success can be predicted from a concept rubric;
- a concept with a high score will yield strong prose;
- every writer should complete every worksheet;
- exposure to comparable literature is inherently harmful;
- AI use necessarily improves or degrades originality;
- planning should eliminate improvisation during drafting;
- progression must be numerical, combat-centered, or monotonic.

These exclusions are not hedges for their own sake. They prevent the framework from exceeding its evidence.

# 3. Definitions and Key Distinctions

## 3.1 Creativity

A widely used product-level definition treats creativity as requiring both originality and effectiveness [1]. Originality without relevance, coherence, or usefulness is not enough. Recent process-oriented work argues that creativity must also be understood through the processes that produce, develop, and evaluate outcomes [2]. For fiction, effectiveness cannot be reduced to utility in a narrow instrumental sense. It includes fitness for an artistic intention: the capacity to sustain attention, evoke feeling, generate meaningful conflict, embody a theme, satisfy or transform genre expectations, and remain writable within project constraints.

Originality is also relative. An idea can be new to the writer, uncommon within a subgenre, structurally unusual across a wider literary field, or unprecedented in a stronger historical sense. Brainstorming should not collapse these standards. A first-time writer may need personal discovery even when the device is familiar. A publication-oriented project additionally needs awareness of the relevant comparison set.

## 3.2 Ideation and brainstorming

**Ideation** is the broader process by which candidate ideas are generated, externalized, transformed, combined, elaborated, tested, and selected.

**Brainstorming** originally names a particular group method associated with deferred criticism and quantity-oriented idea production [10]. In contemporary practice the term is often used more broadly for individual or collaborative idea generation. This article uses the broader sense but retains the empirical distinction among interacting groups, nominal groups whose members generate separately, brainwriting, hybrid sequences, and AI-assisted interaction [11-18].

Brainstorming is therefore not a single technique. It is a family of search procedures. The relevant question is not whether brainstorming “works” in the abstract, but which procedure supports which phase, under which constraints, for which person or team.

## 3.3 Research

Research acquires or verifies information. Brainstorming transforms information into candidate possibilities. The activities overlap but are not identical. Reading about metallurgy may expand the available design space for a craft-based progression system. It does not decide whether metallurgy should be linked to status, bodily cost, ecological depletion, military monopoly, apprenticeship, or spiritual identity. Those are generative and evaluative decisions.

Research can also become avoidance. A writer may continue collecting mythologies, weapon systems, maps, or cultivation terminology because external information feels more defensible than commitment. A disciplined process assigns research questions to candidate concepts and stops gathering when additional information no longer changes a decision.

## 3.4 Freewriting

Freewriting produces language continuously with reduced local censorship. It can reveal images, emotional associations, voices, and tacit concerns. It is especially useful when the writer does not yet know what they value in the project. It is less reliable as the sole method for searching a multidimensional system because one continuous verbal path tends to elaborate its own assumptions. Freewriting should be treated as one generator, not as the whole search process.

## 3.5 Seed, fragment, and spark

A **seed** is any initial element with generative potential: an image, rule, character contradiction, social institution, sentence, relationship, scene, mood, or question. A tower that grants powers, a farm outside a sect, a month that repeats, a body unable to meet social expectations, an inn in an unfamiliar world, or magic treated as financial capital can all function as seeds in published works [52-59, 63].

A seed is not defective because it is incomplete. Its function is to begin search. The error is to mistake vividness for completeness.

## 3.6 Premise

A **premise** is a concise configuration of agent, situation, desire or pressure, and destabilizing condition. It usually answers some form of “who confronts what under which unusual condition?” A premise increases causal specificity beyond a seed. It still may not explain how the narrative renews itself.

The premise of *Dungeon Crawler Carl* joins an externally imposed apocalypse, a televised game-like dungeon, a human protagonist, and Princess Donut as companion and co-participant [59]. Yet the continuing engine also depends on floors, sponsors, audience response, system rewards, competing crawlers, showrunners, changing rules, and the gap between survival and resistance. The premise opens the door. The engine furnishes rooms beyond it.

## 3.7 Story engine

A **story engine** is a set of coupled relations that can repeatedly generate non-identical conflicts, choices, discoveries, and state changes. It specifies not only what is unusual but what keeps happening, why each recurrence differs, and how recurrence accumulates.

A progression-fantasy engine ordinarily contains:

- an agent with a baseline and desire;
- a gap between present and desired agency;
- a mechanism through which the gap can change;
- feedback by which change becomes legible;
- scarce resources, access rules, or trade-offs;
- opposition capable of response;
- social and relational consequences;
- repeatable but variable situation types;
- a direction toward transformation or closure.

*The Wandering Inn* demonstrates an engine that is not reducible to combat ascent. Erin's inn creates a recurrent social location, her class and skills provide legible occupational progression, and arriving guests connect local routines to expanding political, cultural, and species-level conflicts [63]. The hub produces recurrence; changing visitors and consequences produce variation.

## 3.8 Concept package

A **concept package** is the externalized, testable representation of a candidate engine. At minimum it should contain:

1. a one-sentence premise;
2. the protagonist's baseline, desire, and central contradiction;
3. the progression mechanism and its costs;
4. the world or institution that regulates opportunity;
5. at least three classes of opposition;
6. core relationship functions;
7. several repeatable scene families;
8. a first-book transformation;
9. a possible series-level terminal condition;
10. known uncertainties, research needs, and failure risks.

A concept package remains provisional. It exists so that intuition can be inspected rather than merely felt.

## 3.9 Development and outlining

**Concept development** deepens a candidate after initial generation. It resolves missing relations, simulates consequences, and compares alternatives.

**Outlining** selects and organizes events, states, revelations, and causal links across narrative time. Brainstorming asks what the project could be. Outlining asks how a chosen project will unfold. The boundary is permeable. Testing a candidate may require sketching scenes or mini-arcs, and outlining may expose a concept defect that reopens brainstorming. Nevertheless, separating the dominant question at each stage reduces accidental commitment.

## 3.10 Four levels of originality

The framework distinguishes four levels.

**Surface originality** concerns names, imagery, terminology, aesthetics, setting dress, or visible combinations. Replacing mana with another substance or moving an academy into space may create surface variation without changing the engine.

**Mechanism originality** concerns how progression operates: what actions transform state, how feedback works, what costs accumulate, and what failure means. The architectural construction of power in *Soulhome* creates design decisions different from a simple quantity-accumulation model [64].

**Relational originality** concerns how mechanism, world, character, institution, opposition, and relationships interact. *Street Cultivation* gains much of its distinctiveness from relating cultivation to capitalist distribution, employment, debt, healthcare, and corporate control [57].

**Value-level originality** concerns what the narrative treats as improvement and what it questions. *Beware of Chicken* is distinctive not only because a cultivator farms, but because withdrawal from status competition redefines flourishing, mastery, reciprocity, and strength [58].

These levels can coexist. The distinction is diagnostic, not a hierarchy in which surface invention is worthless. Strong imagery can matter greatly. The point is that unusual decoration should not be counted twice as structural novelty.

# 4. The Object of Brainstorming: From Spark to Story Engine

## 4.1 The wrong unit: the isolated trope

Trope lists are useful inventories of recognizable devices. They are weak final units of concept design. “Academy,” “system,” “reincarnation,” “underdog,” “tower,” “tournament,” “time loop,” and “cultivation” say little about the relation among agency, cost, institution, conflict, and closure.

Compare four academy-centered works. *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* links schooling to tower-derived attunements, technical experimentation, family history, and the search for a missing brother [53]. *Into the Labyrinth* links apparent magical failure to misrecognized aptitude, apprenticeship, friendship, and dangerous institutional testing [54]. *Iron Prince* links military education to measurable combat technology, bodily disadvantage, exceptional growth, ranking, and competitive visibility [55]. *Super Powereds: Year 1* links university life to formerly uncontrollable powers, secret training, ensemble difference, social concealment, and professional selection [61]. “Magic school” describes a common container. The engines differ because the relations differ.

## 4.2 The wrong unit: the unique power

A unique power is often proposed as the concept's center. That can work only when the power changes choices, constraints, relationships, and scene construction. A power that is unique in name but used like a familiar attack is a surface distinction. A familiar capacity can become structurally distinctive when embedded differently.

The brainstorming question should therefore shift from “What power has nobody seen?” to a sequence:

- What actions does the capacity permit?
- What does it not permit?
- What must be risked or consumed?
- Who recognizes, misunderstands, owns, teaches, prohibits, or counters it?
- What noncombat problems can it alter?
- Which old solutions become obsolete?
- Which new vulnerabilities appear?
- What does mastery require the protagonist to become?

The value of *Soulhome* as an example lies less in the abstract claim that its system is unusual than in the kinds of choices its construction model invites. If advancement involves designing and building an internal structure, materials, architecture, sequencing, stability, and long-term planning become narratively relevant [64]. The system generates design problems rather than merely labels.

## 4.3 The right unit: a coupled configuration

The proposed unit of brainstorming is the **coupled candidate engine**. “Coupled” means that changing one component alters several others. A time loop affects knowledge accumulation, risk tolerance, relationship continuity, secrecy, morality, pacing, mystery, and closure. A public ranking system affects motivation, humiliation, sponsorship, opponent selection, social comparison, and reader feedback. A progression system owned by corporations affects access, debt, legality, employment, bodily autonomy, and class conflict.

Coupling creates **causal density**. An element has high causal density when it participates in many meaningful relations without requiring separate explanatory machinery for each. The month-long loop in *Mother of Learning* has high causal density because it supports training, reconnaissance, experimentation, character re-evaluation, and the central mystery [52]. The coercive show in *Dungeon Crawler Carl* has high causal density because entertainment metrics, survival mechanics, public image, sponsorship, alliance, humiliation, and rebellion interact [59].

Causal density is not the same as complexity. A concept with many disconnected subsystems can be complex but low-density. A concept with one clear relation that changes many domains can be simple but high-density.

## 4.4 From noun generation to relation generation

Unstructured brainstorming often generates nouns: schools, beasts, paths, relics, classes, races, cities, ranks, and powers. Relation generation uses verbs and dependencies:

- advancement **requires** public service;
- rank **determines** legal personhood;
- healing **transfers** injury rather than removing it;
- cultivation **competes with** memory;
- mastery **depends on** another person's consent;
- a school **profits from** student failure;
- a resource **regenerates only when** territory is left unused;
- a companion **improves faster when** the protagonist relinquishes control.

These relation prompts are abstract, not fictional examples. They become useful only when tested against a candidate. Published literature shows why relations matter. In *The First Step*, entry into a cultivation sect changes not merely Wu Ying's training location but his labor, status, obligations, social comparison, and access to knowledge [56]. In *The Rage of Dragons*, Tau's pursuit of martial improvement is inseparable from revenge, caste, military structure, bodily extremity, and escalating moral cost [60].

## 4.5 Generative capacity

A candidate's **generative capacity** is its ability to yield multiple causally distinct developments while retaining identity. Repetition alone is not generation. Ten training scenes that differ only in opponent strength count as one scene family repeated ten times. Distinct families might include diagnosis, practice, resource acquisition, social negotiation, experimentation, public testing, rescue, teaching, construction, countermeasure design, institutional bargaining, and moral refusal.

A useful early question is:

> Can the candidate produce at least ten scenes that differ in immediate objective, obstacle type, relationship function, and state change, while all still expressing the central progression engine?

The requirement is diagnostic, not a law. A quiet novella may need fewer families. A twelve-book serial needs more.

## 4.6 Transformation capacity

An engine must also be able to transform itself. The beginning of a progression story cannot simply continue at a higher number forever. The protagonist's increasing agency should change the kinds of problems that can reach them, the institutions they can affect, the people who depend on them, and the meaning of earlier desires.

*Unsouled* begins with exclusion inside Sacred Valley, but the series' engine expands as Lindon's competence, relationships, knowledge, and scale of responsibility change [51]. *The Wandering Inn* preserves the inn as an identity-bearing hub while extending the social and geopolitical networks that pass through it [63]. These works illustrate two different mutation strategies: mobile scale expansion and stable-center network expansion.

## 4.7 Closure capacity

A brainstormed engine needs a credible way to stop. “Become stronger” is indefinitely extensible but terminally weak. Closure requires a condition under which the central transformation can be judged complete, rejected, redefined, or transferred.

Possible terminal forms include:

- goal completion, such as rescue, return, protection, or institutional reform;
- identity completion, where the protagonist becomes able to make a once-impossible kind of choice;
- mastery with limitation, where further power remains possible but no longer defines the story;
- succession, where competence is transmitted to others;
- systemic transformation, where access rules or institutions change;
- renunciation, where the protagonist refuses the ladder's governing value;
- tragic closure, where the pursued progression achieves its instrumental aim while destroying another value.

*Beware of Chicken* is useful because it exposes renunciation and redefinition as legitimate progression structures [58]. *Cradle* demonstrates the power of a long-range external horizon that can ultimately be answered rather than merely replaced by another rank [51]. Closure should be brainstormed early enough to constrain escalation, but not frozen so rigidly that discovery becomes impossible.

# 5. A Formal Model of the Progression-Fantasy Idea Space

## 5.1 Purpose and limits of formalization

Formal models can clarify relations that prose intuition leaves implicit. They can also create false precision. The model below is not an equation for artistic quality. It is a completeness and comparison device. Its variables name design questions; they do not eliminate interpretation.

Let a candidate progression-fantasy concept be represented as:

$$
I = \langle A, B, D, M, V, E, O, R, T, F, X \rangle
$$

where:

- **A** = agent configuration;
- **B** = baseline state and asymmetry;
- **D** = desired transformation or pressure;
- **M** = progression mechanism;
- **V** = visibility and feedback;
- **E** = ecology and institutional distribution;
- **O** = opposition and counterplay;
- **R** = relationship architecture;
- **T** = thematic and value tension;
- **F** = narrative form, scene generators, and serial structure;
- **X** = exit, closure, or terminal condition.

A candidate need not begin with **A**. A writer may begin with a system, setting, image, theme, or conflict. The vector records the relations that eventually require attention.

## 5.2 Agent configuration, A

The agent is not merely a demographic profile. It includes perspective, agency distribution, competence history, dependencies, interpretive habits, and the degree to which progress belongs to one person or a group.

Relevant alternatives include:

- single protagonist;
- protagonist and bonded companion;
- ensemble with independent trajectories;
- community or institution as the effective progressing unit;
- rotating point of view around a stable system;
- mentor-protege succession;
- protagonist whose capacity grows while autonomy decreases.

*Super Powereds: Year 1* shows why an ensemble cannot be brainstormed as one protagonist multiplied. The students' powers, control problems, secrets, social needs, and training relations create complementary and conflicting trajectories [61]. *Dungeon Crawler Carl* uses a paired center in which Carl and Donut possess different social and tactical affordances [59].

## 5.3 Baseline and asymmetry, B

Baseline specifies what the agent can currently do, what others expect, and where the mismatch lies. “Weak” is insufficiently precise. A protagonist can be weak in output but strong in learning, weak in status but rich in information, competent in a devalued domain, physically powerful but institutionally illegal, or skilled yet unable to control the relevant ability.

Published works demonstrate distinct asymmetries:

- Lindon lacks recognized standing in his local sacred-arts order [51].
- Zorian begins as a capable but limited student whose knowledge and social understanding can accumulate through repetition [52].
- Hugh appears to fail at ordinary magical practice before the basis of that failure is reinterpreted [54].
- Reidon begins with severe bodily disadvantage but receives extraordinary growth potential [55].
- the students in *Super Powereds* begin with powers whose former uncontrollability shaped their identities [61].

The brainstorming value lies in specifying the mismatch, not merely maximizing deprivation.

## 5.4 Desired transformation, D

Desire can be external, internal, relational, epistemic, or imposed. It may change over time. A robust candidate distinguishes:

- stated goal;
- underlying need;
- feared outcome;
- socially assigned goal;
- progression target;
- condition for “enough.”

Corin's advancement in *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* is tied to finding answers about his brother, not only to becoming a more capable attuned [53]. Wu Ying's entry into cultivation begins within material and social pressures different from a simple desire for supremacy [56]. Tau's training is driven by revenge, which gives improvement urgency while also narrowing value [60]. These motives produce different decisions even when all involve becoming more capable.

## 5.5 Progression mechanism, M

The mechanism defines how state changes. It can include practice, study, resource conversion, risk, bonding, craft, insight, social recognition, contracts, inheritance, quests, construction, teaching, or sacrifice. A complete mechanism specifies:

1. **input:** time, material, information, danger, relationship, status, or other resource;
2. **operation:** the action or process performed;
3. **transformation:** what changes in the agent or network;
4. **feedback:** how success or failure becomes knowable;
5. **retention:** whether gains persist, decay, transfer, or require maintenance;
6. **interaction:** how components combine;
7. **failure:** what goes wrong and whether failure itself yields information;
8. **counterplay:** how others interrupt, exploit, or redirect the process.

The time loop in *Mother of Learning* is not itself the whole progression mechanism. It changes retention conditions: most external states reset while Zorian's knowledge and certain forms of competence persist [52]. That asymmetry turns failed attempts into information. *Soulhome* places construction logic inside progression, making sequence and structural compatibility central [64].

## 5.6 Visibility and feedback, V

Progress must be legible without being exhaustively quantified. Feedback can come from ranks, tests, opponents, tool behavior, access privileges, new perceptions, economic value, social response, or changed problem-solving options.

*Iron Prince* uses explicit measurement and competitive comparison [55]. *Into the Labyrinth* uses instruction, spell performance, affinity discovery, and group trials [54]. *The Wandering Inn* combines class and skill notifications with consequences in hospitality, protection, reputation, and network reach [63]. The brainstorming task is to design several feedback channels so that one number does not carry every narrative function.

## 5.7 Ecology and institutional distribution, E

Progression does not occur in a vacuum. Ecology specifies where resources come from, who controls them, what labor sustains the system, how knowledge travels, what damage is externalized, and which institutions certify or suppress advancement.

*Street Cultivation* makes this dimension central by connecting qi to corporate and economic structures [57]. *The First Step* uses sect membership to organize access, labor, mentorship, hierarchy, and social mobility [56]. *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* links supernatural access to towers, attunements, education, family expectations, and military-social systems [53].

A brainstorming process that develops **M** without **E** commonly produces a world in which scarcity appears only when the plot needs delay.

## 5.8 Opposition and counterplay, O

Opposition includes antagonists but is broader. It may consist of:

- direct rivals;
- predators or hostile environments;
- institutions;
- resource bottlenecks;
- incompatible goals among allies;
- misinformation;
- legal or status barriers;
- internal compulsion;
- time limits;
- system-generated exploitation;
- consequences of previous gains.

Counterplay is the capacity of opposition to respond specifically to the protagonist's method. *Dungeon Crawler Carl* supports adaptive opposition through changing floors, show incentives, administrators, sponsors, other crawlers, and the system's interest in spectacle [59]. *Cradle* repeatedly changes not only opponent strength but the knowledge, resources, jurisdictions, and obligations around conflict [51].

## 5.9 Relationship architecture, R

Relationships should not be attached after the progression system is complete. Mentors control interpretation and access. Rivals provide comparison. Dependents create non-maximizing goals. Friends enable combination. Institutions can recognize a rank while intimate relationships resist the identity attached to it.

*Into the Labyrinth* derives much of its engine from the relation among apprenticeship, peer friendship, complementary magical differences, and group trial [54]. *Super Powereds: Year 1* makes control, secrecy, competition, and mutual understanding parts of one ensemble system [61]. *Beware of Chicken* redistributes progress among household, land, animals, and community [58].

## 5.10 Thematic and value tension, T

Theme is not a slogan placed on top of plot. It is a recurrent conflict among values made concrete through progression. Candidate tensions include:

- mastery versus autonomy;
- security versus freedom;
- excellence versus belonging;
- individual ascent versus collective flourishing;
- measurable rank versus unrecognized competence;
- revenge versus self-authorship;
- efficiency versus care;
- immortality versus continuity of identity;
- knowledge versus consent;
- strength versus dependence.

*The Rage of Dragons* makes revenge a powerful engine while exposing the narrowing and cost of a life organized around it [60]. *Beware of Chicken* contrasts competitive ascent with an alternative account of a good life [58]. *Street Cultivation* turns the value of power into a question about commodification and unequal access [57].

## 5.11 Form and scene generators, F

Form identifies the recurrent structures through which the engine appears. These include academy terms, dungeon floors, tower attempts, missions, crafting cycles, seasonal labor, tournaments, loops, expeditions, cases, performances, negotiations, or visitors to a hub.

A strong **F** combines recurrence with a variation rule. *Mother of Learning* repeats a time span while varying knowledge, objective, route, alliance, and interpretation [52]. *Dungeon Crawler Carl* repeats advancement through floors while varying environments, rules, public pressures, and strategic possibilities [59]. *The Wandering Inn* repeats hospitality and arrival while allowing the guest network to expand the world [63].

## 5.12 Exit and terminal condition, X

**X** records what would count as completion. It can remain plural early in brainstorming. A candidate may have several plausible exits:

- defeat or transcend a named threat;
- save or relinquish a home;
- solve the mechanism's central mystery;
- reform or escape an institution;
- reach a rank that enables a non-rank goal;
- establish a successor or community;
- reject the metric that initially defined progress.

An engine with no credible **X** is not necessarily unusable, especially for open serial publication. It carries a known closure risk that should not be mistaken for infinite creative abundance.

## 5.13 The concept graph

The vector checks coverage; a graph checks coupling. Represent each component as a node and each meaningful dependency as an edge. An edge should be stated as a verb phrase, not merely a thematic association. For example, in a structural reading of *Street Cultivation*:

- economic status **limits** access to qi-enhancing opportunities;
- qi capacity **affects** employability and safety;
- family responsibility **constrains** risk-taking;
- corporate ownership **converts** supernatural capacity into debt and control;
- combat opportunities **interact with** livelihood rather than replacing it [57].

A candidate with many nodes and few edges is a collection. A candidate with a small number of high-leverage edges may already be an engine.

## 5.14 Evaluation vector

Let preliminary concept quality be represented as a vector rather than a single score:

$$
Q(I) = \langle C, G, H, L, D_f, P, S, X_c, W \rangle
$$

where:

- **C** = coherence among components;
- **G** = generative yield;
- **H** = causal density or leverage;
- **L** = emotional leverage;
- **D_f** = differentiation from relevant comparators;
- **P** = counterplay and problem depth;
- **S** = serial elasticity;
- **X_c** = closure capacity;
- **W** = writability and project feasibility.

The vector prevents compensation errors. A candidate with extreme novelty but no emotional leverage should not be accepted merely because its average score is high. A low-cost, highly writable concept may be preferable for a short project even if another has greater theoretical range. Selection is multi-objective.

## 5.15 Generative yield

For practical purposes, generative yield can be estimated as:

$$
G(I) = N_s + \alpha N_m + \beta N_r
$$

where **N_s** is the number of causally distinct scene families, **N_m** the number of credible engine mutations, and **N_r** the number of meaningful relational changes. The weights are deliberately unspecified because project aims differ. The formula is a reminder that sheer scene count, mutation, and relationship consequence are separate dimensions.

## 5.16 Idea uncertainty

Every candidate also has an uncertainty set:

$$
U(I) = \{u_1, u_2, ... , u_n\}
$$

Each uncertainty should be classified as:

- factual: research can answer it;
- generative: several inventions remain possible;
- evaluative: the writer has not chosen a criterion;
- experiential: only drafting or reader response is likely to answer it;
- dependency: the answer changes when another component changes.

The purpose is not to eliminate uncertainty. It is to prevent unknowns from masquerading as solved design.

# 6. The Cognitive Architecture of Ideation

## 6.1 Creativity is not one mental operation

The ordinary word *brainstorming* can suggest a single state in which ideas simply flow. Research instead supports a family of interacting processes: retrieval from memory, association, recombination, abstraction, simulation, evaluation, inhibition, reframing, and selection [2-9, 23]. These processes can cooperate and interfere. A highly accessible example can stimulate a useful direction while also making nearby solutions disproportionately likely [19-21]. Evaluation can improve a fragment by detecting incompatibility, but the same evaluation can terminate an unfamiliar idea before its consequences are understood.

A practical framework should therefore avoid two crude doctrines:

1. “Never judge while generating.” This is useful as a temporary instruction, not a full model of cognition. Some implicit evaluation occurs whenever attention moves toward one association rather than another.
2. “Only keep good ideas.” This is circular when goodness has not yet been defined and dangerous when the value of an idea depends on later combination.

The better distinction is between **local viability filtering** and **comparative commitment**. Local filtering may reject an option that violates a fixed project constraint, such as a single-volume length or an explicitly nonviolent premise. Comparative commitment ranks candidates and directs resources toward one. The latter should generally occur after enough variation and externalization exist to make comparison meaningful.

## 6.2 Associative search and controlled persistence

Mednick's associative account connected creativity to the ability to bring remote elements into useful combination [4]. Later creative-cognition approaches emphasize the generation of preinventive structures and their exploration, interpretation, and transformation [5]. These accounts support two complementary search behaviors.

**Associative breadth** moves across categories. A writer brainstorming a progression mechanism might move from martial training to apprenticeship, finance, ecology, music, legal status, architecture, epidemiology, pilgrimage, translation, or caregiving. The purpose is not random oddity. It is to locate relationships that the default genre vocabulary does not immediately supply.

**Controlled persistence** remains with a problem long enough to develop depth. Instead of replacing the first answer, the writer asks what must be true for it to work, what follows at second and third order, and where it breaks. Breadth without persistence produces novelty fragments. Persistence without breadth produces elaborated convention.

The real works in the comparative set illustrate both. *Soulhome* can be understood as a remote structural connection between progression and architecture: improvement becomes an act of internal construction with materials and design consequences [64]. *Street Cultivation* persistently develops the relation between supernatural capacity and economic institutions rather than using corporate language as a passing aesthetic [57]. *Mother of Learning* explores the implications of retained knowledge across a resetting period through training, reconnaissance, social revision, and mystery [52].

## 6.3 Problem finding before problem solving

A brainstorm is partly determined by the question posed. “What is a cool power?” retrieves a different region of memory from “What form of improvement would make the protagonist more useful to the institution that exploits them?” or “What capacity becomes less morally usable as it becomes stronger?” The first question is not illegitimate, but it frames the search around an object rather than a conflictual relation.

Design research describes problem and solution as capable of coevolving: developing a tentative solution changes understanding of the problem, which in turn changes the solution space [24]. Fictional concept development behaves similarly. A writer may begin with an academy, discover that certification is the interesting source of control, then reframe the project from “students learn magic” to “students compete to become legally recognized persons.” The new problem suggests different powers, relationships, and endpoints.

This coevolution means that a brainstorming brief should not be treated as immutable unless it represents an actual project constraint. A target length may be fixed. The assumption that the protagonist must attend an academy may not be.

## 6.4 Retrieval structure and path dependence

Ideas are not sampled independently from an infinite space. One answer activates related answers. Once a writer selects “cultivation sect,” nearby concepts such as elders, manuals, cores, pills, rankings, tournaments, and hidden realms become easier to retrieve. This clustering is efficient because conventions carry bundled solutions. It also creates path dependence.

The solution is not to purge convention. Genres depend on recognizable expectations [34-36]. The solution is to control when a cluster is exploited and when search deliberately changes category. A practical session can alternate:

- **within-cluster depth:** generate ten consequences of sect ownership of training resources;
- **between-cluster transfer:** compare the sect with a hospital, guild, monastery, sports league, university, corporation, family, or licensing board;
- **constraint reversal:** ask what happens if the sect cannot own land, cannot retain members, cannot recognize power, or depends on public trust;
- **scale shift:** examine the same system at body, household, city, state, and ecosystem levels.

The purpose is structured movement, not novelty theater.

## 6.5 Generative and evaluative modes

Experimental and neurocognitive work distinguishes generative and evaluative contributions to creative work while also showing that creative cognition recruits interacting systems rather than a simple on-off switch [2, 23]. For practice, the useful implication is temporal emphasis:

- during a **generative pass**, the standard for recording an idea is low;
- during an **elaborative pass**, the question is what the idea could become under favorable interpretation;
- during an **evaluative pass**, explicit criteria and comparison are introduced;
- during a **revision pass**, weaknesses become prompts for transformation rather than automatic reasons for deletion.

A fragment should often be evaluated twice: first for *potential under development*, later for *performance after development*. This matters because some high-value concepts are initially harder to articulate. A familiar academy-plus-ranking idea may arrive as a polished package because the culture already supplies its defaults. A relationally unusual idea may arrive as a clumsy question.

## 6.6 Externalization and cognitive load

Long-form fiction exceeds the capacity of unaided working memory. Writing research treats planning, translating, and reviewing as recursive rather than strictly linear processes [31-33]. Brainstorming likewise improves when ideas are moved into an external medium where they can be compared, grouped, contradicted, and recombined.

Externalization should preserve provenance and uncertainty. A practical idea record includes:

- the idea itself;
- the prompt or source that produced it;
- the component of the idea vector it addresses;
- assumptions it relies on;
- related candidates;
- objections;
- possible combinations;
- status: fragment, active candidate, parked, rejected, or selected.

This record prevents a rejected idea from disappearing without explanation and reduces repeated debate. It also reveals when one favored candidate has received ten times more elaboration than its competitors, making later comparison unfair.

## 6.7 Incubation

A meta-analysis found an overall positive incubation effect in problem solving, with important moderators including task type, preparation, and the cognitive demands of the intervening activity [22]. The evidence does not justify mystical claims that leaving every problem alone will produce an answer. It does justify treating interruption as a potential component of method rather than as failure.

For progression-fantasy brainstorming, incubation is most useful after the problem has been represented and several unsuccessful or partial routes have been attempted. A writer can record the live question precisely, shift to a lower-demand or unrelated activity, and return for a new generative pass before reading the old answers. The last instruction matters. Immediate rereading may restore the same fixation.

## 6.8 Expertise, convention, and flexibility

Genre knowledge supplies useful chunks. A writer familiar with cultivation fiction does not have to reinvent every way ranks, sects, resources, inheritances, and breakthroughs can function. Expertise increases available patterns and permits more accurate simulation. It can also make dominant patterns more accessible and alternatives less visible.

The appropriate response is not ignorance. *Beware of Chicken* depends for much of its effect on an intelligible relation to cultivation conventions [58]. *Street Cultivation* also transforms a known structure by relocating its resource and status logic into a modern economic order [57]. In both cases, departure is legible because the source pattern remains recognizable.

The method should therefore alternate **genre fluency** and **functional abstraction**. The writer identifies what a convention does, then asks what else could perform that function. A tournament can compare competence, publicize rivals, allocate resources, force preparation, and reveal institutional values. Other structures could perform some or all of those functions: licensing examinations, public works, rescue rotations, artistic performances, trials, elections, expeditions, markets, or communal crises. The aim is not to replace every tournament. It is to choose one rather than inherit it unconsciously.

## 6.9 Emotion and personal salience

A concept is not merely a system to optimize. Writers sustain long projects partly through attachment, curiosity, anger, pleasure, fear, humor, aesthetic fascination, and unresolved questions. These affective signals are not infallible evidence of quality, but excluding them from evaluation produces sterile selection.

The candidate card should therefore record:

- which part the writer most wants to witness;
- which question feels personally unresolved;
- which relationship carries emotional charge;
- which scene the writer would draft even if the project were never sold;
- which part feels prestigious but emotionally inert;
- which element the writer is protecting from criticism and why.

The last question can reveal either a genuine artistic center or an attachment that prevents repair.

# 7. Divergence, Convergence, and Candidate Portfolios

## 7.1 The limits of “more ideas”

Quantity can increase the opportunity for variation, but raw count is not the same as search quality. One hundred names for rank levels do not equal one hundred candidate engines. A useful divergence phase varies categories, assumptions, causal relations, and value structures.

Three measures should be separated:

- **fluency:** number of ideas recorded;
- **flexibility:** number of distinct conceptual categories or approaches;
- **depth:** number of developed consequences within an approach.

A session that produces high fluency but low flexibility may feel productive while circling one cluster. A session that produces high flexibility but no depth may never discover whether any option works.

## 7.2 Divergence as controlled variation

Divergence is best understood as the deliberate production of alternatives under a temporarily permissive recording rule. The writer varies one or more dimensions while holding others stable. Examples from published literature show why dimensions matter.

Hold the academy constant and vary the advancement relation:

- tower-granted attunement and technical study in *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* [53];
- reinterpreted aptitude, apprenticeship, and team complementarity in *Into the Labyrinth* [54];
- measurable combat technology and exceptional growth in *Iron Prince* [55];
- power control, secrecy, and ensemble professionalization in *Super Powereds: Year 1* [61].

Hold visible progression constant and vary the social ecology:

- sacred-arts hierarchy in *Unsouled* [51];
- corporate commodification in *Street Cultivation* [57];
- coercive entertainment market in *Dungeon Crawler Carl* [59];
- cultivation sect and agrarian origin in *The First Step* [56].

This method produces analytically meaningful alternatives rather than cosmetic changes.

## 7.3 Convergence as staged commitment

Convergence includes grouping, comparison, combination, rejection, and selection. It should occur in stages.

**Stage 1: admissibility.** Remove candidates that violate fixed constraints or ethical commitments.

**Stage 2: clarification.** Rewrite each surviving candidate in the same format. Ambiguity should not be mistaken for depth.

**Stage 3: charitable development.** Give each candidate a fair chance by adding missing components and strongest plausible relations.

**Stage 4: comparative evaluation.** Apply explicit criteria, independent judgments if a team is involved, and veto conditions.

**Stage 5: simulation.** Produce representative scenes, consequences, or mini-arcs.

**Stage 6: commitment with review point.** Select a lead concept and define evidence that would reopen the choice.

This sequence reduces two errors: rejecting an underdeveloped unusual idea in favor of a fully elaborated familiar one, and endlessly preserving every possibility so that no project begins.

## 7.4 Why a portfolio is superior to one favorite

Idea-selection research shows that generating and selecting are distinct problems, and people do not always choose their most creative ideas [16-18]. Hybrid structures in which people generate individually before interacting can improve the quality of the best identified ideas under some conditions [17]. For a solitary writer, the analogous principle is to preserve several developed candidates rather than polishing the first favorite until it becomes incomparable.

A practical portfolio contains:

- one **lead candidate**;
- one **structural alternative** that solves the same artistic problem differently;
- one **wild candidate** with high distinctiveness and unresolved feasibility;
- one **fallback candidate** with high writability and lower structural risk;
- a **component bank** of strong elements not currently assigned.

The portfolio is not an obligation to write five books. It protects optionality during early development.

## 7.5 Candidate equality before comparison

Comparison is invalid when candidates differ mainly in development time. The writer should normalize presentation. Each candidate receives the same word allowance and answers the same core questions. The goal is not bureaucratic symmetry. It is to prevent familiarity and elaboration from masquerading as quality.

A one-page concept package should therefore be completed for at least three candidates before final selection. If one cannot answer a category, the blank is data. It may reveal a weakness, or it may reveal that the category is irrelevant to that particular artistic form. The writer should state which.

## 7.6 Combination without accumulation

Combining ideas can produce leverage, but it can also produce a premise that contains every attractive fragment and no hierarchy. Combination should be governed by a relation test:

> Does element B change how element A works, or does B merely sit beside A?

In *Dungeon Crawler Carl*, the reality-show structure changes the dungeon because visibility, audience approval, sponsorship, and producer incentives affect survival [59]. The elements are coupled. Merely placing cameras in a dungeon without consequences would be accumulation.

A combination that does not create a new dependency, conflict, affordance, or interpretation should remain optional until it earns its cost.

## 7.7 Negative selection and kill criteria

Writers often preserve weak candidates because abandonment feels like wasted effort. A portfolio benefits from explicit kill criteria. A candidate may be retired when:

- its primary appeal disappears after the first reveal;
- its engine yields only one scene family;
- its advancement mechanism has no stable limits or costs;
- its central conflict could be removed without changing progression;
- its differentiation depends mainly on terminology;
- its serial expansion requires permanent postponement of the stated goal;
- the writer feels obligation but no curiosity after charitable development;
- it cannot fit non-negotiable project resources.

Retirement is not deletion. Components can return in another configuration.

# 8. Fixation, Anchoring, and Premature Commitment

## 8.1 Fixation as narrowed search

Fixation occurs when prior knowledge, examples, or an early solution disproportionately constrain later generation. Experiments show that examples can shape creative outputs even when participants are told to avoid copying specific features [20], and design research has documented persistence of example features despite their defects [21]. Other people's ideas can also produce collaborative fixation [19].

Fixation is not identical to influence. All writing occurs within languages, genres, traditions, and prior reading. The practical concern is reduced flexibility combined with low awareness of the reduction.

## 8.2 First-idea attachment

The first idea has advantages unrelated to quality. It frames later questions, receives more elaboration, accumulates emotional ownership, and becomes the reference point against which alternatives are judged. The writer may say that later ideas “do not fit,” when fit means conformity to assumptions introduced by the first.

Countermeasures include:

- do not name the project after the first mechanism;
- generate at least three baselines, three desires, and three mechanisms independently;
- write a strongest-case version of an incompatible candidate;
- delay the logline until several concept graphs exist;
- conduct one “anti-premise” pass that reverses the favored value or causal direction.

The goal is not to distrust the first idea automatically. It is to remove its procedural privilege.

## 8.3 Exemplar fixation

Published progression fantasy is especially likely to induce exemplar fixation because its systems are easy to summarize as reusable packages. Reading *Mother of Learning* may make “time loop plus academy” highly accessible; reading *Cradle* may activate sacred-arts ranks, sects, and an excluded underdog; reading *Dungeon Crawler Carl* may activate lethal floors, system messages, and an audience [51-52, 59].

A responsible literature exercise uses three columns:

1. **surface feature:** the visible device;
2. **structural function:** what the device accomplishes;
3. **alternative implementation:** a different device capable of performing that function.

For the loop in *Mother of Learning*, structural functions include repeatable comparison, retained learning, low-permanence experimentation, hidden-cause investigation, and changing interpretations of the same people [52]. A new work need not use a loop to obtain those functions. It might use simulations, reincarnated historical roles, legal retrials, staged replicas, inherited memories, repeated expeditions, or a city that periodically reconstructs itself. These are search prompts, not story examples.

## 8.4 Trope bundle fixation

Tropes often arrive in bundles. Selecting “academy” may silently import age cohort, examinations, houses, rival students, professors, tournament, graduation, and hidden forbidden wing. Selecting “cultivation” may import cores, pills, realms, sect elders, inheritance caves, and heavenly tribulation. Bundles are efficient, but inherited elements should be classified:

- necessary to the intended genre signal;
- functionally useful;
- deliberately transformed;
- optional convention;
- contradictory to the project;
- present only because it arrived with the label.

*Into the Labyrinth* uses recognizable school structures but makes peer complementarity, misdiagnosed ability, and apprenticeship central [54]. *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* uses academy and tower conventions but links them to technical enchantment, family pressure, and a missing-person investigation [53]. Their distinctiveness comes partly from which parts of the bundle are emphasized and recombined.

## 8.5 Prestige fixation

A writer may fixate on ideas that signal complexity, seriousness, originality, or scale. The concept then becomes optimized for being described rather than experienced. Prestige fixation appears when the writer can explain the metaphysics for twenty minutes but cannot name a scene they urgently want to write.

The corrective is an experience test:

- What does the protagonist do on page?
- What choice becomes difficult?
- What changes between two people?
- What can the reader anticipate?
- What pleasure or disturbance recurs?

A concept is not shallow because it is legible. Legibility permits complexity to become experience.

## 8.6 System fixation

Progression-fantasy writers may elaborate ranks, abilities, resources, and combinations before deciding what human problem those systems intensify. System fixation produces technically coherent advancement with low narrative necessity.

The corrective is to express every major system component as a pressure on desire, identity, relationship, or institution. *Street Cultivation* makes capacity inseparable from livelihood and family responsibility [57]. *The Rage of Dragons* makes training inseparable from revenge and hierarchy [60]. *Soulhome* links construction expertise to rebuilding after betrayal and to a larger mystery [64]. The system earns centrality by carrying non-systemic stakes.

## 8.7 Lore fixation

Worldbuilding fragments can multiply because each answer creates more factual questions. Lore fixation differs from productive ecology design. Productive ecology changes options and consequences. Lore fixation accumulates facts with no decision-bearing role.

A lore item should be tagged with at least one function:

- enables or blocks action;
- changes interpretation;
- establishes cost or scarcity;
- produces conflict;
- differentiates a group;
- pays a promise;
- supports atmosphere essential to the project's aim.

Untagged lore is not forbidden. It is lower priority.

## 8.8 Metric fixation

Visible ranks and numbers can dominate because they are easy to compare. The brainstorm then treats every problem as insufficient magnitude. *Iron Prince* shows that explicit metrics can be dramatically useful when joined to bodily history, institutional competition, relationships, and extraordinary growth potential [55]. The lesson is not to avoid metrics but to prevent them from becoming the only feedback channel.

A metric audit asks what cannot be measured, who controls measurement, how metrics can be gamed, and which forms of excellence remain invisible. This creates political, relational, and epistemic story possibilities.

## 8.9 Market fixation

Market awareness can clarify genre promise and project scope. It becomes fixation when every choice is filtered through a guessed average reader before the project has an identity. The writer then reproduces the most visible recent packages because visibility is mistaken for demand.

A better market pass occurs after an initial personal and structural pass. It asks:

- which expectations the concept clearly satisfies;
- which it modifies;
- which audience must be educated;
- which comparison titles share readership rather than merely surface traits;
- whether differentiation is communicable in one or two relations.

Market fit is one criterion, not the generative source of all criteria.

## 8.10 AI anchoring

Generative AI can rapidly supply polished options. That fluency may anchor the user to statistically accessible combinations. Recent studies have found benefits to individual creative output in some tasks alongside risks of reduced collective diversity, and design research has reported fixation effects under some generative-AI conditions [48-49]. These findings do not prove that every AI-assisted novel becomes homogeneous. They justify procedural safeguards.

Safeguards include:

- generate a human candidate set before requesting model suggestions;
- ask the model for distant categories, objections, and missing relations rather than a finished premise;
- use multiple prompt framings;
- conceal earlier outputs during a fresh human pass;
- compare structural feature overlap across candidates;
- retain provenance;
- never use model confidence as evidence of novelty or factual correctness;
- require the writer to articulate why a selected relation matters personally.

## 8.11 The fixation audit

Before selecting a concept, the writer should answer:

1. Which work or recent trend is most accessible in my mind?
2. Which features have I imported together without independent justification?
3. Which candidate received the most development merely because it came first?
4. Which terminology makes a familiar mechanism sound new?
5. Which alternative did I dismiss before simulating?
6. Which project constraint is real, and which is inherited from an exemplar?
7. What would this concept become if its most recognizable trope were removed?
8. Which feature would remain distinctive in a plain-language summary?

The audit cannot guarantee originality. It makes path dependence visible.

# 9. Constraints, Questions, Research, and Productive Search

## 9.1 The false opposition between freedom and constraint

Constraints can inhibit creativity, direct effort, reduce overload, sharpen contrast, or stimulate recombination depending on their type, source, timing, and intensity [27-28]. “No constraints” is not neutral. Language, genre knowledge, available time, market, memory, and personal values already constrain search. The practical aim is to distinguish **enabling constraints** from **premature foreclosure**.

An enabling constraint narrows a dimension while opening consequences. “The protagonist cannot accumulate power alone” immediately raises questions about trust, coordination, ownership, betrayal, consent, and group composition. A foreclosing constraint narrows without generating. “It must be exactly like the most successful academy series” suppresses difference while leaving the difficult relational work unsolved.

## 9.2 Constraint taxonomy

Constraints should be recorded by type.

**Material constraints:** length, time, budget, research access, illustration needs, production cadence.

**Formal constraints:** single viewpoint, epistolary form, fixed setting, episodic structure, no explicit statistics, predetermined number of books.

**Genre constraints:** central advancement, process visibility, comparative legibility, consequential gains.

**World constraints:** metaphysical laws, geography, scarcity, technology, institutional jurisdiction.

**Character constraints:** disability, oath, dependence, ignorance, identity, values, trauma, obligation.

**Ethical constraints:** representations or outcomes the writer refuses to instrumentalize.

**Aesthetic constraints:** tone, prose density, degree of violence, scale, humor, wonder, horror.

**Market constraints:** target readership, format, content expectations, discoverability.

Each should be marked as fixed, preferred, provisional, inherited, or unknown.

## 9.3 Constraint timing

Early search benefits from a small number of high-value constraints. Too many specific constraints turn brainstorming into filling blanks. Too few create diffuse possibility.

A useful sequence is:

1. fix project realities and ethical boundaries;
2. select two or three generative constraints;
3. diverge broadly;
4. add genre-function constraints;
5. simulate;
6. add production and market constraints before final selection.

For a long series, terminal and scalability constraints should enter earlier than they would for a short standalone.

## 9.4 Question quality

A question is productive when it changes the search distribution. Questions can operate at several levels.

**Descriptive:** What is the power source?

**Operational:** What action converts the source into capability?

**Causal:** Why does that action change capability?

**Distributional:** Who controls the source and who performs the labor?

**Adversarial:** How can the process be interrupted or exploited?

**Relational:** Whose cooperation or consent does advancement require?

**Normative:** What does the system reward that the protagonist should not become?

**Serial:** How does the engine produce a different problem after mastery of the first?

**Terminal:** What outcome makes further advancement unnecessary to the story?

The later questions tend to create coupling.

## 9.5 “How might” and “what must be true”

Two complementary prompt forms are useful.

**How might** prompts expand options: “How might progress remain visible without ranks?” “How might an opponent become more dangerous as the protagonist becomes more knowledgeable?”

**What must be true** prompts test dependencies: “What must be true for this academy to monopolize certification?” “What must be true for repeated failure to remain emotionally consequential in a time loop?”

The first prevents premature closure. The second exposes hidden assumptions.

## 9.6 Research as targeted uncertainty reduction

Research should be attached to decisions. For each candidate, record:

- claim or mechanism requiring verification;
- consequence of being wrong;
- source type needed;
- stopping condition;
- whether factual accuracy or plausible inspiration is the goal.

A progression system inspired by medicine, finance, martial arts, architecture, law, agriculture, or disability may require domain research. The research should not be treated as a license to reproduce stereotypes or extract isolated terminology. It should improve causal plausibility and reveal constraints the writer would not invent alone.

## 9.7 Morphological analysis

Morphological analysis systematically varies dimensions and examines combinations [29]. For progression fantasy, columns might include:

- baseline asymmetry;
- desired transformation;
- acquisition operation;
- feedback mode;
- cost;
- resource ownership;
- institutional form;
- opposition type;
- relationship dependency;
- scene engine;
- terminal condition.

The writer generates several options per column, then combines across non-adjacent rows. The method is powerful because it makes inherited bundles separable. It is limited because not all combinations are meaningful. Selection requires causal integration.

## 9.8 Contradiction prompts

Many productive concepts arise from tensions rather than additions. Contradiction prompts include:

- advancement that increases dependence;
- healing that undermines identity;
- public recognition that reduces freedom;
- a system that rewards behavior the protagonist morally rejects;
- a mentor who becomes less useful as the student improves;
- a resource that is abundant but legally inaccessible;
- a rank that signifies competence to institutions but danger to loved ones;
- a protagonist who progresses by refusing the culturally dominant metric.

Published literature demonstrates the value of contradiction. *Beware of Chicken* turns withdrawal from the expected competitive path into another form of advancement [58]. *Street Cultivation* joins supernatural self-improvement to structural economic constraint [57]. *Dungeon Crawler Carl* makes success inside the game simultaneously useful for survival and useful to the system that exploits the contestants [59].

## 9.9 Scale prompts

Every candidate should be examined at five scales:

1. body and cognition;
2. intimate relationships;
3. household or team;
4. institution and political economy;
5. ecology, history, or cosmology.

Not every scale needs equal narrative weight. The pass identifies second-order consequences. A power that creates food affects the body, family obligations, markets, military logistics, land use, and religious meaning differently. The writer can then choose which consequences the story will own and which it will bracket.

## 9.10 Counterfactual prompts

Counterfactual variation tests whether a component is doing real work:

- If the protagonist received the same power but had a different desire, would the engine change?
- If everyone could access the system, which conflict disappears?
- If progress were invisible, what social structures would change?
- If the mentor were removed, could the mechanism still be learned?
- If the first-book goal were achieved at the midpoint, what story remains?
- If the protagonist could not fight, which progression scenes survive?
- If the rank names were removed, could the reader still compare states?

These questions expose decorative components.

# 10. The Genre-Specific Brainstorming Problem

## 10.1 Operational account of progression fantasy

For concept development, a work can be treated as progression fantasy to the degree that four conditions are central:

1. **agency transformation:** the protagonist or focal collective gains, loses, reorganizes, or learns to deploy meaningful capacities;
2. **process visibility:** the narrative attends to how that transformation occurs;
3. **comparability:** readers can distinguish earlier, present, and prospective states;
4. **consequence:** state changes alter future options, conflicts, roles, or relationships.

This account does not require numbers. It accommodates martial, magical, intellectual, professional, social, political, craft, communal, and mixed progression. It also permits regression and refusal.

## 10.2 Progression centrality

A candidate may contain advancement without being organized by it. The centrality test asks:

> If the progression process were summarized between chapters rather than dramatized, would the work lose one of its principal anticipated pleasures and causal structures?

In *Iron Prince*, removing training, measurement, adaptation, and competition would remove the novel's engine [55]. In *Mother of Learning*, summarizing Zorian's accumulation of knowledge and skill would also collapse the loop's investigative structure [52]. In *The Wandering Inn*, progression shares centrality with community, hospitality, and world-spanning narrative, so the answer is more distributed [63]. Centrality can be plural.

## 10.3 The progress object

Writers should specify what is progressing. Common objects include:

- raw capability;
- control or reliability;
- repertoire;
- knowledge and inference;
- resources and tools;
- social status or legal recognition;
- institutional access;
- relationship capacity;
- team coordination;
- craft output;
- territory or infrastructure;
- self-understanding;
- freedom from dependence;
- capacity to care for others;
- ability to choose among values.

A work can track several. The object should not be called “power” if the actual narrative interest is control, knowledge, or social permission.

## 10.4 Progression topology

Brainstorming should select or discover a topology: the shape of change.

**Ladder:** ordered stages with thresholds.

**Tree:** branching specializations and exclusions.

**Network:** capabilities arise through combinations and relationships.

**Spiral:** the protagonist revisits similar situations with deeper capacity or interpretation.

**Cycle:** gains require recurring maintenance or decay.

**Portfolio:** multiple resources or skills are managed without one total order.

**Conversion system:** one form of capital becomes another with loss or risk.

**Territorial system:** agency depends on controlled spaces, infrastructure, or networks.

**Collective system:** the relevant unit is a team, household, sect, guild, or community.

Published works often combine forms. *Iron Prince* uses visible rankings and attributes with adaptive combat development [55]. *Soulhome* supports architectural and combinatorial thinking [64]. *The Wandering Inn* mixes class-based gains with a growing social network and institution [63]. *Beware of Chicken* distributes progress across person, farm, companions, and community [58].

## 10.5 Process visibility without exposition overload

The genre promise encourages explanation, but explanation can replace drama. Brainstorming should identify **situations that reveal rules through use**:

- failed attempt;
- comparison with a rival;
- resource negotiation;
- teaching;
- diagnosis;
- repair;
- public test;
- rule exploitation;
- conflict between two system interpretations;
- unexpected interaction;
- cost appearing after apparent success.

*Into the Labyrinth* uses instruction and trial to reveal the meaning of Hugh's abilities [54]. *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* uses technical inquiry, enchantment, schooling, and tower danger [53]. *Mother of Learning* can test hypotheses across repetitions [52]. The mechanism becomes plot.

## 10.6 Earnedness

Brainstorming should not equate earnedness with training duration. Four forms can be separated:

- **causal earnedness:** the gain follows from established conditions;
- **agential earnedness:** the protagonist makes a relevant choice, risk, or effort;
- **epistemic earnedness:** the reader has enough information to understand possibility;
- **dramatic earnedness:** the gain's effect is proportionate to preparation, cost, and consequence.

Reidon receives an extraordinary growth-related advantage, but its narrative function depends on the body, work, competition, relationships, and institutional context around it [55]. Lindon's path begins with interventions and opportunities he did not create, yet his choices and continued adaptation remain central [51]. A gift can begin an engine if retaining, interpreting, and using it remain agential.

## 10.7 Escalation is not progression

Escalation increases magnitude or urgency. Progression changes feasible action. A writer who brainstorms only larger enemies eventually narrows conflict to output comparison. Alternative difficulty sources include:

- incomplete information;
- jurisdiction;
- conflicting objectives;
- coordination;
- public accountability;
- asymmetric counters;
- collateral risk;
- time scarcity;
- status consequences;
- moral incompatibility;
- resource opportunity cost;
- an opponent who benefits from the protagonist's usual method.

*Dungeon Crawler Carl* creates difficulty through changing rules, spectacle, sponsorship, and manipulation, not only stronger monsters [59]. *Mother of Learning* creates difficulty through hidden causality, social knowledge, and limited retained advantages [52].

## 10.8 Progression and identity

A progression system tells the protagonist what kinds of person are valuable. Brainstorming becomes richer when the protagonist's relation to that valuation is specified:

- acceptance;
- aspiration;
- shame;
- strategic compliance;
- reform;
- refusal;
- divided loyalty;
- dependence without belief;
- success followed by disillusionment.

*Unsouled* begins with a social identity imposed by local classification [51]. *Beware of Chicken* reorganizes identity around withdrawal from a dominant pursuit [58]. *The Rage of Dragons* shows a life increasingly organized by a chosen but consuming aim [60].

## 10.9 The minimum viable progression engine

Before moving to outlining, a candidate should be able to state:

1. who changes;
2. what dimension changes;
3. why change matters now;
4. what action or process produces change;
5. what limits or costs it;
6. how the reader knows change occurred;
7. who responds;
8. what new problem the gain creates;
9. what relationship is altered;
10. what would count as enough.

This is a minimum, not a formula for greatness. It prevents a progression label from concealing an undeveloped engine.

# 11. Character-First Ideation

## 11.1 Character-first does not mean biography-first

Character-first brainstorming begins with an agent whose desires, constraints, interpretations, and relationships can organize progression. It does not require writing an exhaustive childhood history before discovering the story. The useful unit is a **motivated asymmetry**: the mismatch between what the protagonist wants or must do and what they can presently do, combined with a reason this mismatch cannot remain stable.

A character-first pass should identify:

- present capability;
- socially perceived capability;
- hidden or undervalued capability;
- central desire and immediate pressure;
- dominant strategy;
- false assumption or incomplete interpretation;
- dependency;
- boundary the character will not cross;
- circumstance that may force revision of that boundary;
- form of progress the character desires;
- form of progress the story may ultimately value.

The distinction between desired and narratively valued progress is essential. Tau in *The Rage of Dragons* desires martial advancement as an instrument of revenge, while the novel's structure permits the reader to examine the cost of that organizing purpose [60]. Jin in *Beware of Chicken* rejects the expected path of competitive cultivation, and the narrative develops an alternative field of growth around work, care, place, and community [58].

## 11.2 Baseline without generic weakness

“Weak protagonist” is not a complete character concept. Weakness must be dimensional and contextual. A protagonist may be:

- low in recognized rank but high in persistence;
- physically limited but unusually adaptive;
- powerful but uncontrolled;
- knowledgeable but uncredentialed;
- socially connected but technically unskilled;
- gifted in a domain the institution misclassifies;
- competent alone but unable to cooperate;
- capable of rapid growth but unable to survive ordinary training;
- strong in a way that violates law or identity.

Lindon's opening condition in *Unsouled* matters because it is institutionally interpreted and socially enforced, not simply because his combat output is low [51]. Hugh's difficulty in *Into the Labyrinth* matters because apparent failure is tied to pedagogy, self-conception, and institutional judgment [54]. Reidon's bodily limitation in *Iron Prince* matters in relation to selection, technology, visibility, and growth potential [55]. Each baseline is a relation between person and system.

## 11.3 Desire architecture

A protagonist can hold several goals, but brainstorming should distinguish their causal roles.

**Instrumental goal:** the next concrete outcome sought.

**Terminal goal:** the condition valued for its own sake.

**Protective goal:** what must not be lost.

**Identity goal:** the kind of person the protagonist wants to be or be seen as.

**Assigned goal:** what family, institution, culture, or system demands.

**Latent need:** a transformation the protagonist does not yet recognize.

Corin in *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* pursues answers concerning his brother while navigating attunement, education, family expectation, and danger [53]. Lindon's desire to avert catastrophe interacts with the long history of exclusion that shaped how he understands capability and worth [51]. Rick in *Street Cultivation* is concerned with material security and care for his sister rather than abstract supremacy [57]. These architectures produce different progression choices.

## 11.4 Progression as a temptation

A strong character engine often makes progress both necessary and dangerous. The capacity may tempt the protagonist to:

- adopt the institution's values;
- instrumentalize relationships;
- prefer measurable success to humane judgment;
- accept debt or dependence;
- narrow identity around one competence;
- confuse control with safety;
- escalate a grievance into a life purpose;
- treat other people as resources or benchmarks.

The brainstorm should specify which temptation is structurally generated by the mechanism. In *The Rage of Dragons*, extreme training is not an abstract vice pasted onto the character. It follows from revenge, hierarchy, and the available means of closing the gap [60]. In *Dungeon Crawler Carl*, performing well helps survival while also feeding a spectacle organized by hostile powers [59].

## 11.5 Agency under gifted or inherited power

A protagonist does not need to earn initial access through prior labor. Gifts, inheritance, reincarnation, accidental bonds, and external selection can be powerful inciting conditions. The character-first question is where meaningful agency begins.

Possible sites of agency include:

- accepting or refusing the gift;
- interpreting an ambiguous capacity;
- selecting among development paths;
- concealing or revealing it;
- choosing a teacher;
- absorbing its social consequences;
- deciding what it will not be used for;
- paying maintenance costs;
- correcting harm caused by early misuse;
- redistributing access.

Reidon's exceptional growth potential in *Iron Prince* creates story because its practical value must be realized within a body, institution, training process, competitive field, and social network [55]. Theo's retained experience in *Soulhome* gives him an asymmetry, but he still confronts rebuilding, trust, changed circumstances, and the possibility that prior interpretations were wrong [64].

## 11.6 Character progression beyond power

Brainstorming should track at least one non-capability dimension. Possibilities include:

- tolerance for dependence;
- ability to teach;
- willingness to be known;
- capacity for reciprocal trust;
- judgment under uncertainty;
- ability to recognize another person's agency;
- freedom from an inherited metric;
- integration of multiple identities;
- ability to stop.

*Super Powereds: Year 1* is useful because control and professional competence develop alongside friendship, secrecy, shame, rivalry, and belonging [61]. *Into the Labyrinth* similarly links magical development to peer relations and revised self-understanding [54].

## 11.7 The character-first generation sequence

A practical sequence is:

1. write five motivated asymmetries;
2. give each asymmetry three different desired transformations;
3. assign each transformation one mechanism that fits the character and one that conflicts with them;
4. identify who benefits if the protagonist advances;
5. identify who loses access, status, safety, or control;
6. identify one relationship that improvement strains;
7. identify one trait that aids early progress but becomes harmful later;
8. identify one capacity the protagonist cannot gain without changing values or dependencies;
9. state a first-book transformation and a series-level “enough” condition;
10. compare the resulting engines rather than selecting the most sympathetic biography.

# 12. Progression-System-First Ideation

## 12.1 Begin with operations, not labels

System-first brainstorming is productive when the writer is fascinated by rules, abilities, training, resources, or interactions. Its main risk is building an inventory before discovering a story. The correction is to begin with operations.

A progression system can be described through eight verbs:

1. **acquire:** how access begins;
2. **practice:** what repeated action changes performance;
3. **convert:** what inputs become what capacities;
4. **combine:** how components interact;
5. **test:** how state becomes visible;
6. **retain:** what persists, decays, or requires maintenance;
7. **counter:** how the system is resisted or exploited;
8. **transmit:** how knowledge or capacity moves between people.

Nouns such as mana, core, class, attunement, device, and skill become meaningful only through these operations.

## 12.2 The mechanism chain

For each major advancement, brainstorm a complete chain:

$$
Resource \rightarrow Access \rightarrow Attempt \rightarrow Feedback \rightarrow Revision \rightarrow Integration \rightarrow Consequence
$$

A story need not dramatize every link every time. The concept should know they exist. Missing links create familiar defects:

- resource without scarcity produces arbitrary abundance;
- access without gatekeepers produces unexplained inequality;
- attempt without feedback produces inscrutable training;
- feedback without revision produces repetitive failure;
- gain without integration produces a new ability that appears only at the climax;
- integration without consequence produces decorative progression.

## 12.3 Capability, control, repertoire, and judgment

System design should distinguish several kinds of improvement.

**Capability:** maximum possible effect.

**Control:** reliability, precision, restraint, or safety.

**Repertoire:** number and diversity of available actions.

**Efficiency:** result per unit of cost.

**Judgment:** ability to select the right action under uncertainty.

**Coordination:** ability to combine with others.

**Resilience:** ability to recover, adapt, or continue under disruption.

A system becomes narratively richer when these dimensions can diverge. The students in *Super Powereds: Year 1* make control a central object rather than treating raw capacity as sufficient [61]. Corin's technical and analytical relation to magic in *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* supports progress through understanding and construction, not only output [53].

## 12.4 Costs and opportunity structures

Costs should not be brainstormed as generic pain added to make power feel serious. A cost matters when it changes choice. Useful categories include:

- consumption of scarce material;
- time that cannot be spent on another obligation;
- bodily risk or recovery;
- cognitive alteration;
- legal exposure;
- debt;
- relationship dependence;
- reputation;
- ecological damage;
- loss of future options;
- value compromise;
- attention from stronger actors.

*Street Cultivation* demonstrates costs embedded in political economy rather than isolated from daily survival [57]. *The Rage of Dragons* demonstrates time, bodily extremity, and psychological narrowing as components of a chosen path [60]. *Dungeon Crawler Carl* makes rewards inseparable from surveillance and manipulation [59].

## 12.5 Feedback architecture

Feedback should answer different questions through different channels.

- **internal feedback:** sensation, perception, stability, fatigue;
- **instrumental feedback:** test, score, rank, device reading;
- **performance feedback:** result under challenge;
- **social feedback:** recognition, fear, invitation, exclusion;
- **economic feedback:** price, wage, sponsorship, resource access;
- **institutional feedback:** certification, promotion, jurisdiction;
- **relational feedback:** trust, dependence, estrangement;
- **adversarial feedback:** a counter specifically designed for the new ability.

*Iron Prince* uses explicit measurements and competition, but those measurements matter because people and institutions react to them [55]. *The Wandering Inn* uses system notifications while the practical meaning of a class or skill emerges through guests, crises, reputation, and community [63].

## 12.6 Branching and exclusion

A system in which every useful capacity can eventually be acquired may lose character differentiation. Brainstorming should determine what choices exclude, delay, weaken, or transform alternatives. Exclusion can arise from:

- incompatible materials;
- finite training time;
- identity commitments;
- bodily architecture;
- social contract;
- jurisdiction;
- risk tolerance;
- mutually exclusive teachers;
- public affiliation;
- moral refusal.

Branching is meaningful when it changes future problems, not merely visual style. *Soulhome* is analytically useful because construction implies path dependence: what is built shapes what can be supported later [64].

## 12.7 Failure as information

Repeated attempts become tedious when failure only delays success. Failure can instead:

- reveal a hidden rule;
- disconfirm an interpretation;
- expose the wrong metric;
- change a relationship;
- consume a future option;
- teach an opponent;
- alter public status;
- create a partial capability;
- transfer cost to someone else;
- reveal that the desired progression is misframed.

The time-loop structure in *Mother of Learning* makes failed plans unusually information-rich because some knowledge persists across reset [52]. Yet even there, not every consequence is costless, and the mystery of the loop constrains experimentation. The lesson is to design retention and loss together.

## 12.8 Counter-system design

For every high-value ability, brainstorm at least four counters:

1. direct negation;
2. indirect environmental constraint;
3. strategic incentive that makes use undesirable;
4. social or legal response.

A fifth, more powerful counter is **learning opposition**: an actor studies the protagonist's pattern and changes behavior. This prevents the system from becoming a fixed key for every lock.

## 12.9 Distribution and monopoly

A progression mechanism immediately raises distributive questions:

- Why does everyone not use it?
- Who controls instruction?
- Who certifies competence?
- Who produces the inputs?
- Who bears failed experimentation?
- Can capacity be inherited, bought, stolen, licensed, or revoked?
- What secondary labor makes elite advancement possible?
- Which groups define legitimate progression?

*The First Step* places cultivation within sect hierarchy and labor [56]. *Street Cultivation* places it within corporate and class structures [57]. *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* ties attunement access to towers and social institutions [53]. A system becomes a world when its distribution is designed.

## 12.10 System-first exit test

A system-first concept should answer:

> What makes the protagonist's story complete even if the system contains higher levels?

Without that answer, the ladder becomes the plot's only terminal logic. The response can be relational, institutional, moral, or goal-based. The protagonist may reach sufficient capacity to rescue, expose, reform, protect, create, teach, or refuse. The system may remain larger than the narrative.

# 13. World, Ecology, and Institution-First Ideation

## 13.1 Worldbuilding as action structure

World-first brainstorming often begins with history, geography, culture, cosmology, or political order. For progression fantasy, the most productive question is not “What exists?” but “How does this arrangement distribute feasible action?” A world detail enters the engine when it enables, limits, prices, recognizes, or interprets progression.

Worldbuilding can be separated into:

- **ontological layer:** what forces, entities, and transformations are possible;
- **ecological layer:** where resources come from and what processes replenish them;
- **institutional layer:** who organizes access, knowledge, certification, and force;
- **cultural layer:** what forms of advancement are admired, feared, gendered, sacred, stigmatized, or denied;
- **historical layer:** how prior conflicts produced current distributions;
- **experiential layer:** what daily life feels like for people at different positions.

## 13.2 The institutional triad

For each progression pathway, brainstorm three institutions:

1. an institution that **enables** progression;
2. an institution that **extracts value from** progression;
3. an institution that **limits or legitimizes** progression.

One institution may perform all three, creating internal conflict. A sect can teach, collect labor, and punish unauthorized practice. An academy can educate, rank, recruit, and reproduce class hierarchy. A corporation can finance training, own enhancements, and enforce debt.

*Street Cultivation* develops the extraction dimension explicitly [57]. *The First Step* develops sect membership as access, obligation, hierarchy, and mobility [56]. *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* uses schooling, family, military expectation, and tower access as intersecting institutions [53].

## 13.3 Resource ecology

A progression resource should have an origin, renewal cycle, extraction method, transport problem, storage limit, and externality unless the world explicitly establishes otherwise. These questions create story:

- Who lives near the source?
- Who is displaced by extraction?
- What labor is dangerous but socially invisible?
- What substitutes exist?
- What happens when a high-level actor consumes at scale?
- Does the resource respond to use?
- Can it be cultivated, synthesized, or conserved?
- What black markets arise?

The answer need not become an economic treatise. It prevents resources from appearing only as rewards.

## 13.4 Status and recognition

Power and status may correlate without being identical. Brainstorm alternatives:

- status follows certified power;
- status follows lineage despite demonstrated weakness;
- power is illegal without status;
- low-status labor sustains elite power;
- one culture values a capacity another devalues;
- public ranking mismeasures private usefulness;
- institutions recognize individual ability but not collective capacity.

The opening of *Unsouled* links classification to social standing and permitted practice [51]. *Iron Prince* links performance data to visibility and institutional judgment [55]. *Street Cultivation* ties capacity to socioeconomic position while preserving structural barriers [57].

## 13.5 Daily life test

A world-first brainstorm should produce one ordinary day for at least four positions:

- person excluded from the system;
- novice participant;
- institutional gatekeeper;
- elite beneficiary.

No invented scene is required at this stage. The writer records schedules, obligations, risks, services, and dependencies. If every position spends the day training or fighting, the ecology is probably underdeveloped.

## 13.6 Historical causation

Historical lore should explain present constraints rather than merely add antiquity. Ask:

- Which current monopoly arose from an earlier emergency?
- Which safety rule preserves a past power arrangement?
- Which rank name encodes a discarded worldview?
- Which “natural” inequality was engineered?
- Which resource crisis shaped institutions?
- Which defeated progression path survives in marginal practice?

This transforms history into contested infrastructure.

## 13.7 World mutation under protagonist growth

As the protagonist advances, the world should not remain at the same interpretive scale. New access reveals different institutions, costs, and histories. *Cradle* repeatedly expands Lindon's understanding of the world's power orders and jurisdictions as his capabilities grow [51]. *The Wandering Inn* expands through a network centered on a recurring place, allowing local hospitality to intersect with larger conflicts [63]. The brainstorm should specify what becomes visible at each scale and why it was not visible earlier.

# 14. Opposition, Challenge, and Counterplay

## 14.1 Opposition as a portfolio

One antagonist cannot carry every form of resistance across a long series without becoming implausibly omnipresent. Brainstorm an opposition portfolio:

- **mirror rival:** seeks a similar goal by another path;
- **gatekeeper:** controls access or legitimacy;
- **predatory actor:** exploits the protagonist's vulnerability;
- **system defender:** benefits from current distribution;
- **environmental challenge:** resists without intention;
- **internalized opponent:** habit, compulsion, fear, or identity;
- **relationship conflict:** incompatible good pursued by an ally;
- **meta-opposition:** mechanism that changes the rules or interprets performance.

*Dungeon Crawler Carl* benefits from layered opposition: creatures and floors, rival participants, producers, sponsors, audience pressures, and the larger coercive structure [59]. *Super Powereds: Year 1* combines training competition, secrecy, personal history, institutional selection, and social conflict [61].

## 14.2 Adversary capability model

For each major opponent, record:

- objective;
- information;
- resources;
- constraints;
- model of the protagonist;
- response threshold;
- preferred indirect method;
- vulnerability unrelated to raw strength;
- reason they cannot simply end the conflict now.

Counterplay becomes credible when the opponent's response follows from what they know. Omniscient counters feel arbitrary; no counters make progress frictionless.

## 14.3 Challenge dimensions

A challenge can vary along several dimensions:

- required output;
- precision;
- endurance;
- adaptability;
- information quality;
- coordination;
- moral cost;
- public exposure;
- reversibility;
- time pressure;
- legal consequence;
- resource efficiency;
- protection of third parties.

The same capability can be tested differently by changing a dimension. This produces variation without constant inflation.

## 14.4 Dynamic difficulty

Dynamic difficulty follows a feedback loop:

$$
Protagonist\ Gain \rightarrow World\ Observation \rightarrow Opponent\ Update \rightarrow New\ Constraint
$$

The response need not be immediate. Some gains remain secret. Others create reputation before the protagonist understands them. *Iron Prince* uses visible competition to make growth socially legible [55]. *Dungeon Crawler Carl* makes performance part of an observed spectacle, allowing the system and audience to react [59].

## 14.5 Opposition that changes values

The strongest opponent may not block advancement but reveal what advancement serves. A rival can embody the successful endpoint of the protagonist's current values. An institution can offer exactly the desired rank at an unacceptable price. A dependent person can make maximal training morally impossible.

*The Rage of Dragons* provides a useful structure for examining how a compelling goal can narrow the protagonist's life [60]. *Beware of Chicken* places a different life beside the dominant cultivation logic [58]. Opposition can be comparative rather than purely combative.

# 15. Relationship and Ensemble Ideation

## 15.1 Relationships as progression infrastructure

Relationships often provide instruction, resources, information, emotional regulation, access, combination, and feedback. Treating them only as subplots understates their systemic role.

For each core relationship, brainstorm:

- what each person can do that the other cannot;
- what each person misreads about the other;
- what resource or permission flows between them;
- how one person's progress changes the other's options;
- what asymmetry creates resentment or dependence;
- what would make cooperation costly;
- what the relationship looks like after the original need disappears.

## 15.2 Mentor design

A mentor can function as teacher, gatekeeper, interpreter, patron, manipulator, model, obstacle, or future rival. The mentor should have a reason to teach and a limit on usefulness. Limits include:

- incomplete knowledge;
- conflicting values;
- political constraint;
- inability to understand the protagonist's difference;
- cost of intervention;
- incentive to preserve dependence;
- competence in an earlier stage but not a later one.

*Into the Labyrinth* uses apprenticeship to reinterpret Hugh's apparent incapacity and to organize group development [54]. *Soulhome* makes prior mentorship and betrayal part of Theo's relation to knowledge and trust [64].

## 15.3 Rival design

A rival is most useful when comparison reveals a meaningful difference rather than only a score. Vary:

- same goal, different method;
- same method, different value;
- similar baseline, unequal access;
- different baseline, converging competence;
- private friendship, public competition;
- superior output, inferior control;
- institutional favorite versus unofficial innovator.

Visible competition in *Iron Prince* provides repeated comparison while relationships prevent every rival from functioning as a disposable obstacle [55].

## 15.4 Ensemble progression

An ensemble needs more than several individual ladders. Brainstorm **joint capacities** that exist only through coordination. The group may progress in:

- shared vocabulary;
- trust under uncertainty;
- division of labor;
- combination techniques;
- conflict resolution;
- distributed knowledge;
- institutional reputation;
- ability to recover from member loss;
- ethical decision procedures.

*Super Powereds: Year 1* and *Into the Labyrinth* both demonstrate the narrative value of different abilities developing inside peer networks rather than in isolation [54, 61]. *Beware of Chicken* extends progression across a household and community with non-identical forms of growth [58].

## 15.5 Dependency and autonomy

Progression stories often celebrate increasing autonomy. Yet complete independence can erase relationship stakes. A richer engine may change the type of dependence rather than eliminate it. Early dependence may be imposed and opaque; later interdependence may be chosen and reciprocal.

The brainstorm should ask not only “What can the protagonist eventually do alone?” but “What can they finally entrust to someone else?”

# 16. Theme, Value, Cost, and Transformation

## 16.1 Theme as a design relation

Theme emerges from repeated contrasts and consequences. It is not secured by including a statement about ambition or friendship. A brainstorm becomes thematic when the same value conflict appears through mechanism, institution, relationship, and outcome.

For example, **power versus autonomy** can be embodied by:

- a mechanism that requires a binding contract;
- an institution that owns enhancements;
- a mentor who controls access;
- a relationship strained by surveillance;
- an ending that distinguishes capacity from self-government.

The specific configuration would need to be developed as its own work. The example here names a relation rather than an invented plot.

## 16.2 The governing value of progression

Every progression system defines improvement according to some metric. Brainstorm:

- what the culture counts;
- what the protagonist counts;
- what the reader can see that both miss;
- who benefits from the metric;
- what cannot be represented by it;
- what behavior it incentivizes;
- what happens when the protagonist excels by the wrong measure.

*Iron Prince* makes quantification and growth potential narratively explicit [55]. *Street Cultivation* connects power to monetary and institutional value [57]. *Beware of Chicken* questions whether the dominant ladder exhausts meaningful flourishing [58].

## 16.3 Cost as thematic evidence

A cost has thematic force when it reveals a value hierarchy. Time spent training may cost a relationship because the protagonist treats every non-training hour as waste. Public recognition may cost safety because the system commodifies visible talent. Mastery may cost continuity of self because the mechanism changes memory or desire.

Generic fatigue is less useful unless it changes a decision. The brainstorm should link each major cost to:

- who bears it;
- who can avoid it;
- whether it is visible;
- whether it is reversible;
- what value justifies it;
- what alternative the protagonist refuses.

## 16.4 Value inversion

Value inversion asks whether the apparent weakness can become a different kind of strength, or the desired strength can reveal a weakness. The technique should not be used to claim that every disadvantage is secretly beneficial. It is a search method.

*Unsouled* begins with a stigmatized classification that helps define Lindon's relation to ingenuity, opportunity, and status [51]. *Into the Labyrinth* reinterprets apparent magical failure through a different understanding of aptitude [54]. *Beware of Chicken* turns withdrawal from competitive ascent into the basis for another mode of growth [58].

## 16.5 Thematic endpoint

A terminal rank is not necessarily a thematic ending. The thematic endpoint answers a value question through action. It might show that the protagonist can:

- use power without reproducing domination;
- accept dependence without surrendering agency;
- stop optimizing;
- teach rather than monopolize;
- choose care when the metric rewards victory;
- abandon revenge after gaining the ability to complete it;
- reform the institution rather than merely enter its elite.

The precise answer should emerge from the work, not from a craft rule. Brainstorming should make the question visible.

# 17. Plot Engines, Scene Yield, and Serial Elasticity

## 17.1 Premise versus engine in practice

A premise can produce a strong opening and still fail to generate a middle. Engine testing asks what recurrent activity will produce differentiated consequences. Common progression-fantasy engines include:

- academy term;
- expedition;
- dungeon floor;
- tower attempt;
- tournament circuit;
- craft commission;
- apprenticeship cycle;
- guild contract;
- repeated time interval;
- settlement development;
- business or profession hub;
- pilgrimage;
- military campaign;
- investigation tied to advancement;
- political office and jurisdiction.

The label is not sufficient. Each engine needs a variation rule.

## 17.2 Scene families

A **scene family** is a recurrent type of dramatic problem with its own objective, obstacle, and state change. A developed concept should generate several:

- acquisition;
- instruction;
- experiment;
- failure analysis;
- resource negotiation;
- social comparison;
- public demonstration;
- private concealment;
- countermeasure design;
- rescue;
- construction;
- teaching;
- institutional bargaining;
- alliance formation;
- moral refusal;
- recovery;
- consequence management.

*Mother of Learning* can move among reconnaissance, practice, social experimentation, alliance, and mystery while preserving the loop as a common engine [52]. *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* moves among tower danger, education, technical experimentation, relationship conflict, and investigation [53]. *The Wandering Inn* moves among hospitality, community formation, local crisis, class progression, and far-reaching network consequences [63].

## 17.3 The ten-scene yield test

Before selection, write ten one-sentence scene propositions. Each must identify:

- immediate objective;
- obstacle type;
- progression dimension;
- relationship involved;
- state change;
- why this scene could occur only in this concept.

Reject superficial differences. Three duels with stronger opponents count as one family unless their goals, constraints, or consequences differ.

A candidate passes provisionally when the ten scenes express at least five families and no more than three rely on the same victory condition. The thresholds are heuristic.

## 17.4 Loop and difference

Serial engines need both recurrence and difference. Formally:

$$
Episode_{n+1} = Stable\ Identity + Changed\ State + New\ Constraint
$$

The stable identity tells the reader what kind of pleasure to anticipate. Changed state makes prior events matter. New constraint prevents repetition.

In *Dungeon Crawler Carl*, floor progression provides stable identity, accumulated abilities and relationships provide changed state, and new rules, environments, politics, and audience pressures provide new constraints [59]. In *Mother of Learning*, the repeated month is stable, retained knowledge changes state, and revised objectives and discoveries create difference [52].

## 17.5 Mutation points

A long-form engine should change mode before recurrence exhausts its possibilities. Brainstorm at least three mutation points:

1. **competence mutation:** the protagonist outgrows basic training;
2. **social mutation:** private progress becomes publicly consequential;
3. **institutional mutation:** the protagonist can affect or threaten access rules;
4. **scale mutation:** individual problems become collective;
5. **value mutation:** the original goal is reinterpreted;
6. **mechanism mutation:** a hidden property changes how advancement works;
7. **role mutation:** learner becomes teacher, follower becomes leader, entrant becomes gatekeeper.

*Cradle* repeatedly changes scale, jurisdiction, responsibility, and team function while preserving the central expectation of intelligible advancement [51]. *The Wandering Inn* expands the reach of a stable occupational-social center [63].

## 17.6 Serial elasticity

**Serial elasticity** is the capacity to expand or contract without breaking the engine. A concept has high elasticity when:

- arcs can close at several lengths;
- secondary paths can be developed without postponing the central goal indefinitely;
- the world contains lateral variation, not only higher tiers;
- relationships can carry arcs independent of rank;
- mysteries have staged answers;
- the terminal condition remains visible.

High elasticity is not always desirable. A tightly designed trilogy may benefit from low elasticity and strong convergence. An open web serial may need greater modularity.

## 17.7 Escalation ceilings

Brainstorm the consequences of the highest plausible capability. Ask:

- What ordinary conflict becomes impossible?
- What geography becomes irrelevant?
- Which institutions lose authority?
- What collateral stakes become implausible?
- Can uncertainty, jurisdiction, value conflict, or coordination remain meaningful?
- Does the protagonist still need other people?
- What can oppose the protagonist without retroactively trivializing the world?

An escalation ceiling is not merely the strongest possible enemy. It is the point at which the engine's current problem types stop functioning.

## 17.8 Lateral progression

Lateral progression expands options without simply increasing magnitude. It can include:

- new domain;
- new social role;
- teaching;
- leadership;
- crafting;
- political jurisdiction;
- team combination;
- interpretation of hidden rules;
- ability to operate under a different constraint;
- reduction of collateral cost.

Lateral movement preserves challenge and character distinction. It is visible in the technical breadth of *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* [53], the social and occupational network of *The Wandering Inn* [63], and the team-oriented development of *Into the Labyrinth* [54].

## 17.9 Book-level promise

A series concept should not defer all satisfaction to the terminal book. Each volume needs a local transformation. Brainstorm for Book 1:

- a capability or understanding that becomes reliable;
- a relationship that changes state;
- an institution newly entered, escaped, or understood;
- a question answered;
- a larger question sharpened;
- a consequence that makes return to baseline impossible.

The first book of *Mother of Learning* can provide meaningful discoveries and capability changes while preserving the larger loop mystery [52]. *Unsouled* establishes a decisive departure and new path while keeping the future threat in view [51].

# 18. Analogical Transfer and Responsible Use of Literature

## 18.1 Influence is unavoidable; copying is not

Writers think with prior forms. The ethical and creative task is not to achieve influence-free invention. It is to transform knowledge rather than reproduce protected expression or unexamined feature bundles. Analogy research distinguishes relational structure from surface similarity [25-26]. This supports a practical method: extract the relation that performs narrative work, then seek a different domain, value, or mechanism capable of instantiating it.

## 18.2 Surface mapping and structural mapping

A surface mapping from *Mother of Learning* would copy a magical student trapped in a repeating month. A structural mapping might extract:

- an environment that can be revisited;
- retention of some state and reset of another;
- safe or semi-safe hypothesis testing;
- comparative observation of people;
- a hidden cause that makes repetition finite and urgent [52].

A surface mapping from *Street Cultivation* would copy modern qi corporations. A structural mapping might extract:

- power as a form of capital;
- institutions that own access;
- advancement embedded in ordinary survival;
- family obligation limiting optimization;
- social class reproduced through enhancement [57].

A surface mapping from *Beware of Chicken* would copy a cultivator who farms. A structural mapping might extract:

- refusal of the genre's dominant status ladder;
- an alternative progress object;
- local work creating community;
- irony produced by divergence between self-understanding and external consequence [58].

## 18.3 The abstraction ladder

Analyze a source at four levels:

1. **expression:** names, scenes, dialogue, imagery, sequence;
2. **device:** time loop, tower, academy, inn, farm, dungeon show;
3. **function:** repeated comparison, gatekeeping, stable hub, value inversion, public pressure;
4. **relation:** retained knowledge versus reset world; capacity versus ownership; progress versus autonomy; place stability versus network expansion.

Transfer should usually occur at levels 3 or 4. Levels 1 and 2 require greater caution because recognizable expression and distinctive combinations belong to the source work.

## 18.4 Multi-source triangulation

One-source inspiration increases fixation. Triangulation uses at least three structurally relevant works and one non-fictional domain. For example, an academy brainstorm might compare:

- attunement and technical study in *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* [53];
- aptitude reclassification and ensemble apprenticeship in *Into the Labyrinth* [54];
- measured combat growth in *Iron Prince* [55];
- an actual institution such as a conservatory, trade apprenticeship, research laboratory, military academy, or licensing regime.

The writer records similarities, differences, hidden labor, gatekeeping, and failure modes. The goal is not a collage of visible features but a new causal configuration.

## 18.5 Transformation distance

A candidate inspired by a source should differ along several dimensions:

- protagonist desire;
- mechanism;
- social distribution;
- value conflict;
- relationship architecture;
- scene engine;
- tone;
- terminal condition.

Changing only names and setting is low transformation distance. Changing device while preserving the same relations may also be low. A useful test is whether the candidate can be explained without naming the source.

## 18.6 Respect for cultural forms

Cultivation fiction, wuxia, xianxia, martial-arts traditions, religious concepts, and culturally specific institutions should not be treated as neutral bags of exotic terminology. Scholarship on Chinese internet literature and transformations of martial protagonists provides historical and cultural context beyond trope lists [38-39]. Responsible brainstorming asks which concepts are being transferred, what histories they carry, and whether surface borrowing has replaced understanding.

The practical standard is not that writers may use only their own cultural materials. It is that research, specificity, and transformation should increase with distance from lived knowledge and with the sensitivity of the material.

## 18.7 Citation in private development documents

Even private brainstorming benefits from source tracking. Record:

- title and author;
- edition or serial location;
- exact feature observed;
- whether the note is quotation, paraphrase, inference, or personal reaction;
- intended level of transfer;
- changes made to avoid expressive copying;
- unresolved factual or cultural questions.

Source tracking protects both ethics and originality. It makes accidental convergence easier to detect.

# 19. Comparative Studies in Published Progression Fantasy

## 19.1 Method of comparison

This chapter does not rank the works or claim that their identified features caused commercial success. Each study isolates a design relation that a brainstorming process could discover, test, or transform. The repeated analytical categories are seed, coupled engine, generative consequences, closure pressure, and transferable question.

## 19.2 *Unsouled* by Will Wight

**Seed and baseline.** *Unsouled* begins with Wei Shi Lindon occupying a socially stigmatized position inside a culture organized around the sacred arts. His lack is classified, displayed, and institutionally consequential [51]. The baseline is therefore not private weakness. It is a public relation among body, label, family, custom, and permission.

**Coupled engine.** The concept connects exclusion to ingenuity and urgency, then connects urgency to a future catastrophe that cannot be prevented from inside the existing local order. Departure becomes both practical necessity and identity rupture. The wider progression ladder is attractive not merely because it promises strength, but because strength is tied to saving a home that denied Lindon full participation.

**Generative consequences.** The engine can repeatedly produce resource problems, mentorship and alliance problems, status misrecognition, technique adaptation, comparative trials, and expanding knowledge of the world's actual scale. A key source of renewal is epistemic expansion: each new level of competence reveals that local categories were partial.

**Closure pressure.** A future threat to home supplies a long-range horizon more determinate than “become strongest.” That horizon can constrain escalation and permit eventual return.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** What initial classification simultaneously limits action, shapes identity, and misdescribes the protagonist's future possibilities? The transferable relation is not simply “weak underdog.” It is **socially enforced baseline plus external horizon plus departure into a wider ontology**.

## 19.3 *Mother of Learning: ARC 1* by Domagoj Kurmaić

**Seed and baseline.** Zorian is a student in a magical academy caught in a repeating period of time. He retains memory across resets while much of the external world returns to an earlier state [52].

**Coupled engine.** The loop links progression, investigation, and characterization. Knowledge becomes the principal retained resource. Repetition permits comparative observation: the same person can be approached differently, the same event can be intercepted at another point, and the same skill can be practiced under revised conditions. The mechanism also creates ethical pressure because other people reset while Zorian's knowledge of them accumulates asymmetrically.

**Generative consequences.** The engine produces training, experimentation, route optimization, alliance testing, social revision, threat mapping, and causal inference. Repetition does not automatically guarantee variety. Variety comes from changing objective, information, risk, relationship, and retained state.

**Closure pressure.** A loop can become indefinitely renewable. The narrative therefore needs causes, limits, escalating knowledge, and a route toward exit. Mystery provides direction that pure optimization would lack.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** What asymmetry of retention allows failure to become information while preserving stakes? The relation is **repeatable environment plus selective persistence plus hidden finite cause**.

## 19.4 *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* by Andrew Rowe

**Seed and baseline.** Corin Cadence enters the Serpent Spire, a dangerous tower associated with magical attunements, while pursuing answers connected to his missing brother [53].

**Coupled engine.** The tower, attunement, academy, technical experimentation, family expectation, and investigation are not separate attractions. The tower provides gatekeeping and mystery. The attunement provides a structured capacity. Schooling provides social comparison and instruction. Corin's analytical disposition gives system understanding a character-specific form. The missing brother converts advancement into means rather than endpoint.

**Generative consequences.** The concept supports tower trials, classes, enchantment and technical work, team preparation, family conflict, duels, and investigation. Because technical understanding matters, progress can occur through repertoire, efficiency, construction, and insight rather than only raw output.

**Closure pressure.** A tower and academy can generate endless floors and terms. The personal investigation supplies a more definite question, but the system's larger mysteries can continually expand. Brainstorming should distinguish which mystery belongs to a book and which to the series.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** How can one advancement institution also function as the source of a personal mystery? The relation is **gatekeeping ordeal plus technical system plus emotionally specific inquiry**.

## 19.5 *Into the Labyrinth* by John Bierce

**Seed and baseline.** Hugh of Emblin believes himself to be an exceptionally poor magical student. Ordinary attempts fail, and institutional life reinforces the interpretation that he is inadequate [54].

**Coupled engine.** An unusual mentor and apprenticeship reframe failure. The engine joins misdiagnosed aptitude, pedagogy, peer friendship, complementary specialization, and a dangerous first-year trial. Improvement depends partly on learning what kind of learner and mage Hugh is, not merely doing more of the same practice.

**Generative consequences.** The concept supports diagnostic scenes, alternative instruction, team formation, specialized practice, social recovery, and group tests. Because peers differ, advancement can produce joint capacities rather than one total ranking.

**Closure pressure.** Academy progression supplies natural year and trial boundaries. The risk is that each installment becomes another term with a larger exam. Ensemble relationships, wider institutions, and evolving magical understanding provide mutation routes.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** What apparent incapacity is actually a mismatch among person, method, and institution? The relation is **failed standard pedagogy plus reclassification plus interdependent ensemble**.

## 19.6 *Iron Prince* by Bryce O'Connor and Luke Chmilenko

**Seed and baseline.** Reidon Ward begins from severe bodily disadvantage in a culture of technologically mediated combat progression. His Combat Assistance Device has poor initial specifications but extraordinary growth potential [55].

**Coupled engine.** The concept joins underdog body, explicit measurement, future potential, academy competition, public comparison, and adaptive combat. Growth is both promise and social signal. Others can observe the discrepancy between current weakness and future threat, producing attention, doubt, sponsorship, rivalry, and fear.

**Generative consequences.** The engine yields training, ranked matches, adaptation, data interpretation, institutional evaluation, friendship, rivalry, and strategic responses to an unusual developmental curve. Explicit metrics create anticipation, but relationships and tactical variation keep the metrics from becoming the whole experience.

**Closure pressure.** Exceptional growth invites open-ended ascent. A series needs goals and social transformations that are not merely the next rank. It also needs limits on how long “future potential” can substitute for present consequence.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** What if the protagonist's most important advantage is not current power but a publicly legible rate of change? The relation is **disadvantaged present plus exceptional derivative plus competitive observation**.

## 19.7 *The First Step* by Tao Wong

**Seed and baseline.** Long Wu Ying begins from an agrarian background and enters the institutional world of cultivation through an unexpected opening [56].

**Coupled engine.** The movement from farm to sect joins social mobility, labor, education, hierarchy, resource access, and identity. Cultivation is not simply a private meditation technology. It is embedded in an institution that allocates tasks, status, instruction, and opportunity.

**Generative consequences.** The engine can produce training, work assignments, resource acquisition, peer comparison, mentorship, institutional politics, travel, and negotiation between prior obligations and new aspirations. The agrarian baseline gives material contrast to sect life.

**Closure pressure.** Sect advancement can extend through ranks and wider worlds. The brainstorm should identify whether the central completion concerns personal mastery, family and origin, institutional belonging, or a changed relation to cultivation's hierarchy.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** How does entry into an advancement institution change ordinary labor and social identity before it changes combat ability? The relation is **class transition plus institutional access plus obligation**.

## 19.8 *Street Cultivation* by Sarah Lin

**Seed and baseline.** The work relocates cultivation into a modern social order in which qi capacity is economically valued and institutional power is concentrated. Rick's aims are bound to material survival and care for his sister [57].

**Coupled engine.** The strongest innovation is relational. Power, money, employment, debt, healthcare, corporate control, and fighting are mutually implicated. A gain in spiritual capacity can affect income and safety, while poverty limits opportunities to acquire that gain. Family responsibility constrains the protagonist's risk tolerance.

**Generative consequences.** The engine produces jobs, contracts, training, fights, predatory offers, medical needs, status negotiation, and trade-offs between immediate cash and long-term development. It supports progression without requiring a detached heroic economy.

**Closure pressure.** Structural inequality cannot be solved merely by one person becoming strong. This creates both thematic depth and a closure challenge. The narrative must decide whether completion means individual security, family care, mobility, resistance, reform, or clearer limits on what one protagonist can change.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** What happens when the advancement resource is also economic capital and access is institutionally owned? The relation is **capacity as commodity plus family obligation plus unequal opportunity**.

## 19.9 *Beware of Chicken* by Casualfarmer

**Seed and baseline.** A person from Earth inhabits the body of Jin Rou and rejects the expected danger and competition of cultivation life, seeking instead to farm [58].

**Coupled engine.** The concept operates through value inversion. Withdrawal from the dominant ladder creates another progression field involving agriculture, household, relationships, place, animals, and community. Humor and dramatic irony arise because Jin's interpretation of ordinary life does not exhaust the larger effects of what he does.

**Generative consequences.** The engine supports seasonal work, construction, food, local relationships, community problems, the development of nonhuman companions, and encounters with the wider cultivation world. Progress is distributed rather than monopolized by one hero.

**Closure pressure.** A pastoral or community-centered engine can continue through expansion of household and region. It risks losing focus if every new member merely repeats belonging. Closure can be organized around stable flourishing, protection of a way of life, or successful negotiation with the larger system.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** What alternative good becomes visible when the protagonist refuses the genre's default metric? The relation is **renunciation of competitive ascent plus local constructive work plus distributed growth**.

## 19.10 *Dungeon Crawler Carl* by Matt Dinniman

**Seed and baseline.** Following catastrophic destruction, Carl and Princess Donut enter a lethal dungeon structured as entertainment for an external audience. Survival occurs under game-like rules, public observation, sponsorship, and manipulation [59].

**Coupled engine.** The dungeon is not merely a sequence of combat spaces. The show transforms behavior. Performance affects audience, rewards, public identity, and producer incentives. The protagonists must become effective within rules designed by exploiters while preserving the capacity to resist those rules.

**Generative consequences.** The engine supports floor challenges, build decisions, alliances, audience management, sponsor relations, public performance, system exploitation, political discovery, and resistance. Carl and Donut's different social and tactical affordances enrich the paired center.

**Closure pressure.** Floor progression offers a visible path, but the larger coercive system exceeds any one floor. The series must coordinate local survival closure with systemic resistance. Endless spectacle is both an engine strength and a thematic danger.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** What if success is measured and rewarded by the institution whose domination the protagonist opposes? The relation is **coercive rules plus public spectacle plus survival-resistance contradiction**.

## 19.11 *The Rage of Dragons* by Evan Winter

**Seed and baseline.** Tau exists within a stratified martial society and organizes his life increasingly around revenge and the pursuit of combat capacity [60].

**Coupled engine.** The concept connects social hierarchy, military training, extraordinary deliberate effort, bodily danger, and a singular emotional aim. Progress has high urgency because capability is imagined as the route across a protected status gap.

**Generative consequences.** The engine supports training, selection, military bonds, tactical development, confrontation with hierarchy, and increasingly costly optimization. The protagonist's intensity generates momentum while also making narrowing and self-destruction available thematic consequences.

**Closure pressure.** Revenge supplies a terminal act but not automatically a completed identity. A brainstorm using revenge should ask what remains if the protagonist becomes capable of succeeding, and whether completion, refusal, or transformation answers the deeper arc.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** Which personal aim makes extreme advancement rational while also threatening to consume the person pursuing it? The relation is **status barrier plus revenge horizon plus self-narrowing optimization**.

## 19.12 *Super Powereds: Year 1* by Drew Hayes

**Seed and baseline.** Five young people who formerly could not control their abilities enter a program that allows them to train toward recognized superhero status while managing secrecy and social life [61].

**Coupled engine.** The work links control, education, professional selection, ensemble difference, friendship, competition, concealment, and identity. Progress is not simply acquiring more power. It includes learning reliability, judgment, teamwork, and public role.

**Generative consequences.** The ensemble produces classes, tests, social events, rivalries, secret management, team combinations, and divergent personal consequences. Different abilities and histories prevent one ladder from fully ordering everyone.

**Closure pressure.** Academic years provide installment boundaries, while professional qualification provides a longer horizon. The risk is repetitive school structure. Changing team dynamics, institutional knowledge, and public stakes permit mutation.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** How does a group progress when each member's central deficit is different and their futures are institutionally compared? The relation is **control deficit plus ensemble interdependence plus professional gatekeeping**.

## 19.13 *Bastion* by Phil Tucker

**Seed and baseline.** Scorio awakens within a system of rebirth and expected identity but is rejected and cast outside the path that should have defined his place [62].

**Coupled engine.** The concept joins lost or inaccessible past significance, present exclusion, hostile institutional judgment, unusual progression routes, and the need to reconstruct both capability and identity. Rebirth does not simply provide competence. It creates a discrepancy between expected destiny and present status.

**Generative consequences.** The engine supports survival outside sanctioned structures, alternative alliances, discovery of system rules, contests over legitimacy, and gradual reinterpretation of past and present. Exclusion creates pressure to seek methods the institution does not validate.

**Closure pressure.** Reincarnation and hidden past can continually enlarge mystery. The brainstorm should determine which identity question can be answered before all cosmology is known.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** What if the protagonist is excluded not because they lack a promised identity, but because the institution refuses or corrupts its recognition? The relation is **rebirth expectation plus institutional rejection plus reconstructed legitimacy**.

## 19.14 *The Wandering Inn* by pirateaba

**Seed and baseline.** Erin Solstice arrives in another world, occupies an abandoned inn, and gains the [Innkeeper] class [63].

**Coupled engine.** The inn acts as stable place, profession, social interface, and network hub. Class progression is legible, but the larger engine is relational: guests, food, refuge, reputation, danger, and hospitality connect local routines to distant peoples and political conflicts.

**Generative consequences.** The engine supports arrivals, departures, service problems, community formation, defense, cultural encounter, economic exchange, and network expansion. A fixed hub can produce broad worldbuilding because the world comes through it and because relationships extend beyond it.

**Closure pressure.** Network expansion has very high serial elasticity and correspondingly high convergence difficulty. Local arcs can close while the global network continues. Brainstorming a similar hub should identify what scale of closure belongs to the protagonist, the institution, and the world.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** What profession or place can convert ordinary recurring work into a portal for expanding social consequence? The relation is **stable occupational hub plus changing visitors plus cumulative network**.

## 19.15 *Soulhome* by Sarah Lin

**Seed and baseline.** Theo returns to the Nine Worlds in a new body after betrayal, carrying experience and questions from a prior life while needing to rebuild [64]. The progression system is organized around constructing internal “soulhomes.”

**Coupled engine.** Architecture becomes a model of self-development. Materials, design, sequencing, stability, and intended function can shape advancement. Theo's prior knowledge creates an advantage but also carries mistaken assumptions, emotional injury, and trust problems.

**Generative consequences.** The engine supports material acquisition, design decisions, construction, repair, experimentation, teaching, cross-cultural comparison, alliance, and mystery. Because built structure constrains later options, progression can carry path dependence.

**Closure pressure.** A construction system can expand indefinitely through larger and more elaborate structures. Personal mystery, betrayal, and the need to build a viable life provide non-quantitative endpoints.

**Transferable brainstorming question.** What domain of design can become a literal model of internal progression, with path dependence and visible structural trade-offs? The relation is **embodied construction plus retained expertise plus mistrust after betrayal**.

## 19.16 Comparative synthesis

The cases demonstrate that no single surface device defines a durable engine. Their highest-leverage relations differ:

| Work | High-leverage relation | Primary renewal source | Central brainstorming warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Unsouled* | classification, exclusion, and future horizon | widening ontology and scale | underdog status alone is insufficient |
| *Mother of Learning* | selective retention in a resetting world | changed knowledge and route | repetition needs finite mystery and variation |
| *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* | gatekeeping ordeal, technical system, personal inquiry | study, trials, construction, investigation | academy and tower must share causality |
| *Into the Labyrinth* | misdiagnosed aptitude and ensemble apprenticeship | reclassification and group complementarity | weakness must be specific |
| *Iron Prince* | disadvantaged present and exceptional growth rate | visible competition and adaptation | metrics need social and tactical consequence |
| *The First Step* | class transition into institutional cultivation | labor, access, hierarchy, travel | progression requires an ecology |
| *Street Cultivation* | capacity as economic capital | material trade-offs and structural pressure | modern dress is not enough without economic causation |
| *Beware of Chicken* | rejection of dominant ladder and alternative flourishing | constructive local work and distributed growth | inversion must generate more than parody |
| *Dungeon Crawler Carl* | survival inside an exploitative spectacle | rule changes, audience, politics, resistance | floors alone create repetition |
| *The Rage of Dragons* | revenge-driven optimization inside hierarchy | training, military bonds, moral cost | intensity needs a post-revenge question |
| *Super Powereds: Year 1* | control deficit and ensemble professionalization | differentiated training and relationships | multiple powers need joint structure |
| *Bastion* | rebirth expectation and institutional rejection | alternative legitimacy and recovered identity | mystery cannot postpone every answer |
| *The Wandering Inn* | stable occupational hub and cumulative network | changing visitors and expanding consequence | elasticity increases closure risk |
| *Soulhome* | internal construction with path dependence | design, materials, repair, cross-cultural learning | novel mechanics need character stakes |

The common lesson is not a formula but a test: the most productive seed changes several domains at once.

# 20. An Ordered Protocol for Brainstorming Progression Fantasy

## 20.1 Purpose of the protocol

The protocol converts the preceding theory into an ordered process. It is not the only valid sequence. It is designed for a writer or small creative team developing a novel or series before full outlining. Its central safeguards are:

- separate fixed constraints from inherited assumptions;
- generate components independently before accepting familiar bundles;
- preserve several candidates long enough for fair comparison;
- use literature through structural abstraction;
- evaluate engines through scenes and consequences rather than slogans;
- retain reversibility until simulation produces evidence;
- define closure before escalation becomes the only direction.

The protocol contains seven phases and thirty-six steps. A short project may compress them. A large series-development project may repeat them at series, book, and subplot scales.

## Phase I. Establish the search problem

### Step 1. Define the creative objective

**Question:** What experience, inquiry, or sustained pleasure is the project intended to produce?

Write a paragraph identifying the desired reader experience and the writer's own attraction. Avoid premise language at first. Possible objectives include sustained competence growth, technical problem solving, communal construction, rivalry, wonder, moral pressure, strategic adaptation, or the transformation of a social institution.

**Output:** a creative-intent statement of 100-250 words.

**Check:** The statement should describe effects and interests, not declare that the book must copy a particular title.

### Step 2. Define project realities

Record constraints that are externally real or genuinely chosen:

- standalone, trilogy, fixed series, or open serial;
- approximate length per unit;
- release model;
- viewpoint constraints;
- age category if relevant;
- violence, romance, horror, or content boundaries;
- research capacity;
- co-author or production requirements;
- intellectual-property or franchise constraints;
- completion deadline if one exists.

**Output:** a constraint register marked fixed, preferred, provisional, or unknown.

**Check:** Do not list a trope as fixed merely because it appeared in the first thought.

### Step 3. Build an assumption and uncertainty register

List claims being treated as true. Examples of claim types include genre beliefs, reader expectations, factual assumptions, system rules, and personal predictions about what will be enjoyable to write. For each, record evidence, confidence, and consequence if false.

**Output:** the register in Appendix B.

**Check:** At least five assumptions should be visible before concept selection. A blank register usually means assumptions remain hidden, not absent.

### Step 4. Define the comparison class

Originality and fit are relative to a comparison set. Identify:

- three close progression-fantasy comparators;
- three structurally relevant but less obvious comparators;
- one non-fictional domain;
- one work that embodies what the project should not become.

Use real works and record precise features rather than general admiration. For example, *Mother of Learning* may be relevant for selective retention and repeated comparison, while *The Wandering Inn* may be relevant for a stable social hub [52, 63].

**Output:** a source map with intended analytical function.

**Check:** No single title should supply the protagonist, setting, mechanism, and scene engine together.

## Phase II. Generate independent component banks

### Step 5. Generate progression objects

Produce at least twelve answers to “What changes?” Include combat and noncombat possibilities. Distinguish capability, control, repertoire, knowledge, status, access, resources, coordination, craft, infrastructure, judgment, autonomy, and care.

**Output:** twelve to twenty progression objects.

**Check:** At least half should not be reducible to raw output.

### Step 6. Generate baseline asymmetries

Produce at least twelve specific mismatches between agent and world. Use the pattern:

> The protagonist is low in ___, high in ___, misperceived as ___, and constrained because ___.

The blanks force multidimensional baselines. Lindon's status in *Unsouled*, Hugh's misdiagnosed aptitude in *Into the Labyrinth*, and Reidon's bodily disadvantage paired with growth potential in *Iron Prince* show distinct asymmetry structures [51, 54-55].

**Output:** baseline bank.

**Check:** Delete or rewrite entries that say only “weak,” “ordinary,” “chosen,” or “overpowered.”

### Step 7. Generate desire architectures

For six promising baselines, write:

- immediate goal;
- terminal goal;
- protective goal;
- identity goal;
- assigned goal;
- feared outcome;
- latent need.

**Output:** six desire profiles.

**Check:** At least two goals should be capable of conflict. Perfect alignment produces fewer choices.

### Step 8. Generate progression operations

Generate verbs before nouns. Produce alternatives for acquire, practice, convert, combine, test, retain, counter, and transmit. Seek operations from different domains: apprenticeship, experimentation, cultivation, construction, negotiation, care, performance, logistics, research, ritual, finance, law, ecology, and art.

**Output:** operation bank with at least five alternatives per verb.

**Check:** Each operation should imply an action that can be dramatized.

### Step 9. Generate feedback systems

Produce at least ten ways progress can become legible. Include internal, instrumental, performance, social, economic, institutional, relational, and adversarial feedback.

**Output:** feedback bank.

**Check:** No candidate should depend entirely on an unexplained narrator declaration.

### Step 10. Generate costs and exclusions

Produce costs that alter decisions rather than merely decorate power. Generate at least three in each of four categories:

- resource and time;
- body and cognition;
- social and institutional;
- moral and relational.

Also generate branch exclusions: what cannot be pursued if this path is chosen.

**Output:** cost and exclusion bank.

**Check:** A cost that never changes action, access, or relationship is not yet functional.

### Step 11. Generate institutional forms

List at least twelve institutions or organizational arrangements that could enable, extract from, certify, prohibit, or interpret progression. Include more than academies and sects. Possibilities include guilds, families, militaries, firms, temples, hospitals, licensing boards, cooperatives, courts, entertainment platforms, municipalities, research orders, and informal networks.

**Output:** institutional bank.

**Check:** For each institution, identify who performs the labor that elite participants do not see.

### Step 12. Generate opposition classes

For each of four promising mechanism families, generate:

- direct rival;
- gatekeeper;
- environmental obstacle;
- adaptive antagonist;
- relationship conflict;
- internalized obstacle;
- system-level contradiction.

**Output:** opposition bank.

**Check:** At least one opponent should benefit when the protagonist progresses in the expected way.

### Step 13. Generate relationship dependencies

Create at least twelve dependency relations using verbs: teaches, certifies, funds, stabilizes, conceals, interprets, counters, remembers, owns, needs, mistrusts, or succeeds. Assign two-way effects.

**Output:** relationship bank.

**Check:** Avoid treating every companion as emotional support plus combat ability. Give each a distinct causal function.

### Step 14. Generate thematic tensions

Produce at least ten value pairs, then convert each into a decision-bearing conflict. Examples of abstract pairs include autonomy versus mastery, excellence versus belonging, revenge versus self-authorship, individual ascent versus collective flourishing, and security versus freedom.

**Output:** value-conflict bank.

**Check:** A tension should be expressible through mechanism and consequence, not only dialogue.

## Phase III. Create diverse candidate engines

### Step 15. Build a morphological matrix

Place the banks from Steps 5-14 into columns. Select combinations across columns without privileging adjacent or familiar bundles. Create at least twenty rough combinations.

**Output:** completed matrix and twenty rows.

**Check:** Mark combinations that are impossible, merely cosmetic, or unexpectedly productive. Do not force every cell to fit.

### Step 16. Conduct a bundle-breaking pass

For every recognizable trope bundle, remove or replace two inherited features. If the concept uses an academy, vary age structure, certification, residence, competition, teacher role, funding, or graduation. If it uses cultivation, vary resource ownership, social recognition, bodily model, transmission, and endpoint.

**Output:** revised combinations.

**Check:** State which feature is retained for genre legibility and why.

### Step 17. Conduct a contradiction pass

Add one productive contradiction to each of ten combinations:

- advancement increases dependence;
- success empowers the antagonist;
- measurement lowers accuracy;
- the safest path damages the desired relationship;
- public rank reduces legal freedom;
- mastery requires preserving uncertainty;
- the protagonist is best at a culturally devalued function;
- progress for the individual causes regression for the community.

**Output:** ten contradiction-enhanced combinations.

**Check:** The contradiction must create choices, not only irony.

### Step 18. Conduct a scale-shift pass

Examine five combinations at body, relationship, household or team, institution, and ecology or history scales. Record one consequence at each scale.

**Output:** twenty-five scale consequences.

**Check:** Choose which scales the story will dramatize. The pass expands awareness; it does not require total coverage.

### Step 19. Write independent concept snapshots

Without rereading prior prose, write six one-page snapshots. Each must include all eleven components of the idea vector. Limit each to the same length.

**Output:** six normalized candidate snapshots.

**Check:** No candidate may be named after its mechanism. Temporary neutral labels reduce emotional and branding bias.

## Phase IV. Use literature as structured stimulus

### Step 20. Triangulate each leading candidate

For each of the six snapshots, select three published works that share different functions. A candidate might compare the public metrics of *Iron Prince*, the economic embedding of *Street Cultivation*, and the distributed community growth of *Beware of Chicken* [55, 57-58].

**Output:** an eighteen-source comparison grid, allowing repeated works where their functions differ.

**Check:** The purpose is not to combine three plots. It is to identify functions, risks, and unexplored relations.

### Step 21. Abstract source features

For every source feature recorded, move up the abstraction ladder:

- expression;
- device;
- function;
- relation.

Then write two alternative devices that could instantiate the relation.

**Output:** literature analysis worksheets from Appendix F.

**Check:** If the candidate still requires the source title to be intelligible, abstraction is incomplete.

### Step 22. Run an anti-copy transformation audit

Compare the candidate with its nearest source across protagonist desire, baseline, mechanism, distribution, value conflict, relationships, scene engine, tone, and ending. Require meaningful difference across several dimensions.

**Output:** similarity and transformation report.

**Check:** A change of setting and terminology is insufficient when causal relations remain the same.

### Step 23. Add non-fictional constraint

For each top candidate, research one real domain that can introduce constraints unavailable in trope memory. The domain may be relevant to pedagogy, professional licensing, agriculture, logistics, finance, architecture, medicine, disability, ecology, law, craft, or organizational behavior.

**Output:** a two-page research note per candidate, with sources and decisions changed.

**Check:** Stop when research no longer changes the concept. Unused facts are not evidence of rigor.

## Phase V. Couple and develop the engines

### Step 24. Build the concept graph

Create nodes for agent, baseline, desire, mechanism, feedback, ecology, opposition, relationships, theme, form, and exit. Connect them with verb-labeled edges.

**Output:** one graph per candidate.

**Check:** Identify orphan nodes and overloaded nodes. An orphan may be decorative; an overloaded node may need simplification or stronger support.

### Step 25. Identify high-leverage relations

For each graph, circle the three edges that generate the most consequences. Explain what disappears if each edge is removed.

**Output:** leverage statement.

**Check:** The three edges should not all mean “makes stronger.”

### Step 26. Complete the progression mechanism chain

For the three leading candidates, specify resource, access, attempt, feedback, revision, integration, retention, failure, counter, and consequence.

**Output:** Appendix E for each candidate.

**Check:** Every gain must alter at least one later option and one social, relational, institutional, or resource condition.

### Step 27. Design adaptive opposition

Give each candidate an opposition portfolio and model what major opponents know at three points: beginning, midpoint, and end of Book 1. Design responses based on observed behavior.

**Output:** adaptive-opposition map.

**Check:** Avoid both omniscient counters and opponents who never learn.

### Step 28. Design relationship transformations

Select three core relationships. For each, state initial exchange, hidden asymmetry, progression dependency, midpoint reversal, and possible end-state.

**Output:** relationship transformation table.

**Check:** At least one relationship should become harder because of success rather than failure.

### Step 29. Design repeatable scene families

Generate at least eight scene families for each leading candidate. State what repeats, what varies, and what accumulates.

**Output:** scene-family inventory.

**Check:** Include at least one family centered on failure analysis, one on social consequence, one on resource or institutional negotiation, and one on relationship change.

### Step 30. Define engine mutations

Identify at least three points at which the engine changes mode: competence, public status, institution, scale, value, mechanism, or role.

**Output:** mutation map.

**Check:** A mutation must change problem type, not merely increase magnitude.

## Phase VI. Simulate and stress-test

### Step 31. Run the ten-scene yield test

Write ten scene propositions for each of the top three candidates. Each proposition includes objective, obstacle, progression dimension, relationship, state change, and concept-specific reason.

**Output:** thirty scene propositions.

**Check:** Count families and repeated victory conditions. Record where invention becomes forced.

### Step 32. Run the three-arc simulation

Sketch three short arcs:

1. acquisition or entry;
2. integration under pressure;
3. consequence or counterplay.

Each arc should end in a different kind of state change.

**Output:** one-page three-arc map per candidate.

**Check:** If every arc ends in rank increase, vary the progression object.

### Step 33. Run removal and substitution tests

Remove the candidate's most marketable feature. Ask whether the core relation remains. Then substitute another device that performs the same function.

**Output:** resilience note.

**Check:** A candidate whose identity disappears when terminology is removed may be surface-dependent. That is not automatically fatal, but it should be known.

### Step 34. Run escalation, seriality, and closure tests

For each candidate, answer:

- What is the highest plausible scale?
- Which problem types disappear there?
- What lateral paths remain?
- What closes Book 1?
- What can close the series?
- What can expand without postponing the central goal?
- What is the cost of continuing after the natural endpoint?

**Output:** serial-runway and closure report.

**Check:** “There is always a stronger enemy” is not a closure plan.

### Step 35. Run feasibility and desire tests

Estimate research load, continuity burden, cast size, system complexity, expected length, and personal motivation. Draft three micro-scenes or 1,500-3,000 words from each finalist if practical:

- an early failure;
- a social or relational consequence of progress;
- an advanced scene after the engine has mutated.

**Output:** pilot packet and feasibility table.

**Check:** Distinguish concept failure from prose difficulty. A scene may be hard because the writer is still discovering voice.

## Phase VII. Select, incubate, and hand off

### Step 36. Select through a reversible decision package

Complete the following sequence:

1. score finalists independently on the evaluation vector;
2. record vetoes and disagreements before discussion;
3. select a lead, alternate, and component reserve;
4. incubate the decision without rereading for an appropriate interval;
5. return with a fresh summary written from memory;
6. compare remembered core with the documented package;
7. revise or confirm;
8. freeze a concept charter for outlining;
9. define explicit reopening conditions.

**Output:** selected concept charter, alternate concept card, parked-component bank, and reopening criteria.

**Check:** Selection should identify why the concept is being chosen, which risks are accepted, and which uncertainties must be answered in outlining or drafting.

## 20.2 Compact output package

At completion, the brainstorming phase should produce:

- creative-intent statement;
- constraint register;
- assumption and uncertainty register;
- source and comparison map;
- component banks and morphological matrix;
- at least three normalized concept cards;
- literature abstraction and similarity audits;
- concept graphs;
- progression mechanism sheets;
- opposition and relationship maps;
- scene-family and mutation inventories;
- ten-scene and three-arc simulations;
- serial-runway and closure report;
- feasibility and desire assessment;
- selection ledger;
- lead concept charter and alternate.

This package is sufficient to begin formal outlining without pretending that all discovery is complete.

## 20.3 Reopening conditions

Brainstorming should reopen when development reveals one of the following:

- the engine cannot produce differentiated scenes;
- the central mechanism has no credible limit;
- the protagonist's desire does not require the chosen progression;
- the world cannot sustain the resource or institutional logic;
- the nearest published comparator is substantially closer than initially understood;
- the natural ending occurs far earlier than intended;
- the writer's strongest interest lies in a supposedly secondary relation;
- pilot prose repeatedly comes alive only when the planned engine is absent.

Reopening is not failure. It is the recursive correction expected in creative design [24, 31-33].

# 21. Alternative Brainstorming Modes

## 21.1 Why no single entry point is mandatory

The formal idea vector may suggest that brainstorming should always begin with character and proceed in a fixed order. That is not the claim. Writers differ in cognition, experience, and project attraction. One may discover through character voice, another through systems, images, institutions, themes, or scenes. An adequate method permits multiple entry points while requiring eventual integration.

Each mode below has a characteristic strength, failure risk, and corrective move.

## 21.2 Character-first mode

**Entry point:** motivated asymmetry, voice, relationship, or identity conflict.

**Strength:** emotional leverage and specific choice can guide system design.

**Risk:** the progression mechanism becomes generic because the character could inhabit any story.

**Corrective:** require the character's psychology and situation to change how advancement operates. In *The Rage of Dragons*, Tau's motive makes the intensity and cost of training structurally central [60]. In *Street Cultivation*, Rick's family obligations constrain risk and define what improvement is for [57].

**Best suited to:** projects where moral transformation, voice, or intimate relationships are primary.

## 21.3 System-first mode

**Entry point:** operation, rule, resource, feedback, or interaction.

**Strength:** clear progression process and abundant problem-solving possibilities.

**Risk:** inventory without narrative necessity.

**Corrective:** connect every major operation to desire, institution, relationship, and cost. *Soulhome* demonstrates how a distinctive construction logic can interact with rebuilding, expertise, trust, and mystery [64].

**Best suited to:** technical, craft-centered, tactical, or puzzle-rich projects.

## 21.4 World-first mode

**Entry point:** social order, ecology, institution, history, or cosmology.

**Strength:** progression feels embedded and consequential.

**Risk:** encyclopedic development without a focal engine.

**Corrective:** select one agent whose desired movement reveals the distribution system. *The First Step* uses entry from farm life into sect life to make institutional cultivation experiential [56]. *Street Cultivation* uses material and family pressure to make economic structure immediate [57].

**Best suited to:** projects concerned with class, politics, ecology, empire, culture, or institutional reform.

## 21.5 Conflict-first mode

**Entry point:** impossible task, rivalry, injustice, catastrophe, or incompatible goals.

**Strength:** urgency and causal direction arise quickly.

**Risk:** progression is retrofitted as a convenient solution rather than an object of interest.

**Corrective:** ensure that attempts to progress produce new conflicts and value changes. *Unsouled* links future catastrophe to departure and long-term advancement [51]. *Dungeon Crawler Carl* links survival conflict to participation in an exploitative spectacle [59].

**Best suited to:** high-pressure, action-forward, thriller-like, or revenge-driven narratives.

## 21.6 Relationship-first mode

**Entry point:** mentor and student, rivals, siblings, bonded partners, team, dependent and caregiver, or community.

**Strength:** progression has immediate human consequence and distributed agency.

**Risk:** abilities become symbolic labels without operational depth.

**Corrective:** specify resource flows, complementary capacities, conflict thresholds, and joint skills. *Into the Labyrinth* and *Super Powereds: Year 1* both integrate peer and institutional relationships with distinct progression problems [54, 61].

**Best suited to:** ensemble fiction, found family, mentorship, romance-adjacent progression, and community narratives.

## 21.7 Theme-first mode

**Entry point:** value conflict or philosophical question.

**Strength:** mechanism and outcome can be designed to embody a coherent inquiry.

**Risk:** characters become arguments and the world becomes a demonstration.

**Corrective:** generate multiple plausible positions, distribute them among actors with independent goals, and let consequences complicate the initial framing. *Beware of Chicken* explores alternatives to competitive ascent through lived work and relationship rather than abstract thesis alone [58]. *The Rage of Dragons* makes revenge's power and cost experiential [60].

**Best suited to:** revisionist, tragic, satirical, or morally complex progression.

## 21.8 Image-first mode

**Entry point:** a striking place, object, ritual, body transformation, or scene image.

**Strength:** aesthetic identity and affective commitment.

**Risk:** the image supports a cover or opening but no engine.

**Corrective:** ask what recurrent operation, institution, desire, and cost make the image inevitable. A tower in *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* matters because it grants access, creates danger, structures mystery, and interacts with education and family history [53]. The inn in *The Wandering Inn* matters because it organizes labor, hospitality, classes, guests, and network growth [63].

**Best suited to:** writers with visual or atmospheric discovery processes.

## 21.9 Scene-first mode

**Entry point:** a confrontation, training failure, test, conversation, or revelation the writer wants to draft.

**Strength:** immediate dramatic evidence and voice.

**Risk:** the scene's implied world and system remain contradictory, or no second scene follows.

**Corrective:** reverse-engineer the scene through the idea vector. Identify what must be true before it, what it changes, and what new scene families it enables.

**Best suited to:** discovery writers and projects driven by voice or set pieces.

## 21.10 Market-first mode

**Entry point:** target readership, format, comparable titles, release cadence, or commercial opportunity.

**Strength:** clearer promise and production fit.

**Risk:** overfitting to visible conventions and recent successes.

**Corrective:** conduct a personal-salience and structural-differentiation pass before selection. Use comparators to identify functions and expectations, not to reconstruct a bestseller's package.

**Best suited to:** commissioned work, collaborative production, rapid-release series, or projects with defined publisher constraints.

## 21.11 Discovery-first mode

**Entry point:** exploratory drafting with minimal precommitment.

**Strength:** embodied discovery of voice, character, and unexpected relations.

**Risk:** extensive prose accumulates before the progression engine, resource ecology, or endpoint is viable.

**Corrective:** use short probes rather than a full draft. After 3,000-10,000 words, extract the implied idea vector, scene families, and terminal question. Compare the discovered engine with alternatives.

**Best suited to:** writers whose strongest ideas emerge in prose and who tolerate revision.

## 21.12 Hybrid mode

Most projects benefit from a hybrid. A writer may begin character-first, use system-first analysis, world-first consequence mapping, and scene-first simulation. The key is to name the current mode so that its blind spots can be corrected.

# 22. Individual, Collaborative, and AI-Assisted Ideation

## 22.1 Individual ideation

Individual brainstorming avoids production blocking caused when only one person can speak at a time. It also reduces immediate conformity pressure. Research comparing interactive and nominal groups has repeatedly found that interacting groups can produce fewer ideas than the pooled output of individuals working separately, although interaction may provide cognitive stimulation and social benefits [11-16].

For a solitary writer, the principal risks are not production blocking but path dependence, unchallenged assumptions, and attachment. Useful countermeasures include delayed self-review, multiple prompt frames, externalized candidate equality, and independent passes from memory.

## 22.2 Interactive groups

A live group can stimulate associations, clarify explanations, and expose knowledge gaps. It can also create:

- turn-taking bottlenecks;
- evaluation apprehension;
- social loafing;
- dominance by confident speakers;
- convergence on shared examples;
- collaborative fixation [11-15, 19].

A productive session should not simply put several people in a room and announce “brainstorm.” It should specify roles, phase, recording method, and selection process.

## 22.3 Hybrid individual-group workflow

Evidence supports hybrid structures in which individuals first generate independently and later collaborate [17]. For fiction development, a strong workflow is:

1. distribute a common brief;
2. have each participant generate privately;
3. collect ideas without author names if practical;
4. cluster by function rather than popularity;
5. allow written building and mutation;
6. discuss only after independent evaluation;
7. record minority objections;
8. return to individual synthesis;
9. select through explicit criteria.

This sequence preserves variety while using interaction for combination and criticism.

## 22.4 Brainwriting

Brainwriting replaces or supplements speech with written idea exchange. Participants can produce simultaneously, reducing production blocking. Written records also preserve exact contributions and allow quieter participants greater participation [15].

For a co-authored progression-fantasy project, a brainwriting round can use the idea vector. Each person receives a candidate card, adds one relation, one cost, one counter, and one mutation, then passes it. The rule should forbid mere praise. Every addition must change the engine.

## 22.5 Collaborative role separation

Small teams benefit from temporary roles:

- **generator:** expands possibilities;
- **integrator:** searches for causal coupling;
- **adversary:** develops counters and failure cases;
- **continuity analyst:** tracks contradictions and scale effects;
- **reader advocate:** identifies promise and legibility;
- **producer:** evaluates scope and feasibility;
- **source steward:** tracks influences and research.

Roles rotate. Permanent assignment of “creative” and “critical” identities can distort ownership and authority.

## 22.6 Disagreement as information

Disagreement should be classified before resolution:

- factual disagreement;
- different interpretation of the same candidate;
- different artistic objective;
- different risk tolerance;
- different audience model;
- preference without articulated criterion;
- hidden concern about feasibility or originality.

A vote should not erase the category. The selection ledger records majority choice and unresolved dissent.

## 22.7 Generative AI as search instrument

Large language models can generate options, recombine language, ask questions, critique gaps, and transform representations quickly. They also tend to produce fluent answers that can appear more complete than they are. Evidence from recent studies suggests a mixed picture: AI assistance can improve judged individual output in some creative tasks, particularly for lower baseline performers, while increasing similarity across outputs; other work reports risks of design fixation and reduced divergent breadth under particular conditions [48-49]. Qualitative research on AI-assisted prewriting reports both stimulation and concerns about agency, ownership, and dependence [50].

These findings support conditional use, not a universal verdict.

## 22.8 Appropriate AI functions

AI is relatively well suited to:

- expanding a user-created component bank;
- generating counterarguments and missing questions;
- translating a concept into the formal idea vector;
- varying one dimension while holding others stable;
- identifying repeated structures across candidate summaries;
- simulating institutional reactions;
- proposing counterplay;
- checking whether scene propositions are structurally distinct;
- formatting a portfolio or ledger;
- suggesting research queries, followed by independent verification.

AI should not be treated as authoritative evidence that a premise is original, culturally responsible, factually correct, or commercially viable.

## 22.9 A human-first AI protocol

A defensible sequence is:

1. the writer creates an unassisted seed bank;
2. the writer marks personal salience and non-negotiable values;
3. the model receives only selected components, not a request for “the best idea”;
4. the model generates alternatives across specified distant categories;
5. outputs are decomposed into reusable relations;
6. the writer performs a fresh unassisted pass before rereading;
7. duplicates and generic clusters are identified;
8. the writer recombines and rewrites in their own conceptual language;
9. final selection is justified without reference to model endorsement;
10. factual and source claims are verified.

This protocol uses AI to widen or interrogate search while preserving human objective setting and evaluation.

## 22.10 Prompt design for diversity

Prompts should request variation dimensions, not ten polished pitches from one default frame. Better prompt structures include:

- “Generate mechanisms from ten unrelated domains and state the operation borrowed from each.”
- “For this candidate, produce counters that attack information, legitimacy, coordination, time, and values rather than output.”
- “List assumptions shared by these five concepts, then reverse each assumption.”
- “Create three alternatives that preserve the emotional problem but replace the academy, ranking, and combat structure.”
- “Compare these concepts for causal density and explain what each component changes.”

These are methodological examples, not fictional ones.

## 22.11 Provenance and versioning

AI-assisted projects should retain:

- human seed before model use;
- prompt and model output;
- accepted, rejected, and transformed components;
- source verification notes;
- similarity concerns;
- final human rationale.

This record supports originality audits and prevents the model's polished wording from becoming invisible scaffolding.

## 22.12 When not to use AI

AI may be counterproductive when:

- the writer has not yet formed any personal relation to the material;
- the main problem is emotional honesty rather than option scarcity;
- exposure to fluent alternatives is likely to overwrite a fragile voice;
- cultural or factual sensitivity requires primary research;
- the project depends on a private experiential insight the model cannot supply;
- the writer repeatedly accepts the first output without transformation.

Tool refusal is not purity. It is phase-appropriate method selection.

# 23. Evaluation, Selection, and Concept Development

## 23.1 Evaluation is goal-relative

No concept is best independently of artistic purpose, project scale, audience, resources, and writer motivation. Evaluation should therefore begin with the creative-intent statement and fixed constraints. A concept designed for an open serial should not be penalized merely because it lacks the compression of a standalone. A quiet profession-centered progression story should not be scored by duel frequency.

## 23.2 The evaluation dimensions

Score each finalist from 0 to 4, with written evidence, on the following dimensions:

1. **progression centrality:** advancement is a principal source of anticipation and causation;
2. **mechanism clarity:** inputs, operations, feedback, retention, failure, and counters are intelligible;
3. **causal density:** major elements change several domains;
4. **emotional leverage:** the engine matters to desire, identity, or relationship;
5. **scene yield:** the concept produces differentiated dramatic situations;
6. **counterplay:** opposition can adapt specifically;
7. **world integration:** resources and institutions explain access and consequence;
8. **relationship consequence:** progress changes interdependence, status, or trust;
9. **thematic pressure:** the mechanism embodies a value conflict;
10. **differentiation:** distinction survives removal of terminology;
11. **serial elasticity:** the engine can vary across intended length;
12. **closure capacity:** local and terminal completion are credible;
13. **writability:** research, complexity, and production demands fit resources;
14. **writer commitment:** curiosity persists after analytical challenge.

The score is not a measurement of literary worth. It forces explicit comparison.

## 23.3 Veto conditions

Some weaknesses should not be averaged away. Candidate vetoes may include:

- violation of fixed ethical or production constraints;
- untransformably close dependence on another work's protected expression;
- no credible protagonist agency;
- no mechanism by which progression affects later action;
- no plausible ending under intended length;
- research burden beyond available resources;
- central premise dependent on a factual claim known to be false, unless openly counterfactual;
- authorial aversion that persists after charitable development.

Vetoes should be declared before scoring.

## 23.4 Evidence notes

Every score should include a brief evidence note. “Scene yield: 4” is weak. “Produces acquisition, diagnostic, repair, negotiation, public test, teaching, resource, and countermeasure scenes, demonstrated by ten-scene test” is inspectable.

Evidence may be:

- concept-graph relation;
- scene proposition;
- mini-arc;
- research source;
- comparator analysis;
- pilot prose;
- collaborator judgment;
- known uncertainty.

## 23.5 Independent rating before discussion

Teams should rate independently before discussing. A solitary writer can approximate independence by rating at different times, changing candidate order, and writing a fresh summary from memory. The purpose is to reduce anchoring and rhetorical dominance.

## 23.6 Selection under uncertainty

A candidate can be selected with unresolved uncertainties if they are named and bounded. The decision package should state:

- reasons for selection;
- accepted risks;
- decisive advantages;
- closest alternative;
- information that could reverse the choice;
- first low-cost test;
- date or milestone for review.

This changes commitment from faith to managed uncertainty.

## 23.7 Development after selection

Selection does not end ideation. The selected engine receives focused development:

- resolve high-impact uncertainties;
- increase coupling among weak components;
- simplify decorative complexity;
- develop opposition models;
- define Book 1 transformation;
- define information release boundaries;
- generate additional scene families;
- test voice and point of view;
- hand off to outlining with known alternatives preserved.

## 23.8 The alternate concept

The strongest alternate should be retained in a one-page form. It serves three functions:

1. prevents sunk-cost pressure from making the lead irreversible;
2. preserves a solution if pilot drafting exposes a structural defect;
3. provides components that may solve later subproblems without restarting from zero.

The alternate is not a threat to commitment. It is an option with a defined expiration or review point.

# 24. Common Failure Modes and Their Repairs

## 24.1 Failure Mode 1: The vivid seed is treated as a complete concept

**Symptom:** The project can be summarized through one striking image, power, setting, or twist, but questions about recurring action produce vague answers.

**Cause:** Vividness creates a false sense of completeness.

**Diagnostic:** Remove the seed's name and ask for five scene families, three forms of opposition, and a terminal condition.

**Repair:** Build the full idea vector and identify at least three high-leverage relations. The tower in *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* functions because it is connected to attunement, danger, personal inquiry, and institutions [53].

## 24.2 Failure Mode 2: Premise novelty substitutes for engine novelty

**Symptom:** The opening situation is unusual, but subsequent events become conventional training, ranking, and combat.

**Cause:** Originality was assessed at the inciting condition only.

**Diagnostic:** Compare the middle-book scene families with close comparators after removing terminology.

**Repair:** Design a continuing variation rule. *Mother of Learning* makes selective retention reshape training, investigation, and relationships throughout the engine [52].

## 24.3 Failure Mode 3: New terminology masks familiar causality

**Symptom:** Ranks, energies, and abilities have original names, but each functions like a linear quantity ladder.

**Cause:** Surface originality is counted as mechanism and relational originality.

**Diagnostic:** Rewrite the system in plain language without proper nouns.

**Repair:** Change operations, distribution, costs, feedback, or value. *Soulhome* changes the design logic of progression through construction and path dependence [64].

## 24.4 Failure Mode 4: The protagonist is only “weak”

**Symptom:** The baseline offers no competence, misclassification, social relation, or strategic asymmetry.

**Cause:** Underdog status is used as a complete character design.

**Diagnostic:** Complete: low in ___, high in ___, misperceived as ___, constrained because ___.

**Repair:** Specify dimensions. Lindon, Hugh, and Reidon begin from importantly different baseline relations [51, 54-55].

## 24.5 Failure Mode 5: The protagonist's desire does not require progression

**Symptom:** The stated goal could be achieved through a simpler non-progression plot, while advancement continues because the genre expects it.

**Cause:** Character and system were generated separately.

**Diagnostic:** Remove the progression mechanism and list plausible routes to the goal.

**Repair:** Revise goal, mechanism, or obstacle until advancement produces necessary options and consequential costs. Corin's advancement and investigation are linked through tower access, magical study, and the search concerning his brother [53].

## 24.6 Failure Mode 6: Progression has no operation

**Symptom:** Characters “train,” “cultivate,” or “level” without a dramatizable process.

**Cause:** Labels replaced actions.

**Diagnostic:** State input, operation, feedback, revision, and integration.

**Repair:** Design verbs and failure information. *Mother of Learning* turns repeated attempts into comparative learning and investigation [52].

## 24.7 Failure Mode 7: Costs do not alter decisions

**Symptom:** Advancement causes pain, fatigue, or danger, but the protagonist always chooses it and no other obligation changes.

**Cause:** Cost was added for tone rather than opportunity structure.

**Diagnostic:** Identify a scene in which the cost makes a rational character choose differently.

**Repair:** Link cost to time, debt, relationship, law, or future options. *Street Cultivation* embeds advancement in livelihood and family responsibility [57].

## 24.8 Failure Mode 8: Scarcity appears only when delay is needed

**Symptom:** Resources are abundant in setup, vanish before a breakthrough, and reappear after the plot obstacle.

**Cause:** Mechanism was built without ecology.

**Diagnostic:** Map origin, ownership, renewal, transport, storage, and competing uses.

**Repair:** Make scarcity institutional or ecological and allow actors to adapt. *The First Step* situates resources and instruction within sect structure [56].

## 24.9 Failure Mode 9: Measurement does all the narrative work

**Symptom:** The only evidence of progress is a higher number or rank.

**Cause:** Legibility was confused with quantification.

**Diagnostic:** Remove metrics and ask how behavior, access, social response, and options differ.

**Repair:** Add performance, relational, institutional, and adversarial feedback. *Iron Prince* connects metrics to competition, visibility, relationships, and tactical development [55].

## 24.10 Failure Mode 10: Every challenge tests output

**Symptom:** Problems differ only in how much force is required.

**Cause:** Progression was equated with escalation.

**Diagnostic:** Classify challenges by precision, information, coordination, time, law, collateral risk, and moral cost.

**Repair:** Vary challenge dimensions. *Dungeon Crawler Carl* adds spectacle, rules, sponsorship, politics, and manipulation [59].

## 24.11 Failure Mode 11: Opponents never learn

**Symptom:** The protagonist repeats a successful technique while informed adversaries respond identically.

**Cause:** Antagonists were designed as tests rather than agents.

**Diagnostic:** State what each opponent observed and what update follows.

**Repair:** Build the observation-update-constraint loop. Publicly visible growth in *Iron Prince* naturally invites social and tactical response [55].

## 24.12 Failure Mode 12: Counters are arbitrary

**Symptom:** A new enemy negates the protagonist through a previously unknown immunity introduced at confrontation.

**Cause:** Counterplay was improvised after the ability became too strong.

**Diagnostic:** Ask whether the counter follows from established world rules and opponent knowledge.

**Repair:** Brainstorm direct, environmental, incentive, and institutional counters during concept development.

## 24.13 Failure Mode 13: The world does not react

**Symptom:** The protagonist acquires rare or disruptive capacities without attention from employers, states, criminals, teachers, markets, or communities.

**Cause:** Worldbuilding is static scenery.

**Diagnostic:** Identify winners, losers, gatekeepers, and observers for each major gain.

**Repair:** Attach social and institutional consequences. *Street Cultivation* makes capacity economically and institutionally legible [57].

## 24.14 Failure Mode 14: Institutions exist only to deliver training

**Symptom:** The academy, sect, or guild has no finances, labor, politics, jurisdiction, or reason to select students.

**Cause:** The institution is a plot service.

**Diagnostic:** Ask what the institution gains, fears, produces, and externalizes.

**Repair:** Assign enabling, extractive, and legitimating functions. *The First Step* and *Sufficiently Advanced Magic* place training within larger institutional relations [53, 56].

## 24.15 Failure Mode 15: Relationships are detachable

**Symptom:** Allies provide banter or combat roles, but replacing them with tools would not change progression.

**Cause:** Relationship design occurred after system design.

**Diagnostic:** Remove one relationship and list which access, interpretation, control, or identity changes disappear.

**Repair:** Give each relationship a progression dependency and reversal. *Into the Labyrinth* and *Super Powereds: Year 1* integrate peer development with capability [54, 61].

## 24.16 Failure Mode 16: The mentor has no limit

**Symptom:** The mentor can answer every question but withholds information whenever suspense is needed.

**Cause:** Mentorship lacks objective constraints.

**Diagnostic:** Specify ignorance, incentive, jurisdiction, value conflict, and cost of intervention.

**Repair:** Design a changing usefulness curve. *Soulhome* shows how prior mentorship and betrayal can complicate trust in expertise [64].

## 24.17 Failure Mode 17: Ensemble members occupy one ladder

**Symptom:** Every character can be totally ordered by strength, and team scenes reduce to adding outputs.

**Cause:** Distinct abilities have no distinct problem domains.

**Diagnostic:** Identify a situation in which each member is indispensable for a different reason.

**Repair:** Design complementary constraints and joint capacities. *Into the Labyrinth* and *Super Powereds: Year 1* provide ensemble-centered contrasts [54, 61].

## 24.18 Failure Mode 18: Theme is attached as commentary

**Symptom:** Characters discuss ambition, inequality, or power, but the mechanism rewards and resolves choices independently of those themes.

**Cause:** Theme was treated as message rather than relation.

**Diagnostic:** Remove thematic dialogue and see whether costs and outcomes still embody the tension.

**Repair:** Put the value conflict into access, cost, and ending. *Beware of Chicken*, *Street Cultivation*, and *The Rage of Dragons* each make values structural in different ways [57-58, 60].

## 24.19 Failure Mode 19: Inversion produces only parody

**Symptom:** The concept rejects a familiar trope but has no independent source of progress, conflict, or care.

**Cause:** Negation was mistaken for an engine.

**Diagnostic:** Ask what the protagonist repeatedly builds, risks, chooses, and transforms after refusal.

**Repair:** Construct an alternative value and scene system. *Beware of Chicken* sustains farming, community, relationships, and distributed growth beyond its initial rejection [58].

## 24.20 Failure Mode 20: Repetition lacks a variation rule

**Symptom:** Terms, floors, trials, or loops repeat with larger obstacles but no new information or relationship structure.

**Cause:** A serial container was mistaken for serial generation.

**Diagnostic:** Complete: stable identity + changed state + new constraint for three consecutive units.

**Repair:** Define what accumulates and what changes. *Mother of Learning* varies route and knowledge; *Dungeon Crawler Carl* varies rules, environments, and spectacle [52, 59].

## 24.21 Failure Mode 21: Infinite ladder, absent ending

**Symptom:** Every endpoint is another rank, realm, continent, world, or cosmic layer.

**Cause:** Extensibility was optimized without closure.

**Diagnostic:** State what would complete the protagonist's central transformation if higher power remained possible.

**Repair:** Add goal, identity, institutional, relational, succession, or renunciation closure. *Unsouled* begins with a future home-related horizon, while *Beware of Chicken* foregrounds alternative measures of flourishing [51, 58].

## 24.22 Failure Mode 22: The first book is only setup

**Symptom:** It establishes system and threat but completes no meaningful transformation.

**Cause:** Series potential displaced local closure.

**Diagnostic:** Identify one capability, relationship, institution, and question that changes irreversibly by the end of Book 1.

**Repair:** Design nested closure. *Unsouled* completes a decisive departure and path change while preserving its larger horizon [51].

## 24.23 Failure Mode 23: The concept is selected by average score alone

**Symptom:** Severe closure or agency defects are obscured by high novelty and worldbuilding scores.

**Cause:** Multi-objective evaluation was collapsed prematurely.

**Diagnostic:** Identify non-compensable vetoes and inspect the full score profile.

**Repair:** Use vectors, evidence notes, and veto conditions rather than one total.

## 24.24 Failure Mode 24: One candidate received all the development

**Symptom:** The favorite appears superior because it has characters, scenes, and research while alternatives remain one sentence.

**Cause:** First-idea attachment and sunk cost.

**Diagnostic:** Normalize finalist packages to the same length and questions.

**Repair:** Develop at least three candidates charitably before comparative selection.

## 24.25 Failure Mode 25: Literature comparison becomes surface imitation

**Symptom:** The candidate can be described mainly as one title with a setting change.

**Cause:** The writer transferred device and expression rather than function and relation.

**Diagnostic:** Explain the concept without naming the comparator, then compare nine transformation dimensions.

**Repair:** Use multi-source triangulation and an abstraction ladder. Compare, for example, the different academy relations in *Sufficiently Advanced Magic*, *Into the Labyrinth*, *Iron Prince*, and *Super Powereds: Year 1* [53-55, 61].

## 24.26 Failure Mode 26: Research becomes indefinite preparation

**Symptom:** Notes expand while no candidate decision changes.

**Cause:** Research provides certainty and postpones selection.

**Diagnostic:** For each source, state which design decision it changed.

**Repair:** Use question-specific research with stopping conditions.

## 24.27 Failure Mode 27: AI fluency is mistaken for originality

**Symptom:** A polished generated pitch is accepted before alternatives are independently produced or similarities examined.

**Cause:** Linguistic completeness produces authority and anchoring.

**Diagnostic:** Strip rhetorical language and compare structural features across outputs and published comparators.

**Repair:** Use human-first generation, dimension-controlled prompts, fresh unassisted passes, and provenance records [48-50].

## 24.28 Failure Mode 28: The writer optimizes a concept they do not want to write

**Symptom:** The package scores well, but pilot scenes feel dutiful and attention repeatedly moves to a secondary idea.

**Cause:** Evaluation excluded affective commitment or over-weighted market and prestige.

**Diagnostic:** Identify the scene the writer would draft without external reward and compare it with the chosen engine.

**Repair:** Revisit creative intent, allow recombination, or select the alternate. Writability includes sustained curiosity, not only technical feasibility.

# 25. Diagnostic and Validation Suite

## 25.1 Purpose

No diagnostic can prove that a concept will become a successful novel. These tests expose specific failure risks before full outlining. A candidate may fail one test for a deliberate artistic reason. The writer should record the reason rather than mechanically repair every deviation.

## 25.2 Test 1: Plain-language reconstruction

Explain the candidate without proper nouns, rank names, invented substances, or comparison titles.

**Pass condition:** a distinctive relation remains.

**Risk revealed:** surface originality without structural difference.

## 25.3 Test 2: Ten-scene yield

Generate ten scene propositions with varied objectives, obstacles, relationships, and state changes.

**Pass condition:** at least five scene families and several victory conditions.

**Risk revealed:** opening premise without a middle engine.

## 25.4 Test 3: No-combat reconstruction

Remove direct combat from five proposed scenes.

**Pass condition:** progression still affects inquiry, negotiation, craft, care, status, institution, or relationship.

**Risk revealed:** output escalation as the only form of consequence.

This is a diagnostic, not a rule against combat. *The Rage of Dragons* and *Iron Prince* legitimately center martial development, but their engines also involve hierarchy, identity, institutions, and relationships [55, 60].

## 25.5 Test 4: No-number reconstruction

Remove explicit metrics and ranks.

**Pass condition:** earlier and later states remain behaviorally and socially distinguishable.

**Risk revealed:** measurement substituting for change.

## 25.6 Test 5: Power-removal test

Remove the protagonist's signature ability while preserving desire and world.

**Pass condition:** the writer can state exactly which causal relations collapse and which remain.

**Risk revealed:** either a detachable gimmick or a character with no existence beyond the gimmick.

## 25.7 Test 6: Opponent adaptation

Give the main opponent accurate observations of three protagonist victories.

**Pass condition:** the opponent can formulate a response consistent with resources and values.

**Risk revealed:** static antagonism.

## 25.8 Test 7: Institutional consequence

Make a major gain public.

**Pass condition:** at least three institutions or organized interests react differently.

**Risk revealed:** inert world.

## 25.9 Test 8: Relationship displacement

Advance the protagonist far enough that the original mentor, rival, friend, or dependent relation cannot remain unchanged.

**Pass condition:** a new exchange or conflict emerges.

**Risk revealed:** detachable relationships and socially consequence-free growth.

## 25.10 Test 9: Midpoint mutation

Assume the protagonist becomes competent at the initial loop halfway through Book 1.

**Pass condition:** the engine can change role, scale, value, or institution without abandoning its identity.

**Risk revealed:** premise exhaustion.

## 25.11 Test 10: Endpoint and after-end test

State the natural series ending, then imagine one additional book.

**Pass condition:** the writer can distinguish legitimate continuation from post-closure inflation.

**Risk revealed:** inability to recognize completion.

## 25.12 Test 11: Similarity audit

Compare the candidate with its closest published work across nine dimensions.

**Pass condition:** differences are causal and value-bearing, not only cosmetic.

**Risk revealed:** unacknowledged imitation.

## 25.13 Test 12: Resource ecology

Trace one unit of the progression resource from origin to elite use.

**Pass condition:** source, labor, ownership, transport, risk, and waste are at least provisionally known.

**Risk revealed:** arbitrary scarcity.

## 25.14 Test 13: Failure yield

Design three failures that do not simply postpone success.

**Pass condition:** each changes information, relationship, resource, status, or future options.

**Risk revealed:** repetitive training.

## 25.15 Test 14: Value conflict

Give the protagonist a choice between faster progression and another sincerely held value.

**Pass condition:** both options carry real loss and follow from the engine.

**Risk revealed:** thematic commentary without structural pressure.

## 25.16 Test 15: Writability

Draft three micro-scenes at early, middle, and advanced states.

**Pass condition:** the writer can find distinct dramatic energy and a sustainable point of view.

**Risk revealed:** analytically elegant concept with low experiential appeal.

## 25.17 Test 16: Memory compression

After incubation, summarize the candidate from memory in 100 words.

**Pass condition:** the remembered core matches the intended high-leverage relations.

**Risk revealed:** concept identity dispersed across too many components.

# 26. Propositions for Future Research

The framework yields empirical propositions. They are hypotheses, not established findings.

**P1.** Progression-fantasy concepts developed through normalized multi-candidate portfolios will show greater structural diversity than concepts developed by immediate elaboration of the first premise.

**P2.** Brainstorming prompts framed around relations and operations will produce concepts with higher causal density than prompts framed around names, powers, and settings.

**P3.** Hybrid individual-then-collaborative ideation will produce a more diverse and higher-quality finalist portfolio than unstructured interactive brainstorming, consistent with broader group-ideation findings [11-18].

**P4.** Literature analysis conducted at function and relation levels will reduce surface overlap with exemplars compared with direct “inspired by” pitching, consistent with fixation and analogy research [20-21, 25-26].

**P5.** Concepts that pass a scene-yield test before outlining will require fewer premise-level revisions during drafting than concepts selected only by logline preference.

**P6.** Concepts that include an explicit terminal condition will show lower escalation inflation and stronger perceived series coherence than otherwise similar concepts with open-ended power goals.

**P7.** Multi-channel feedback systems will produce higher reader comprehension of progress than systems relying exclusively on numerical rank, even when both communicate equivalent underlying change.

**P8.** Adaptive-opposition design will predict perceived earnedness of later victories more strongly than the absolute quantity of training scenes.

**P9.** AI assistance introduced after independent human ideation will preserve more between-concept diversity than AI assistance introduced before any human candidate generation [48-50].

**P10.** The relationship between constraint and concept quality will be curvilinear and moderated by timing: a small number of generative constraints will outperform both diffuse freedom and dense early specification [27-28].

**P11.** Personal-salience ratings will predict project continuation after controlling for concept-rubric scores.

**P12.** Concepts with high causal density will support greater serial variation at lower continuity cost than concepts with many weakly connected subsystems.

A useful research program would combine controlled ideation experiments, coding of concept packages, longitudinal observation of drafting, and reader studies. It should distinguish novice from expert writers, solo from collaborative projects, fixed-length from open serials, and human-only from AI-assisted processes.

# 27. Discussion

## 27.1 The central result

The principal result of this synthesis is a change in the unit of analysis. Progression-fantasy brainstorming should not be centered on the isolated “cool idea.” The relevant unit is a coupled engine whose components alter one another. A time loop matters because of what persists and resets. An academy matters because of what it certifies, extracts, and compares. A unique power matters because of how it changes choice, attention, cost, and counterplay. A progression ladder matters because people and institutions attach value to it.

This shift explains why premise comparison is often misleading. *Unsouled* and *Iron Prince* can both be described at high abstraction as stories about severely disadvantaged protagonists with unusual growth prospects [51, 55]. Yet their mechanisms, worlds, institutions, aesthetics, technologies, and social feedback differ. *Beware of Chicken* and *The Wandering Inn* both support community-centered and nontraditional forms of progress, but one works through withdrawal into agrarian construction and the other through a profession-centered social hub [58, 63]. Structural analysis must preserve both common function and specific relation.

## 27.2 Brainstorming as search under uncertainty

The framework also reframes brainstorming from inspiration to search. Search requires a space, a method of movement, a representation of candidates, and a stopping rule. The writer cannot enumerate all possible novels. The practical aim is to alter the probability of finding high-value configurations.

Divergence increases sampled variation. Constraints make consequences inspectable. Externalization allows comparison. Literature expands available structures but risks fixation. Incubation can loosen dominant paths. Evaluation directs resources. Simulation supplies evidence unavailable to slogans. Selection remains provisional because drafting can reveal properties not visible in abstraction.

## 27.3 Why pluralism is necessary

The article deliberately avoids one mandatory creative sequence. Creativity research does not support a universal recipe applicable without regard to person, task, and domain [2, 7-9]. Genre theory also cautions against treating conventions as timeless essences [34-36]. Progression fantasy accommodates quantified and unquantified systems, combat and noncombat advancement, individual and collective agents, fixed trilogies and enormous serials.

Pluralism does not mean that every method is equally adequate. A process that never generates alternatives is more vulnerable to first-idea attachment. A process that never evaluates produces archives rather than decisions. A concept that cannot generate scenes has not yet demonstrated a narrative engine. Functional requirements can be defended while entry points remain diverse.

## 27.4 Originality as transformation

The four-level originality model helps separate meaningful difference from decorative renaming. Surface originality may attract attention and contribute aesthetic value. Mechanism originality changes how progression operates. Relational originality changes how the system interacts with society, character, and conflict. Value-level originality changes what counts as improvement.

The comparative works often derive their strongest identity from the latter levels. *Street Cultivation* transforms the relation between power and economy [57]. *Beware of Chicken* transforms the value hierarchy of cultivation [58]. *Mother of Learning* transforms failure and repetition through selective retention and mystery [52]. *Soulhome* transforms advancement into design and construction [64].

## 27.5 The importance of closure during brainstorming

Closure is often postponed until outlining or drafting. In progression fantasy, that delay can be costly because the central engine naturally invites “more.” More rank, technique, territory, knowledge, and scale can always be imagined. Without a terminal condition, every solution becomes an invitation to inflate.

Brainstorming closure does not require knowing the final scene. It requires knowing what kind of transformation would make further ascent optional rather than narratively compulsory. A rescue, return, reform, solved mystery, succession, chosen community, relinquished revenge, or rejected metric can complete a story even in a world with greater powers.

## 27.6 Selection is a creative act

Idea generation receives more romantic attention than selection, but selecting and developing a candidate shapes the final work profoundly. Research suggests people may privilege feasibility or familiarity over creativity and may not reliably identify their strongest ideas [16-18]. A rubric cannot remove judgment. It can expose criteria and make trade-offs discussable.

The portfolio model is therefore central. It protects the unusual candidate long enough to be understood, protects the practical candidate from being dismissed as insufficiently flashy, and preserves an alternative when pilot work changes the evidence.

## 27.7 AI changes scale, not the basic problem

Generative AI increases the speed and polish of option production. It does not remove the need to define goals, identify assumptions, distinguish structural from surface novelty, verify facts, evaluate cultural implications, simulate consequences, or choose what matters. In fact, increased fluency can make these functions more important. The danger is not only poor output. It is rapid convergence on plausible, familiar combinations that feel finished.

The recommended human-first protocol is therefore less about defending a metaphysical boundary between human and machine creativity than about preserving source diversity, personal salience, and accountable selection.

# 28. Limitations

First, the framework is conceptual. Its original models, thresholds, and protocol have not been validated as predictors of manuscript quality, reader satisfaction, publication success, or completion.

Second, the literary set is purposive and English-language-centered. It includes works influenced by Chinese cultivation and web-fiction traditions but does not substitute for broad comparative study of Chinese-language xianxia, wuxia, Korean progression and game fiction, Japanese light novels, or other traditions [38-39].

Third, analysis emphasizes structural description. It does not fully address prose style, sentence-level voice, humor, characterization technique, cultural representation, or the aesthetic value of ambiguity.

Fourth, a formal vector can encourage false completeness. A deliberately minimal, lyrical, comic, or experimental work may omit components or keep them indeterminate. The framework should diagnose consequences, not enforce bureaucratic uniformity.

Fifth, judgments of originality depend on the corpus known to the evaluator. No private brainstorming process can establish historical uniqueness with certainty.

Sixth, the AI evidence is recent and task-specific. Findings from short stories, design tasks, and prewriting interviews should not be generalized without qualification to long-series authorship [48-50].

Seventh, published texts do not reveal their actual brainstorming histories. The comparative chapter analyzes available structures, not authorial process unless explicitly documented elsewhere.

# 29. Conclusion

Proper brainstorming for progression fantasy is neither unrestricted idea production nor early optimization of a favored pitch. It is a disciplined form of creative inquiry. The writer constructs and searches a possibility space, develops several candidates, examines assumptions, uses constraints strategically, abstracts relations from literature, simulates consequences, and selects under acknowledged uncertainty.

The central object is the coupled story engine. It joins agent, baseline, desire, mechanism, feedback, ecology, opposition, relationships, values, narrative form, and closure. A concept becomes stronger when these components do more than coexist. They alter one another. Progress changes the protagonist's feasible actions, but it also changes what institutions notice, what opponents counter, what relationships require, what costs become rational, and what kind of person the protagonist can remain.

The framework's most important practical conclusions are:

1. brainstorm relations and operations, not only nouns and powers;
2. separate surface, mechanism, relational, and value-level originality;
3. preserve several equally developed candidates before selection;
4. use published literature through functional abstraction and multi-source triangulation;
5. connect progression to resource distribution, institutions, and daily life;
6. design adaptive opposition and relationship consequences before outlining;
7. test concepts through scene yield, engine mutation, and closure;
8. combine individual generation with structured collaboration rather than relying on unmoderated group talk;
9. use AI as a controlled search and critique instrument, not as authority over novelty or purpose;
10. define what would make further progression unnecessary to the story.

The protocol does not guarantee a great novel. It makes the quality of the starting decision more inspectable, alternatives more available, and major risks less hidden. Its proper standard is not whether it eliminates uncertainty. It is whether uncertainty becomes productive rather than accidental.

# Evidence Status

## Directly supported claims, Status A

The following are supported by the cited research within the limits of the original tasks:

- creative products are commonly evaluated through originality and effectiveness [1];
- creative work involves interacting generative and evaluative processes [2, 23];
- examples can constrain later generation and contribute to design fixation [19-21];
- unstructured interactive groups can suffer productivity losses relative to pooled individual generation [11-16];
- hybrid individual-group structures can improve the quality of identified ideas under some conditions [17];
- idea selection is distinct from idea generation and can privilege impact or feasibility over creativity [16-18];
- incubation has a positive average effect under some conditions, with important moderators [22];
- analogy depends substantially on relational mapping, not only surface similarity [25-26];
- constraints can help or hinder creativity depending on their properties and context [27-28];
- writing and design processes are recursive, with problem representation and solution development capable of coevolution [24, 31-33];
- recent AI studies report both creative assistance and risks of homogenization or fixation in particular tasks [48-50].

## Cross-domain applications, Status B

The following apply broader findings to progression-fantasy ideation and require direct testing:

- independent seed generation before collaborative discussion should preserve concept diversity;
- structural abstraction from published novels should reduce surface imitation;
- incubation after explicit problem representation should aid progression-system ideation;
- morphological variation should expose inherited trope bundles;
- multi-channel feedback should improve brainstorming of reader-legible progression;
- explicit closure ideation should reduce later escalation inflation.

## Original syntheses, Status C

The following are proposed in this article:

- the eleven-component idea vector;
- the concept graph and causal-density criterion;
- four levels of originality;
- generative yield, engine mutation, and closure capacity as separate concept qualities;
- the normalized candidate portfolio;
- the thirty-six-step protocol;
- the evaluation vector and veto model;
- the sixteen-part diagnostic suite;
- the propositions in Chapter 26.

## Practice-contingent guidance, Status D

The following depend heavily on project and author:

- exact numbers of seeds, candidates, scene families, and pilot words;
- duration of incubation;
- relative weights assigned to rubric dimensions;
- use of collaborative roles;
- use or non-use of AI;
- degree of advance closure planning;
- how much exploratory prose precedes selection;
- whether a fixed series or open serial requires greater elasticity.

## C.2 Bundle-breaking questions

For each candidate, identify every component that arrived automatically with another component. Then ask:

1. Does the causal relation require this pairing?
2. Is the pairing a fixed genre promise, a useful convention, or an unexamined default?
3. What function does the second component perform?
4. What structurally different component could perform the same function?
5. What new consequences follow if the conventional function is split among several institutions?
6. What happens if the conventional pairing is reversed?
7. What happens if one component is public and the other hidden?
8. What happens if the protagonist gains the benefit but not the recognized status?
9. What happens if the institution can certify status but cannot measure capability?
10. What happens if capability is common but the opportunity to use it is scarce?

## C.3 Real-literature contrast

The following comparison illustrates abstraction rather than combination.

| Work | Surface package | Structural relation worth abstracting | What should not be copied as a package |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Unsouled* [51] | cultivation clans, sacred arts, ranked advancement | social classification creates exclusion; a wider horizon makes present judgment revisable | names, rank scheme, clan structure, particular revelation sequence |
| *Iron Prince* [55] | military academy, combat device, visible attributes | low present capacity and exceptional rate of change create different social predictions | device terminology, school events, stat package, character roles |
| *Street Cultivation* [57] | contemporary city plus cultivation | capacity is embedded in ownership, debt, work, and unequal institutions | specific economic terminology, plot sequence, family details |
| *The Wandering Inn* [63] | portal fantasy and class levels centered on an inn | a stable occupation and place accumulate a widening social network | specific classes, species, political events, guest arcs |

A candidate that merely mixes the first column has changed surfaces. A candidate that recombines relations from the second column and derives new consequences has begun structural transformation.

## E.2 Abstraction ladder

Move upward until the source's proper nouns and distinctive expression disappear, but the causal relation remains informative.

| Level | Question | Entry |
|---|---|---|

| 6. Transfer | In which structurally different domain could the relation operate? |  |

## E.3 Worked example: *Mother of Learning*

This example abstracts one relation rather than reproducing the novel's expression.

| Level | Analysis |
|---|---|
| Surface | A protagonist repeatedly experiences a bounded period while retaining selected knowledge [52]. |
| Function | Failure can produce usable information without ending the entire narrative. |
| Relation | Asymmetric memory connects repetition to investigation, training, social experimentation, and moral choice. |
| Constraint | Time, access, danger, imperfect knowledge, and other agents' interests prevent unlimited effortless optimization. |
| Dynamic | The value of another repetition changes as mysteries are solved, relationships acquire meaning, and exit becomes possible. |
| Transfer | The same abstract relation could operate through seasonal reconstruction, inherited institutional memory, reversible simulations, ritual reenactment, or a community whose records survive cyclical destruction. |
| Consequence | Each transfer changes who retains knowledge, who bears cost, what counts as failure, and whether repetition is private, public, voluntary, or coerced. |

The transferable structure is not “use a month-long loop.” It is **persistent asymmetry inside reset conditions, combined with finite uncertainty and changing moral stakes**.

## E.4 Worked example: *Beware of Chicken*

| Level | Analysis |
|---|---|
| Surface | A protagonist leaves the expected cultivation pursuit and builds a farm and community [58]. |
| Function | The story tests whether flourishing can arise through a different orientation toward power, labor, and relationship. |
| Relation | Refusal of a dominant ladder redirects effort into place-making, care, production, and distributed development. |
| Constraint | The wider world still contains power, threat, convention, and misunderstanding, so refusal does not remove consequence. |
| Dynamic | Local constructive work creates new responsibilities and new forms of capability rather than static retirement. |
| Transfer | A concept could reject professional ranking, hereditary office, divine selection, fame metrics, or extraction-based advancement while preserving pressure from the rejected order. |
| Consequence | The alternative must produce difficult choices, resource needs, relationships, and exposure to external institutions. |

The transferable structure is not “make cultivation cozy.” It is **reject one definition of advancement while constructing another that still changes the feasible action space**.

## F.2 Red flags

Pause development if several of the following are true:

- the candidate can be pitched as “the source, but with” one changed noun;
- the same components appear in the same causal order;
- the protagonist begins with the same deficit, receives the same exception, and enters the same institution;
- the leading relationships occupy equivalent functions and emotional trajectories;
- the signature challenges arrive in a recognizably parallel sequence;
- changed terminology hides unchanged operations;
- the endpoint answers the same question by the same kind of act;
- removing one distinctive imported feature collapses the entire candidate.

## F.3 Transformation operations

Apply at least three operations at different levels:

1. change the progress object;
2. change who owns or recognizes progress;
3. change the protagonist's baseline competence;
4. change the cost from bodily to relational, institutional, temporal, epistemic, or ecological;
5. change feedback from private certainty to public contest, or the reverse;
6. split one institution's functions among several competing institutions;
7. replace individual progress with collective, distributed, inherited, or infrastructural progress;
8. reverse the value attached to advancement;
9. change the renewal source of scenes;
10. change the terminal condition;
11. introduce a nonfictional system with independent constraints;
12. require consequences that the source's structure does not produce.

## F.4 Provenance note

For private development records, keep a short statement such as:

> Candidate C-04 was stimulated by the memory-retention relation in *Mother of Learning* [52], the capacity-economy relation in *Street Cultivation* [57], and research on professional licensing. The candidate excludes a repeating calendar, cultivation currency, corresponding character roles, source terminology, and parallel plot sequences. Its central relation concerns public institutional memory, contested certification, and collective advancement.

The note is not a legal conclusion. It is a record of conscious transformation.

Published works demonstrate different renewal sources. *Super Powereds: Year 1* can renew through differentiated ensemble training, professional selection, secrecy, and changing relationships [61]. *The Wandering Inn* renews through the cumulative network around a stable occupational hub [63]. *Dungeon Crawler Carl* renews through changing rule environments, spectacle, alliances, and resistance to the system producing the contests [59]. ## G.4 Closure test

Answer each question in plain language.

1. What can the protagonist eventually do that matters?
2. What desire is satisfied, refused, replaced, or revealed as mistaken?
3. Which dependency or institution changes irreversibly?
4. What mystery or uncertainty does not need complete cosmological explanation to close?
5. What would a final advancement add after the central conflict is resolved?
6. Why would that addition no longer require narration?
7. What stable but changed life becomes imaginable after progression ends?
8. For an open serial, which local closure can occur while the wider engine remains active?

# Appendix H. Candidate Evaluation Rubric

## H.1 Rating discipline

Rate independently before discussion. Use evidence notes rather than intuition alone. Do not aggregate until veto conditions have been checked. A “5” means unusually strong evidence at the present development stage, not guaranteed success.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 | Evidence note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Desire to write | little sustained interest | interest with reservations | persistent curiosity across scenes and consequences |  |
| Progression centrality | advancement is replaceable | advancement affects major events | advancement organizes choices, relationships, and resolution |  |
| Causal density | components coexist | several useful connections | most major components alter one another |  |
| Operational clarity | progress is named but not enacted | some repeatable actions exist | operations, feedback, failure, and adaptation are clear |  |
| Generative yield | fewer than five distinct scenes | one installment seems viable | several scene families and arcs emerge from the engine |  |
| Transformative capacity | mainly quantitative increase | some qualitative change | advancement repeatedly changes the kind of problem |  |
| Character pressure | power and identity are weakly linked | growth creates some dilemmas | growth transforms values, self-conception, and agency |  |
| Relationship consequence | relationships are detachable | some bonds change | dependencies and obligations coevolve with progress |  |
| Institutional/world response | setting is static | limited reaction | ecology and institutions adapt plausibly |  |
| Opposition quality | stronger enemies only | some counterplay | informed, constrained, multi-dimensional adaptation |  |
| Distinctiveness | mainly changed labels | one unusual mechanism or relation | several transformed relations with clear distance from sources |  |
| Legibility | progress is obscure or arbitrary | generally inferable | feedback supports anticipation without eliminating uncertainty |  |
| Serial elasticity | engine exhausts quickly | several arcs possible | renewal and mutation coexist with cumulative consequence |  |
| Closure capacity | no endpoint beyond maximum power | provisional resolution exists | terminal condition follows from desire, values, and institutions |  |
| Feasibility | requires unavailable resources or scale | manageable with compromise | matched to form, research, skill, and production model |  |

## H.2 Veto conditions

A candidate should not be selected solely because its total is high when any material veto remains unresolved:

- no reason advancement is necessary to the protagonist's aim;
- no operation by which progress occurs;
- near-identity with a salient source across several structural dimensions;
- central mechanism contradicts a fixed project requirement;
- engine produces no plausible installment-level closure;
- required research is inaccessible or ethically inappropriate;
- the writer consistently avoids imagining scenes in the concept;
- the candidate depends on a reveal that cannot bear examination;
- progression erases agency rather than creating choices, unless that loss is the intentional subject;
- later arcs require forgetting or nullifying established consequences.

## H.3 Pairwise comparison

For finalists A and B, answer:

1. Which produces the stronger best scene?
2. Which produces the stronger tenth distinct scene?
3. Which produces a more meaningful first irreversible choice?
4. Which creates more varied noncombat problems?
5. Which has the clearer reason to become a series rather than one novel?
6. Which has the clearer reason eventually to end?
7. Which uncertainty is most expensive to resolve?
8. Which weakness is transformable, and which is constitutive?
9. Which would the writer choose without market information?
10. Which would the writer choose if only one installment could ever be completed?

# Appendix I. Individual, Collaborative, and AI-Assisted Session Designs

## I.1 Ninety-minute individual session

| Minutes | Activity | Output |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-10 | Restate the search question and separate constraints from assumptions | one-page brief |
| 10-25 | Generate one component bank without consulting examples | 20-30 fragments |
| 25-35 | Shift category and generate remote-domain analogues | 10-15 fragments |
| 35-50 | Build six cross-bank combinations | six rough engines |
| 50-60 | Take a genuine break without reviewing notes | incubation interval |
| 60-70 | Generate again from memory before rereading | fresh alternatives |
| 70-82 | Elaborate the three least familiar viable options | three snapshots |
| 82-90 | Record uncertainties and next tests without selecting | uncertainty ledger |

## I.2 Small-team session

Use individual generation before discussion.

1. Each participant receives the same brief and independently generates seeds.
2. Contributions are pooled anonymously when status or persuasive fluency may distort evaluation.
3. The group clusters by underlying relation rather than wording.
4. One member acts as constraint steward, one as possibility advocate, one as causal auditor, and one as recorder. Roles rotate.
5. Participants independently nominate options for elaboration.
6. Mixed pairs develop different candidates rather than everyone optimizing one.
7. The group compares equally developed cards using evidence notes.
8. Dissenting evaluations are recorded before consensus is sought.

This structure addresses production blocking and evaluation apprehension identified in group-brainstorming research while retaining cognitive stimulation and combination [11-17].

## I.3 AI use principles

Recent studies do not support either “AI makes users creative” or “AI necessarily destroys creativity” as a universal conclusion. In a short-story experiment, access to generative AI improved some individual evaluations but reduced collective diversity because outputs became more similar [48]. In a visual ideation experiment, AI exposure increased fixation on examples and reduced the number, variety, and originality of ideas [49]. A qualitative prewriting study found useful human-AI collaboration patterns as well as breakdowns and changing initiative [50]. The practical response is controlled sequencing.

## I.4 Human-first AI protocol

1. Write the brief and initial component banks without AI.
2. Record three provisional candidates and the conventions already noticed.
3. Assign the model a bounded function: category expansion, contradiction generation, consequence tracing, adversarial critique, or comparison normalization.
4. Ask for dimensions or questions before asking for complete pitches.
5. Request several structurally separated batches, each under different constraints.
6. Hide or delay model-generated prose when the goal is independent human generation.
7. Mark every imported suggestion and its prompt provenance.
8. Reconstruct selected suggestions in plain language without reading the original output.
9. Combine no more than a small number of AI contributions before deriving consequences independently.
10. Use a fresh pass to seek disconfirming cases and overlooked assumptions.
11. Conduct similarity and desire-to-write audits.
12. Make selection and value judgments explicitly human decisions.

## I.5 Prompt patterns

These prompts are instruments, not complete creative briefs.

**Category expansion**

> Identify twelve domains structurally distant from combat, games, and formal schooling in which people develop durable capability under scarcity. For each domain, name the operations, feedback, gatekeepers, and irreversible trade-offs. Do not pitch stories.

**Assumption exposure**

> Given the following concept statement, list every proposition that must be true for its causal engine to function. Separate logical requirements, genre conventions, empirical assumptions, and aesthetic preferences. Do not repair the concept yet.

**Bundle breaking**

> Identify which elements in this concept commonly travel together in progression fantasy. For each bundle, state the function of every element and propose three structurally different replacements for that function. Preserve the core emotional promise.

**Consequence tracing**

> For the stated progression mechanism, trace first-, second-, and third-order consequences for labor, family, law, education, status, warfare, disability, religion, and ecological resource use. Mark uncertainty rather than inventing certainty.

**Opponent adaptation**

> Assume informed opponents observe the protagonist's last three advances. Generate counters constrained by their existing knowledge, resources, values, and institutions. Do not solve the counters for the protagonist.

**Anti-homogeneity request**

> Produce four batches under mutually exclusive search rules: one character-first, one institution-first, one relationship-first, and one value-first. Do not reuse a progression object, setting type, feedback form, or antagonist class across batches.

**Selection challenge**

> Compare the candidates using the supplied rubric, but do not choose a winner. Identify where each score depends on missing evidence, where criteria conflict, and which cheap test would most reduce decision uncertainty.

## J.2 Selection rationale

Write a rationale that answers all of the following:

1. Which candidate best satisfies the actual project objective?
2. Which candidate has the strongest causal engine, not merely the strongest pitch?
3. Which candidate's weaknesses are most tractable?
4. What information is still missing?
5. Why is the alternate structurally different rather than a minor variation?
6. What evidence would reverse the decision?
7. Which features are provisional and must not be treated as canon?
8. Which source influences require continued distance monitoring?

## J.3 Reopening conditions

Do not reopen selection because drafting becomes difficult. Difficulty is expected. Reopen only when a specified condition occurs, such as:

- the scene-yield test fails after genuine development;
- the progression operation cannot be dramatized without repeated exposition;
- research falsifies a load-bearing assumption;
- the first pilot scenes reveal that the writer is interested only in a detachable subplot;
- the core mechanism requires arbitrary exceptions to produce conflict;
- the planned endpoint contradicts the central desire or value arc;
- source similarity remains high after transformation attempts;
- production constraints materially change;
- the alternate performs better on a previously unresolved high-weight criterion.

## J.4 Minimum handoff package

Brainstorming is sufficiently mature for outlining when the package contains:

- a one-page creative brief;
- a live assumption and uncertainty register;
- one selected engine card and one retained alternate;
- a provenance and literature-abstraction record;
- a plain-language progression mechanism chain;
- the resource and institutional ecology;
- protagonist baseline, desire, and value conflict;
- opposition portfolio and adaptation logic;
- relationship dependencies;
- at least four scene families and ten distinct scene premises;
- three provisional engine mutations;
- installment-level promise and closure condition;
- series-level terminal or convergence condition;
- veto review and decision rationale;
- explicit reopening conditions.

The handoff does not freeze invention. It changes the unit of work. Brainstorming asks which engine is worth developing. Outlining asks how the selected engine unfolds through causally ordered events. Discoveries during outlining may revise the engine, but revision should update the decision record rather than silently replacing the project's foundations.

## K.4 Validation invariants

- a protagonist action that can produce advancement;
- feedback sufficient for characters and readers to infer consequential change;
- a reason the desired transformation requires progression;
- at least one resource or opportunity constraint;
- at least one adaptive source of opposition;
- at least one relationship altered by progression;
- a repeatable scene family;
- a mutation or variation rule;
- an installment closure condition;
- a terminal, convergence, or intentionally provisional exit model;
- a provenance record for salient literary or AI-derived inputs;
- an unresolved-risk record.

## K.5 Process safeguards

- Do not overwrite rejected candidates; preserve status and rationale.
- Do not calculate a winner before veto review.
- Do not expose collaborators to examples before independent generation when diversity is a priority.
- Do not classify an AI-generated completion as independent human output.
- Do not treat names, ranks, or numerical scales as evidence of mechanism distinctiveness.
- Do not promote a candidate solely because it is easy to summarize.
- Do not demand equal polish from unlike entry modes; demand comparable causal information.
- Do not close the possibility space permanently. Record the evidence required to reopen it.
